# EGA I 7.3.5–7.3.7: constant rational modules and the reduced structure map

This is an independent comparison and proof dossier, not a new root theorem,
a source-edition revision, or a claim of complete EGA coverage. “Scheme”
includes the nonseparated schemes called preschemes in the source.
Throughout, \(\mathcal R_X\) is EGA's sheafification of regular functions on
topologically dense open subsets. It is not silently replaced by the sheaf
\(\mathcal K_X\) formed using regular denominators.

## 7.3.5: every quasi-coherent rational module is constant

Let \(X\) be a nonempty irreducible scheme, \(\eta\) its generic point,
and \(B=\mathcal O_{X,\eta}\). Neither reducedness nor Noetherianity is
assumed. The generic-point calculation in 7.3.3, proved in the preceding
dossier and compared with 01RV, gives
\[
 \Gamma(U,\mathcal R_X)=B
 \quad\text{for every nonempty open }U\subseteq X,
\]
with identity restrictions. Thus \(\mathcal R_X=\underline B_X\)
canonically as a sheaf of rings and as an \(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra.
In particular, \(B\) is a generic **local ring**, not necessarily a field.

Let \(\mathcal F\) be quasi-coherent on the ringed space
\((X,\mathcal R_X)\). By the local-presentation definition, each point
has a nonempty open neighborhood \(U\) with an exact sequence
\[
 \mathcal R_U^{(I)}\longrightarrow \mathcal R_U^{(J)}
       \longrightarrow\mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(I,J\) are arbitrary sets. Such a neighborhood may be made affine,
as in the printed proof; the constant-sheaf argument itself needs only
irreducibility.

For any \(B\)-module \(M\), write \(\underline M_U\) for its constant
sheaf. A locally constant map on a nonempty irreducible open set is
constant: two distinct nonempty fibers would be disjoint nonempty opens.
The natural map
\(\bigoplus_I\underline B_U\to\underline{B^{(I)}}_U\) is an isomorphism,
since on every stalk it is the identity on the module direct sum.
This uses sheafification and the stalk description of coproducts, not a
false arbitrary global-sections interchange.

The presentation map therefore is induced by one \(B\)-linear map
\(u:B^{(I)}\to B^{(J)}\). Indeed its value on each standard generator is
a section of the target constant sheaf, hence one element of \(B^{(J)}\);
those values determine a unique map on the module direct sum. Every
column has finite support, but there need not be a finite number of
nonzero columns or a finite matrix. The sheaf cokernel is
\(\underline{\operatorname{coker}u}_U\): the natural comparison is the
module cokernel map on every stalk, so is exact and an isomorphism.
This does not assume that global sections preserve cokernels.

It remains to pass from local constancy to global constancy; merely
choosing incompatible local trivializations would not suffice.
Every nonempty neighborhood \(U\) contains \(\eta\), and the restriction
to \(\eta\) identifies its constant fiber canonically with
\(\mathcal F_\eta\). Two such identifications agree on every nonempty
overlap, because the overlap contains \(\eta\) and a map of constant
sheaves there is determined by its map at \(\eta\).
Equivalently, for every nonempty open \(V\), the germ map
\[
 \Gamma(V,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow\mathcal F_\eta
\]
is injective by local constancy, and is surjective by taking the constant
local section with a prescribed generic germ on each member of a cover
and gluing their agreeing restrictions. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal F\simeq\underline{\mathcal F_\eta}_X
\]
canonically and \(\mathcal R_X\)-linearly. This expands all steps compressed
in the printed reference to 0.3.6.2 and the reduction to a constant
presentation.

### Why the scalar ring cannot be left implicit

The preceding definition names \(\mathcal R_X\), not merely an action of
that ring on an arbitrary sheaf. In this particular irreducible-scheme
setting, an \(\mathcal R_X\)-module is quasi-coherent over
\(\mathcal R_X\) if and only if its underlying \(\mathcal O_X\)-module
is quasi-coherent. Here is the scheme-specific justification.

On an affine \(V=\operatorname{Spec}A\), the unique minimal prime is
\(\mathfrak p\), the generic ring is \(B=A_{\mathfrak p}\), and
\(\mathcal R_V=\widetilde B\). Any \(B\)-module \(M\), viewed as an
\(A\)-module, satisfies \(M_f=M\) when \(f\notin\mathfrak p\), since
\(f\) is a unit in \(B\). If \(f\in\mathfrak p\), then \(f\) is nilpotent
in \(A\) (the unique minimal prime is its nilradical),
\(D(f)=\varnothing\), and \(M_f=0\). The principal-open values and
restrictions of \(\widetilde M\) are consequently exactly those of
\(\underline M_V\). Tags 01HV and 01I7 identify this associated sheaf,
its exactness, and its mapping property. Thus the constant sheaves just
obtained from 7.3.5 are underlying \(\mathcal O_X\)-quasi-coherent.

Conversely suppose an \(\mathcal R_X\)-module \(\mathcal F\) is underlying
\(\mathcal O_X\)-quasi-coherent. Locally it is an affine associated
module \(\widetilde M\): 01BK applies since schemes have a basis of
quasi-compact affine neighborhoods, and 01I7 compares its construction
with the affine one. We may restrict its associated-module presentation
to a smaller affine neighborhood if needed. On that affine neighborhood,
\(\Gamma(V,\mathcal R_X)=B\) acts on
\(M=\Gamma(V,\mathcal F)\). A free \(B\)-module presentation with arbitrary
indices sheafifies exactly, by 01HV, to an \(\mathcal R_V\)-presentation
of \(\widetilde M=\mathcal F|_V\). The isomorphism is compatible with the
existing \(\mathcal R_V\)-action: for each \(b\in B\), both actions give
the same endomorphism on global sections and hence the same sheaf
endomorphism by 01I7; every local rational section is locally such a
\(b\). This proves the converse without an invalid general
ringed-space direct-sum assertion.

## 7.3.6: tensorization and the integral specialization

Now let \(\mathcal F\) be \(\mathcal O_X\)-quasi-coherent on the same
nonempty irreducible \(X\), and put
\(\mathcal G=\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal R_X\).
Tensor an arbitrary local \(\mathcal O_X\)-free presentation with
\(\mathcal R_X\). Right exactness (01CC), commutation with arbitrary
direct sums (05NB), and
\(\mathcal O_X\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal R_X=\mathcal R_X\)
give an exact local \(\mathcal R_X\)-presentation
\[
 \mathcal R_X^{(I)}\longrightarrow\mathcal R_X^{(J)}
     \longrightarrow\mathcal G\longrightarrow0.
\]
No flatness or finite-presentation assumption is needed. This is also
the identity-topological-space pullback of 01BG for the morphism of
ringed spaces \((X,\mathcal R_X)\to(X,\mathcal O_X)\).
It is not asserted to be a morphism of locally ringed spaces.

Applying 7.3.5, \(\mathcal G\) is constant. The tensor-stalk formula 01CB
further identifies
\[
 \mathcal G_\eta
  =\mathcal F_\eta\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,\eta}}\mathcal R_{X,\eta}
  =\mathcal F_\eta\otimes_B B
  \simeq\mathcal F_\eta.
\]
Consequently \(\mathcal G\simeq\underline{\mathcal F_\eta}_X\)
canonically. The printed proof gives only the sentence justifying the
second assertion by the field property; the presentation and generic
fiber arguments here supply the first assertion explicitly rather than
pretending that these lines were printed.

If \(X\) is also reduced, it is integral and \(B\) is a field (00EU and
01RV). Choose a basis of the \(B\)-vector space \(\mathcal F_\eta\),
possibly infinite, with index set \(I\). Its module isomorphism
\(B^{(I)}\simeq\mathcal F_\eta\) gives
\(\mathcal R_X^{(I)}\simeq\mathcal G\).
The isomorphism after choosing a basis is not canonical; no finite rank
is claimed. The zero vector space corresponds to the empty basis.

## 7.3.7: local hypotheses and injection on reduced schemes

Suppose \(X\) is locally integral or locally Noetherian.
Quasi-coherence is local. In the first case each point has an integral
open neighborhood, with one irreducible component; in the second, it
has a Noetherian affine neighborhood, with finitely many irreducible
components by 01OZ and 0052. Apply the local form of 7.3.3 on these
neighborhoods, and use \(\mathcal R_X|_U=\mathcal R_U\), to conclude that
\(\mathcal R_X\) is a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra.

This is a local argument, not an assertion of a globally finite number
of components. It imports neither global Noetherianity nor reducedness.
The more general hypothesis of 7.3.3, a locally finite family of irreducible
components, also suffices; a locally Noetherian underlying topological
space suffices for that premise even when the coordinate rings are not
Noetherian. We do not equate these two uses of “Noetherian.”

If in addition \(X\) is reduced, the canonical
\(\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal R_X\) is injective. In fact this second assertion
holds on **every reduced scheme**, without any component finiteness
assumption. To check it, take a section whose image vanishes as a
rational-sheaf germ. A presheaf and its sheafification have the same
stalks, so after shrinking the neighborhood its rational class is zero.
By the definition of dense-open equivalence, the original regular
section then vanishes on a dense open subset \(D\) of that neighborhood.
On an affine open \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) within it, let \(a\in A\) be
the section. The open \(D(a)\) is disjoint from the dense open
\(D\cap\operatorname{Spec}A\): at a point of \(D(a)\), \(a\) is a unit
in the local ring, whereas on \(D\) its germ is zero. Thus
\(D(a)=\varnothing\), so \(a\) is nilpotent; as \(A\) is reduced,
\(a=0\). Tags 01J1 and 01J2 justify passing between reduced schemes
and their affine rings. This proves vanishing locally and therefore
injectivity on stalks and as sheaves.

The printed proof cites 7.3.3 for the first assertion and 7.2.3 for
the second. The latter is consistent with applying uniqueness of
dense-open extension to functions valued in the separated affine line.
The elementary affine proof above supplies that step without requiring
\(X\) itself to be separated. Locally integral schemes are reduced,
since reducedness is local, so the injection applies automatically in
the first case.

## Adverse examples and exact limits

1. On \(\operatorname{Spec}k\), the constant sheaf \(k^2\) satisfies
   7.3.5 but has a proper nonzero \(k\)-submodule \(k\oplus0\).
   It is not simple as a module. This tests the historical terminology.

2. Let \(X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\), with function field \(K\), and
   let \(x\) be a closed point. Define the skyscraper sheaf by
   \(S_x(U)=K\) when \(x\in U\), and \(S_x(U)=0\) otherwise;
   restrictions between opens containing \(x\) are identities, and
   restrictions to opens not containing \(x\) are zero. The constant
   sheaf \(\mathcal R_X=\underline K_X\) acts by multiplication.
   Its underlying \(\mathcal O_X\)-action at \(x\) is through
   \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\to K\), not through the residue field
   \(\kappa(x)\). Thus this is a skyscraper on the topological point,
   not pushforward of a module from the closed subscheme
   \(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x)\). Its stalk at \(x\) is \(K\)
   and its generic stalk is zero; hence it is not constant and, by
   7.3.5, not quasi-coherent over \(\mathcal R_X\).
   A rational-ring action alone is insufficient.

3. On the disjoint union of two copies of \(\operatorname{Spec}k\),
   the quasi-coherent module with fibers \(k\) and \(0\) is not globally
   constant and cannot be \(\mathcal R_X^{(I)}\) for one fixed index set:
   any nonempty \(I\) gives a nonzero fiber at both points, and empty
   \(I\) gives zero at both. Irreducibility cannot be dropped from
   either global conclusion.

4. On \(\operatorname{Spec}B\) for \(B=k[e]/(e^2)\), the constant
   \(B\)-module \(k=B/(e)\) is quasi-coherent and constant but not free.
   Indeed \(e\) annihilates it, whereas \(e\neq0\) does not annihilate
   any nonzero free \(B\)-module. Nonreduced irreducibility does not
   imply the field or free-module conclusion. This module also occurs
   in 7.3.6 since the one-point scheme has \(\mathcal O_X=\mathcal R_X\).

5. For \(A=k[t,e]/(e^2,te)\), \(e\neq0\), as seen by quotienting by
   \(t\). The underlying space is the irreducible affine line, and
   the dense open \(D(t)\) kills \(e\). The generic local ring and
   dense-open rational ring are \(k(t)\); hence the structure map
   to \(\mathcal R_X\) is not injective. By contrast localization at
   non-zero-divisors is injective and preserves nonzero \(e\):
   \(t\) is a zero divisor and cannot be used as a regular denominator.
   This explicitly distinguishes \(\mathcal R_X\) from \(\mathcal K_X\)
   on a nonreduced scheme.

6. Reducedness is sufficient, not necessary, for injection: the
   nonreduced one-point scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}k[e]/(e^2)\)
   has only itself as a nonempty dense open, so
   \(\mathcal O_X=\mathcal R_X\) and the structure map is the identity.

7. The empty scheme has zero sheaves and the empty direct sum;
   it has no generic point. The generic-point proofs above explicitly
   assume nonempty irreducibility and do not invent a generic point
   for the empty case.

These examples qualify the derived claims; none changes a source
edition or declares a printed erratum. All twelve earlier open gaps
remain open and byte-preserved. The next semantic cursor is 7.3.8.
