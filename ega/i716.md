## 7.1.15: the fibre and the embedding are both part of the answer

Let X be integral with generic point eta. The introduction specializes
01RV to give K=R(X)=O_(X,eta)=kappa(eta); 01RW calls this the function
field. Let s be eta's image in S. The structure map fixes an embedding
kappa(s) -> K. The correspondence, with all its data retained, is

`Rat_S(X,Y) = Hom_S(Spec K,Y)`

`             = Hom_(kappa(s))(Spec K,Y_s)`

`             = {(y,iota): y lies over s, iota:kappa(y) -> K is a kappa(s)-embedding}.`

The source inherits the locally Noetherian S and finite-type Y/S
hypotheses of 7.1.12 and adds integrality of X. Existing 0BX8(3) proves
more: an integral source is reduced with one irreducible component, so
locally finite-type Y/S already suffices. The base need not be locally
Noetherian in this stronger result. The source's original hypotheses
remain recorded rather than being silently replaced by these weaker ones.

For the first bijection, the one-component target of 0BX8 consists of
y over s and a local O_(S,s)-algebra map O_(Y,y) -> O_(X,eta).
The local-source classification 01J6 identifies this datum with a map
Spec K -> Y over S. Its construction agrees with generic restriction.

For the second, 01K0 defines Y_s=Y times_S Spec(kappa(s)). The canonical
map Spec K -> S factors through Spec(kappa(s)). The universal property
01JP identifies S-maps to Y with maps over kappa(s) to Y_s. The base-field
map is fixed; it is not a freely varying choice.

Here is the whole two-part argument of the source's proof. By 01K1,
points y over s are precisely the points of the fibre. By 0HA1 the local
ring in the fibre is O_(Y,y)/m_s O_(Y,y), whose maximal-ideal quotient
is still kappa(y). Thus the residue fields agree. The same conclusion
comes from 01JT: kappa(y) tensor_(kappa(s)) kappa(s)=kappa(y), with its
unique prime and the same residue field.

Next, a local homomorphism O_(Y,y) -> K to a field has kernel exactly
m_y: locality says the inverse image of the field's zero maximal ideal
is m_y. It therefore factors uniquely through kappa(y), and a unital
homomorphism of fields is injective. Conversely a field embedding composed
with the residue quotient is local. Compatibility with O_(S,s) is
exactly compatibility with its quotient kappa(s). This proves both
directions of the pair correspondence without suppressing the embedding.
It is also the field-valued specialization explained immediately after
01J6; that prose is supporting unlabelled discussion, not a new theorem.

01J9 alone is not the complete replacement: it quotients field-valued
maps to describe underlying points. Here the chosen embedding into the
fixed K is essential. The proof's dependency on EGA I3.6.3 is reused,
not counted as new root mathematics.

## 7.1.16: algebraic preschemes over a field

Set S=Spec k. It has one point s with kappa(s)=k, and the fibre Y_s is
canonically Y by the fibre-product universal property. The preceding
bijection becomes

`Rat_k(X,Y) = Hom_k(Spec R(X),Y)`.

The source assumes both X and Y are algebraic k-preschemes and X is
integral. In modern Stacks terminology these are finite-type k-schemes,
as in 06LG. No separatedness is added, and Y need not be reduced.
Again 0BX8(3) is stronger: X need not itself be of finite type, and
local finite type of Y suffices. These dispensable source hypotheses
remain visible in the comparison.

The source's ring-valued-point convention at I3.4.4 suppresses Spec in
the notation for points with values in a k-algebra; its existing mapping
is reused. The introductory specialization and the corollary account
for all remaining source material: there is no extra proof after the
closing environment. This correspondence concerns all rational maps,
not only dominant ones.

## Distinctions checked against adverse examples

- Integrality justifies replacing the generic local ring by a field;
  irreducibility alone does not. On Spec(k[e]/(e^2)), maps to A^1_k with coordinate image zero
  or e are distinct rational maps, but become identical over the residue
  field. The one-point source has no smaller nonempty open.
- K need not be algebraically closed: X=Spec Q gives K=Q. No algebraic
  closure or separability condition is part of either corollary.
- The fibre condition matters. Let S be two disjoint copies of Spec k,
  with X over the first and Y over the second. A field embedding k -> k
  exists, but there is no S-map; y must lie above the specified s.
- The embedding matters. X=Y=Spec(Q(sqrt(2))) has one underlying point
  but two Q-morphisms, given by the two signs of sqrt(2). Over
  S=Spec(Q(sqrt(2))) with the identity structures only the identity is
  an S-map. Thus base-field compatibility also matters.
- A constant map A^1_Q -> A^1_Q has image y=0 and gives an embedding
  kappa(y)=Q -> Q(t). It is not a map from the target's function field
  Q(u) sending u to zero. Replacing the pair by a function-field map
  incorrectly discards nondominant rational maps.
- Some target finiteness remains necessary. For X=S=A^1_Q and
  Y=Spec(Q(t)) with its canonical map to S, the generic S-map to Y
  exists. No nonempty open U of X can map to Y over S: its image in S
  contains closed points, whereas Y's image is the generic point alone.
  Y/S is not locally of finite type, so this does not contradict 0BX8(3).
