## 7.1.10: constructing the generic-local restriction

Let X be a nonempty irreducible scheme with generic point eta, and put
G = Spec(O_(X,eta)). The canonical morphism j:G -> X is 02NA. Its image
consists of generalizations of eta by 01J7, hence lies in every open
neighbourhood of eta. Every nonempty open of X contains eta and is dense.
Consequently j factors uniquely through any domain U of a rational map,
and a representative f:U -> Y determines f composed with j:G -> Y.

If two representatives agree on a dense open W, the two composites factor
through W and agree. This proves independence under precisely the relation
in 01RS. The factorization is canonical, so it also preserves a given
S-structure. Thus there is a map

`rho: Rat_S(X,Y) -> Mor_S(G,Y)`.

With no named base the same construction uses ordinary morphisms. There
is no target finiteness, Noetherianity, reducedness, or separatedness
hypothesis for this construction. By 01J6 a map from G is described by a
point y of Y and a local homomorphism O_(Y,y) -> O_(X,eta). This is the
one-component datum appearing in 0BX8, not merely a residue-field map.

## 7.1.11: when restriction is injective or bijective

Specialize 0BX8 to the single irreducible component of X. A pair in its
target describes an S-map from G by 01J6: y lies over the image s of eta,
and the stalk homomorphism is a local O_(S,s)-algebra map. Thus its map is
exactly rho, not a different comparison with the same-looking codomain.

Part (1) of 0BX8 makes rho injective when Y -> S is locally of finite type.
EGA assumes finite type, which implies that hypothesis. No Noetherian base
or source is needed for this injectivity assertion.

For EGA's second assertion, S is locally Noetherian and Y -> S has finite
type. By 01TX it is locally of finite presentation. Part (2) of 0BX8 then
makes rho bijective. The stronger Stacks result needs only local finite
presentation, without requiring a locally Noetherian base.

The complete source proof invokes I6.5.1. Its mechanism is present in
0BX6 and the proof of 0BX8. For uniqueness, finitely many algebra
generators give finitely many equality neighbourhoods; intersect them to
get one neighbourhood of eta. For existence, images of finitely many
generators have representatives on one neighbourhood, and finite
presentation allows the finitely many relations to hold after another
shrinking. Every such neighbourhood is dense in irreducible X. This also
explains why finite type alone does not give the second assertion over an
arbitrary base. These dependencies were already mapped for I6.5.1 and are
reused, not counted as new root mathematics.

There are useful alternatives in the existing theorem: 0BX8(3) gives
bijection for locally finite-type target when X is reduced, and 0BX6
also treats a locally Noetherian source. These are stronger alternatives,
not assumptions silently inserted into EGA's statement.

## 7.1.12: points valued in a local scheme

Apply the representable functor 001O in the category of S-schemes. A point
of Y valued in G means precisely an element of Mor_S(G,Y). The bijection
above therefore gives the stated interpretation of rational S-maps.
This proves the whole terminology-only corollary and its reference to
I3.4.1. All bijectivity hypotheses from 7.1.11 are retained. The valued
scheme G can be nonreduced; these are not necessarily field-valued or
geometric points.

## 7.1.13: the base-compatible local homomorphism

By 01J6 a morphism G -> Y is uniquely determined by the image y of its
closed point and a local homomorphism O_(Y,y) -> O_(X,eta). To be over S,
the image of y must equal s and the two local maps out of O_(S,s) must
agree. Conversely these conditions imply that the two maps G -> S are
equal, by applying the same classification 01J6 to S. This establishes
both directions of the base-compatibility clause.

Now use the bijection of 7.1.11. By 01RV, R(X)=O_(X,eta) for an
irreducible X; no reducedness is needed. We obtain exactly the stated pair
(y, local O_(S,s)-homomorphism O_(Y,y) -> R(X)). This is also the
one-component conclusion of 0BX8 after the 01TX hypothesis conversion.
The complete unwrapped proof uses 7.1.11 and the local-source
classification already mapped for I2.4.4. Neither the word "local" nor
the base algebra may be erased, and R(X) need not be a field.

## 7.1.14: localization at any point preserves this rational-map set

For fixed Y/S the preceding bijection writes the rational-map set as
Mor_S(G,Y). An isomorphism of generic local S-schemes therefore induces
a bijection by precomposition, the variance described in 001O. It is the
S-scheme G that matters, not just its underlying abstract ring.

For any z in X let Z=Spec(O_(X,z)), with its canonical morphism to X and
the induced S-structure. Choose an affine neighbourhood Spec(A) of z.
It contains eta. The prime p defining eta is the unique minimal prime of
A, while z corresponds to q with p contained in q. By 01HV, Z=Spec(A_q).
Its unique minimal prime is pA_q, so Z is irreducible with this generic
point. The generalization description 01J7 identifies its image with eta.
The affine stalk formula 01HV and iterated localization 02C6 give

`O_(Z,pA_q) = (A_q)_(pA_q) = A_p = O_(X,eta)`.

These identifications come from the canonical map Z -> X and respect the
map from the base stalk. Thus the generic local S-schemes are canonically
isomorphic. Applying 7.1.11 to both X and Z gives the required bijection.
It is the natural restriction: the inverse image in irreducible Z of a
dense domain in X contains the generic point, and is therefore dense;
composition restricts its representative. This covers the entire source
proof, not just the abstract existence of an isomorphism of rings. It
does not assert that X and Z are isomorphic or that Z -> X is an open
immersion. The preceding "in particular" is retained with this corollary.

## Adverse comparisons: hypotheses do real work

The square-zero construction below is a specialization of the existing
01RQ example, not a new root result. Put D=Q[t] and
M=direct_sum_(n>=1) D/(t-n), and form A=D semidirect M with M^2=0.
Then Spec(A) is irreducible, its generic point is M, and A_M=Q(t): every
element of M has finite support, killed by a nonzero polynomial.
Nevertheless M remains nonzero on every nonempty open. Such an open
contains D(f) for a nonzero polynomial f, and some positive integer n
has f(n) nonzero, so the corresponding summand survives in M_f.

- Take X=S=Spec(A), Y=Spec(A/M). This closed immersion is finite and
  hence finite type, but not finitely presented: the ideal M is not
  finitely generated, because finitely many elements have finite union
  of supports. There is one S-map from the generic local scheme to Y,
  but none from any dense open of X; that would kill M on such an open.
  Thus finite type alone does not give surjectivity. Taking z=eta also
  shows that the 7.1.14 invariance fails if its hypotheses are removed.
- Over Spec(Q), take X=Y=Spec(A). The identity and the endomorphism
  killing M agree at the generic local scheme but nowhere on a dense
  open. They define distinct rational maps with the same rho image.
  The target is not locally of finite type. This does not obstruct the
  unrestricted construction of rho; it obstructs unrestricted injection.
- For X=Spec(k[e]/(e^2)), the rational maps to A^1_k sending its
  coordinate to e or zero are distinct. The generic local ring retains
  that distinction whereas its residue field does not. Irreducibility
  cannot be substituted for integrality.
- The same abstract Spec(Q), over S=A^1_Q via t->0 or via t->1, has
  respectively zero or one S-map to D(t). Forgetting the S-structure
  therefore destroys 7.1.14's invariance statement.
