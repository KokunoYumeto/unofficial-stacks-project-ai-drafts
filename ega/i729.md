# Local criteria for extending rational maps

Source-bound comparison of EGA I, §§7.2.8–7.2.9, including Lemma 7.2.8.1.
This manuscript accompanies a bounded semantic candidate checkpoint.
It uses existing Stacks results and gives explicit arguments for the source's
local-to-global steps. It is not proof-assistant verification or a claim that
the EGA integration is complete.

## The local scheme and the base convention

Let $X$ and $Y$ be schemes over $S$, with $X$ reduced and $Y$ separated over
$S$, and let $\varphi:X\dashrightarrow Y$ be a rational $S$-map. For $x\in X$
put
$$
L_x=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{X,x}),\qquad j_x:L_x\longrightarrow X.
$$
The image of $j_x$ consists of the generalizations of $x$ and lies in every
open neighbourhood of $x$. This is the canonical local-scheme morphism of
[Tag 02NA](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02NA), with its point description
in [Tag 01J7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01J7).

Pulling a representative back along $j_x$ gives a rational map on $L_x$ only
when its open domain pulls back to a dense open. EGA 7.2.8 establishes this
when $X$ is integral or locally Noetherian. Its nested lemma proves the
topological assertion needed in the second case.

We distinguish $D(\varphi)$, the union of domains of all ordinary
representatives, from $D_S(\varphi)$, the union using only $S$-morphism
representatives. As in the preceding [comparison](i727.md), these need not
coincide. The valid relative extension criterion below uses $D_S$ and a
morphism over $S$ on the whole local scheme.

## Lemma 7.2.8.1: components through a point

Suppose that the underlying space of $X$ is locally Noetherian; the coordinate
rings need not be Noetherian. The irreducible components of $L_x$ are the
traces of the irreducible components of $X$ containing $x$. Moreover, for an
open $W\subset X$,
$$
j_x^{-1}(W)\text{ is dense in }L_x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W\text{ meets every irreducible component of }X\text{ through }x.
$$
In particular this holds if $W$ is dense in $X$.

Choose an affine neighbourhood $V=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ of $x$ contained
in a Noetherian open neighbourhood. Its underlying space is Noetherian by
[Tag 0052](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0052). Write
$\mathfrak p\subset B$ for $x$. Localization identifies $L_x$ with
$\operatorname{Spec}(B_{\mathfrak p})$ and its primes with the primes
$\mathfrak q\subset\mathfrak p$ of $B$
([Tag 00E3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E3)).

Under this correspondence the minimal primes are exactly the minimal primes
of $B$ contained in $\mathfrak p$. Indeed, a smaller prime below the
contraction of a minimal prime of $B_{\mathfrak p}$ would still be contained
in $\mathfrak p$, contradicting minimality after localization. Conversely a
minimal prime contained in $\mathfrak p$ stays minimal on localization.
The minimal-prime description of components in
[Tag 00ES](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00ES), and its localized version
[Tag 00ET](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00ET), now identify the
components of $L_x$.

The components of $V$ containing $x$ are precisely the traces of the global
components through $x$. An irreducible component meeting an open has dense
irreducible trace there; taking the closure of a larger irreducible trace
would contradict maximality. This proves the correspondence, not merely a
bijection of point labels. Iterated localization also identifies the local
rings at corresponding points:
$$
(B_{\mathfrak p})_{\mathfrak qB_{\mathfrak p}}\cong B_{\mathfrak q}
$$
by [Tag 02C6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02C6).

There are finitely many components of $V$, hence finitely many of $L_x$.
An open in a finite-component Noetherian space is dense exactly when it
contains every component's generic point. In one direction, each component
has a nonempty open part not belonging to the others; a dense open meets
that part and therefore contains its generic point. In the other, the
closures of those generic points cover the space. An open of $X$ meets a
component exactly when it contains its generic point. These observations
prove the displayed equivalence.

Finally, if $W$ is dense in $X$, then $W\cap V$ is dense in $V$ and contains
all its generic points. In particular it contains those corresponding to
components through $x$. This supplies the source's final density clause.
No claim that $j_x$ is an open immersion is used.

## Completing the construction in 7.2.8

If $X$ is integral, its generic point is also the generic point of $L_x$.
Every dense open of $X$ contains it, so its pullback to $L_x$ is dense.
If $X$ is locally Noetherian, its underlying space is locally Noetherian
([Tag 01OZ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OZ)), and the lemma applies.

Choose an $S$-morphism representative $f:W\to Y$ of $\varphi$. In either
case its pullback gives a rational $S$-map
$$
\varphi_x:L_x\dashrightarrow Y.
$$
If another representative is chosen, the dense open where the two agree also
pulls back to a dense open. Thus the induced rational class is independent
of the choice. The canonical map $j_x$ is over $S$, so base compatibility is
preserved. This is the unnumbered construction following the nested proof
in the source; it is not part of that lemma's proof.

## Proposition 7.2.9: the relative extension criterion

Assume that $S$ is locally Noetherian, $X$ is reduced and either irreducible
or locally Noetherian, and $Y\to S$ is separated and of finite type.
Separatedness is included in EGA's historical term "$S$-scheme".
Then
$$
x\in D_S(\varphi)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varphi_x\text{ has an }S\text{-morphism representative }h:L_x\to Y
\text{ on all of }L_x.
$$

Necessity follows by restricting a representative defined on a neighbourhood
of $x$, since the image of $j_x$ lies in that neighbourhood.

For sufficiency, let $h$ be such a representative. If $y=h(x)$ and $s$ is
the corresponding point of $S$, the local map
$\mathcal O_{Y,y}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is a map of
$\mathcal O_{S,s}$-algebras. This base condition is essential; see
[Tag 01J6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01J6).
Because $Y/S$ is locally of finite type and $S$ locally Noetherian, it is
locally of finite presentation
([Tag 01TX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01TX)).
Part (2a) of [Tag 0BX6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BX6) spreads $h$
to an $S$-morphism $g:V\to Y$ on an open neighbourhood $V$ of $x$.

### When $X$ is integral

The generic point $\eta$ belongs to $V$, to the domain $W$ of an original
representative $f$, and to $L_x$. Since $h$ represents $\varphi_x$, the local
maps induced by $f$ and $g$ at $\eta$ agree. This is equality of the local
morphisms, not just equality of the image point. Part (1a) of Tag 0BX6 makes
$f$ and $g$ agree on an open neighbourhood of $\eta$ in $V\cap W$.
That open is dense in integral $X$. Hence $g$ represents $\varphi$, its
domain contains $x$, and it is over $S$.

### When $X$ is locally Noetherian

Shrink $V$ to a Noetherian affine neighbourhood of $x$. Only finitely many
global components meet $V$. Remove from $V$ those which do not contain $x$;
this leaves an open neighbourhood of $x$ meeting exactly the components
$X_1,\ldots,X_n$ through $x$.

Their generic points $\eta_i$ belong to $L_x$. The dense open of $L_x$ where
$h$ agrees with the induced representative contains every $\eta_i$, because
$L_x$ has finitely many components. At each $\eta_i$ the local maps of $f$
and $g$ therefore agree. Tag 0BX6 gives an open neighbourhood
$E_i\subset V\cap W$ of $\eta_i$ where they agree. Their union $E$ is
dense in $V$.

The last globalization step matters: $V$ itself need not be dense in $X$.
Put
$$
V_{\mathrm{out}}=X\setminus\overline V,\qquad
Q=V\cup(V_{\mathrm{out}}\cap W).
$$
The opens $V$ and $V_{\mathrm{out}}$ are disjoint, and their union is dense
in $X$. Indeed a nonempty open disjoint from both would be an open subset
of $\overline V$ missing its dense subset $V$. Define a morphism on $Q$
using $g$ on $V$ and $f$ on $V_{\mathrm{out}}\cap W$. The two pieces are
disjoint, so they glue without a compatibility obstruction. Both are over $S$.

The domain $Q$ is dense in $X$, and
$E\cup(V_{\mathrm{out}}\cap W)$ is a dense open on which this morphism
agrees with $f$. It therefore represents $\varphi$ and includes $x$ in
its domain. This proves the full criterion, including the source's
$V\cup(X\setminus\overline V)$ argument.

Reducedness and separatedness make relative representatives agree on all
overlaps where they represent the same rational class:
[Tags 056D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/056D) and
[01RH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RH).
Consequently they glue uniquely on $D_S(\varphi)$.

### A stronger existing Stacks route

Parts (2b) and (2c) of Tag 0BX6 spread the local $S$-map under the integral
or locally Noetherian alternatives on $X$ with $Y/S$ merely locally of finite
type. Thus the same argument gives a stronger result without the locally
Noetherian base hypothesis. This is separate target coverage, not a reason to
rewrite the source's hypotheses or omit its finite-presentation proof route.

## Why an ordinary local morphism does not suffice

The following example satisfies the source's general hypotheses but has an
ordinary local extension which is not over $S$. It shows that replacing the
right side of the criterion by an arbitrary ordinary morphism changes the
theorem. The construction uses the DVR seed in
[Tag 00PB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PB); the whole counterexample
is derived here, not attributed to that tag.

Let $k=\mathbf F_p(t_1,t_2,\ldots)$, and let $D\subset k[[z]]$ consist of
series whose coefficients together generate a finite extension of $k^p$.
For a nonzero series, division by its initial power of $z$ and inversion
of the remaining unit preserve the finite coefficient field. Thus $D$ is
a DVR with uniformizer $z$ and residue field $k$. Write $F=\operatorname{Frac}(D)$.
Its valuation property gives $F\cap k[[z]]=D$.

The series
$$
q=\sum_{i\geq1}t_i z^i
$$
does not lie in $D$, since the field generated by all its coefficients has
infinite degree over $k^p$. But $q^p\in k^p[[z]]\subset D$, and $q\notin F$.
Set
$$
C=D[q],\qquad K=F(q),\qquad A=K\cap k[[z]].
$$
The purely inseparable extension $K/F$ has degree $p$, and
$C\cong D[T]/(T^p-q^p)$ is finite over $D$. Its quotient by $z$ is
$k[T]/(T^p)$, so $C$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian local domain.

Restricting the $z$-adic valuation to $K$ shows that $A$ is a DVR with
uniformizer $z$, residue field $k$, and fraction field $K$. For every
$a\in A$, its $p$th power lies in $F\cap k[[z]]=D$. Hence $A$ is integral
over $C$. Since $A$ is integrally closed in $K$, it is the normalization of
$C$: anything integral over $C$ is also integral over $A$ and therefore lies
in $A$.

This normalization is not finite. Otherwise $A$ would be a finite $D$-module;
as $A/zA=k$ is generated by the image of $1$, Nakayama's lemma would imply
$A=D\cdot1=D$, contrary to $q\in A\setminus D$.

Introduce algebraically independent variables $T_a$ indexed by $a\in A$ and put
$$
R=C[T_a,aT_a\mid a\in A]\subset A[T_a\mid a\in A].
$$
Evaluation at $T_a=1$ for all $a$ gives a surjection $\phi:R\to A$.
Let $\mathfrak p=\phi^{-1}(zA)$. Every $T_a$ is outside $\mathfrak p$,
and therefore $A\subset R_{\mathfrak p}$ by $a=(aT_a)/T_a$.

In contrast, $A$ is not contained in $R_b$ for any $b\notin\mathfrak p$.
Choose finitely many indices $J$ supporting $b$. Set $T_a=1$ for $a\in J$
and $T_a=0$ for other indices. This specializes $R_b$ into
$$
B=C[a\mid a\in J,\ \phi(b)^{-1}]\subset A.
$$
All the finitely many generators, including $\phi(b)^{-1}$, are integral
over $C$. Thus $B$ is finite over $C$. If a constant $a\in A$ lies in $R_b$,
an identity $b^n a=r$ survives specialization and identifies its image with
the same constant $a\in B$. Consequently $A\subset R_b$ would imply
$A=B$, contradicting nonfiniteness. Setting variables outside $J$ to zero
is essential to this argument.

The affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec}(C)$ and $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$
have the common generic open $\operatorname{Spec}(K)=D(z)$. Glue them along
that open to make $S$. It is locally Noetherian and has two distinct closed
points. Set
$$
Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\subset S,\qquad X=\operatorname{Spec}(R),
$$
with $X\to S$ factoring through the $C$-chart. The inclusion $Y\to S$ is
a quasi-compact open immersion: its inverse images on the two affine charts
are affine. It is separated. By
[Tag 01TT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01TT) it is locally of finite
presentation. Quasi-compactness and
separatedness (hence quasi-separatedness) then make it of finite presentation,
and in particular of finite type.
The scheme $X$ is integral, as required by the first source alternative.

On the dense open $W=D(z)\subset X$ the equality
$R[1/z]=K[T_a\mid a\in A]$ gives an $S$-morphism $f:W\to Y$.
At $x=\mathfrak p$, the inclusion $A\subset R_{\mathfrak p}$ gives an
ordinary morphism $h:L_x\to Y$ agreeing with $f$ on the dense open $D(z)$
of $L_x$.

There is no ordinary representative of this rational class on a neighbourhood
of $x$. Such a representative restricts to some $D(b)$ with $b\notin\mathfrak p$.
Agreement at the generic point forces its map $A\to R_b$ to be the canonical
inclusion into $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. This would imply $A\subset R_b$,
already disproved. Thus $x\notin D(\varphi)$ although the induced ordinary
rational map has a morphism representative on all of $L_x$.

Finally, $h$ is not over $S$: its closed point lands in the closed point of
the $A$-chart, whereas the prescribed structure map lands in the distinct
closed point of the $C$-chart. This is precisely the missing base condition;
the example does not contradict the relative criterion.
