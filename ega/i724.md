# EGA I 7.2.1–7.2.4: where a rational map is defined

This comparison covers the beginning of EGA I §7.2, including its heading,
the nested Lemma 7.2.2.1, every proof and the parent proposition's
nonextendible-map discussion. Existing Stacks results supply the valid
mathematics. There is, however, an important distinction in reading the
source: **the largest domain for ordinary representatives need not equal
the largest domain for representatives over a specified base**.

The [checkpoint](../validation/ega-i-7.2.1-7.2.4-semantic-checkpoint-2026-09-07.json)
preserves exact French and English source identities, target blocks and
the separate conclusions below. It does not claim a new theorem, a
machine-checked proof, a printed-source erratum or completion of EGA.
Next is **I7.2.5 at French LF378 / English LF227**.

## Two domains, not one implicit convention

For an ordinary rational class f, write D(f) for the union of the dense
open domains of all representatives. This is [Stacks 0A1X](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1X)
and the definition in EGA I7.2.1. It is open because it is a union of
opens, and dense because it contains the domain of any representative.
Neither reducedness nor separatedness is needed for this definition or
the dense-open conclusion. No single representative on D(f) is yet asserted.

If X and Y are schemes over S and f has an S-morphism representative,
write D_S(f) for the union of the domains of its **S-morphism**
representatives. Then D_S(f) is dense open and D_S(f) is contained in
D(f). Equality requires an argument; it is not part of the definition.
The notation D_S is explicit comparison notation, not a change to the
French or English source.

EGA I7.1.2 defines an S-rational map existentially: its ordinary rational
class has some representative over S (French LF24–26 / English LF16).
I7.2.1 subsequently defines the domain without an explicit relative
qualifier. Its I7.2.2 and I7.2.3 statements need the distinction below
when S is not separated. We record a **domain-convention ambiguity**,
not a proven transcription error. The wording has been checked in the
two raw source editions, but this comparison does not establish its
provenance against the original printing.

## 7.2.2.1: equality on a dense open extends

Suppose X is reduced, Y is separated over S, and two S-morphisms
f_i:U_i -> Y agree on an open V contained in U_1 intersect U_2 that is
dense in X. Restrict to U_1 intersect U_2. This open is reduced and V
remains dense there.

By [01KM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KM), the relative
equalizer E is a closed subscheme. It is the inverse image of the
diagonal of Y over S under (f_1,f_2). The common map on V factors through
the diagonal, so V factors through E. Its density forces the support
of E to be the whole overlap. Reducedness then gives equality as schemes:
on an affine open Spec(A), an ideal I with V(I)=Spec(A) lies in the
nilradical, which is zero.

Equivalently, [056D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/056D) says
that a dense open of a reduced scheme is schematically dense. Thus
no proper closed subscheme of the overlap contains V. The equalizer
is the whole overlap and f_1=f_2 there. This supplies every step of the
source's proof, including the minimal closed-subscheme argument and
the final factorization through the diagonal.

Here Y need not be reduced, X need not be irreducible or separated, S
need not be separated, and there is no finiteness or Noetherian hypothesis.
The essential comparison is between **S-morphisms** and the relative
diagonal; an arbitrary representative of the ordinary class need not
have that base compatibility.

## 7.2.2: the valid maximal representative over S

Continue to assume X reduced and Y separated over S. All
S-representatives of f agree on their overlaps by the preceding
lemma. The mapping property of [01JB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01JB)
therefore glues them to a unique map D_S(f) -> Y. It is over S because
the structural-morphism identity holds on an open cover. It belongs
to the original rational class.

This representative has no extension **as an S-morphism** to a larger
dense open: any such extension would already contribute its domain to
D_S(f). Every S-representative on a smaller domain extends by restricting
this one. This proves the parent proposition's nonextendible-map
consequence, with its relative meaning made visible.

[0A1Y](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1Y) contains the same
closed-equalizer and gluing argument for the ordinary domain when Y is
absolutely separated. Its additional assertion that the maximal map is
over S assumes S separated. It must not be treated as a literal tag for
the unqualified absolute-domain reading of EGA I7.2.2 over arbitrary S.
The valid relative statement above is an existing-component derivation,
not a new root theorem.

## The counterexample to silently equating the domains

Let S be the affine line over k with doubled origin, as constructed in
[01JD](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01JD), and let X and Y be its
opposite affine charts with their open-immersion structure maps. X is
reduced and Y -> S is separated. Their common punctured line U=G_m
maps identically to Y over S.

As an ordinary rational map between affine lines, this class extends
to the coordinate identity X isomorphic to Y. Thus D(f)=X. But there
is no S-morphism X -> Y: the origin of X maps to the first origin of S,
which is missing from Y's image. Every S-representative avoids that
origin, and the map on G_m exists; hence D_S(f)=G_m.

This disproves the literal absolute-domain reading of I7.2.2 under its
stated arbitrary-base hypotheses. It also disproves that reading of
I7.2.3 with its fixed open chosen to be all X: Hom_S(X,Y) is empty, but
the S-rational class is absolutely defined everywhere. The relative
nonextendible map on G_m is extendible as an ordinary map, so that word
also needs its base qualifier.

The source's abuse-of-language convention may explain an intended
relative reading. It does not establish equality of the two sets of
points. We preserve both the source and this evidence-backed distinction;
we neither silently add S separated to the source hypotheses nor claim
the false absolute statement is covered.

## 7.2.3: maps on a fixed dense open

With X reduced and Y separated over S, fix a dense open U and send an
S-morphism U -> Y to its rational
class. Its relative domain contains U. If two such maps give the same
class, they agree on a dense witness and therefore agree on all U by
the dense-equality lemma. This proves injectivity.

Conversely, if D_S(f) contains U, restrict the unique maximal
S-representative to U. This proves surjectivity, and the two constructions
are inverse. Thus the valid statement is a canonical bijection between
Hom_S(U,Y) and the S-rational classes defined on U **relative to S**.
The source's short proof is expanded in both directions. Its
absolute-domain interpretation has the preceding caveat; it becomes
valid under the separated-base conditions of I7.2.4.

## 7.2.4: why separated bases restore compatibility

The historical vocabulary matters. An EGA “scheme” is absolutely
separated, and an EGA “S-scheme” is separated over S. Thus this corollary
assumes S absolutely separated, X reduced, and Y -> S separated. This
normalization is already recorded in the I5.4.1 comparison. By
[01KU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KU), Y is absolutely
separated, so 0A1Y gives a unique ordinary maximal representative
g:D(f) -> Y extending the initial f:U -> Y.

Let phi:X -> S and psi:Y -> S be the structure maps, and j:D(f) -> X
the inclusion. The maps psi composed with g and phi composed with j
agree on U. Their target S is separated and their domain D(f) is
reduced. The closed-equalizer argument proves equality on all D(f).
Therefore g is an S-morphism, and D(f)=D_S(f).

This supplies the proof of 0A1Y's final clause, which is omitted in the
official text, as an explanatory derivation of an existing theorem.
Conversely, its absolutely separated Y is separated over S by
[01KV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KV). These composition and
cancellation steps give the exact historical-to-modern comparison.
The conclusion concerns the maximal domain; it does **not** say that
the original map necessarily extends to all X.

## Other adverse checks and source ownership

- If reducedness is dropped, take X=Spec(k[t,e]/(e^2,t e)), Y=A^1_k.
  The maps with coordinate images 0 and e agree on dense D(t) but differ
  globally. Uniqueness fails even with an affine separated target. This
  shows why the hypothesis cannot generally be removed, not that every
  individual nonreduced-source extension problem must fail.
- If target separatedness is dropped, the two chart inclusions of
  A^1_k into the doubled-origin line agree on G_m and differ at zero.
  They are distinct full-domain representatives of one rational class.
- The opposite-chart example above isolates the separated-base issue
  even when Y -> S is an open immersion. It is not a finiteness issue.
