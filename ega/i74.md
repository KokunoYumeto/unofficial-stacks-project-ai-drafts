# Torsion, generic fibres, and the support correction to EGA I 7.4

Independent source-bound comparison in the unofficial Stacks working edition. The source-order
predecessor is the three-version comparison of EGA I 7.3.8. This text does not
alter an EGA edition, assign official Stacks tags, or claim formal verification
or completion of EGA. All twelve earlier open gaps remain outside this slice.

We write $\mathcal R_X$ for EGA's dense-open rational-function sheaf. On an
integral scheme it is the constant sheaf of the function field $K$. The
general nonreduced distinction from the regular-denominator meromorphic sheaf
is preserved; none of the assertions below identifies the two on arbitrary
schemes. Historical “simple sheaf” is normalized to **constant sheaf**, and
parenthesized powers mean sheaf direct sums with arbitrary index sets.

## 7.4.1: torsion for arbitrary sheaves of modules

Let $X$ be integral, with generic point $\eta$ and function field $K$.
For any $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal F$, define
$$
\mathcal T(\mathcal F)=
\ker\bigl(\mathcal F\longrightarrow
          \mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal R_X\bigr).
$$
The map sends a local section $s$ to $s\otimes1$. At $x\in X$ the local
ring $A_x=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is a domain with fraction field $K$, and the
stalk of this kernel is the kernel of
$M\to M\otimes_{A_x}K$, where $M=\mathcal F_x$.

For $S=A_x\setminus\{0\}$, the fraction $m/1$ is zero in $S^{-1}M$
exactly when some $a\in S$ kills $m$. Thus this is precisely algebraic
torsion at every stalk, with no quasi-coherence or finite-generation
assumption. The quotient $M/\mathcal T(M)$ embeds in $M\otimes K$,
a vector space over $K$. A submodule of such a vector space is torsion-free:
if $a\ne0$ and $av=0$, multiplication by $a^{-1}$ gives $v=0$.
Consequently $\mathcal F/\mathcal T(\mathcal F)$ is torsion-free.

If $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent, so is its torsion subsheaf. On
$U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, write $\mathcal F|_U=\widetilde M$.
The kernel above is $\widetilde{\ker(M\to M\otimes_AK)}$ because
localization is exact. These descriptions agree on restrictions, since
they describe the intrinsic kernel, hence give a quasi-coherent subsheaf.
This is the content of 0AXT with its algebraic input0537. It does not say
that an arbitrary torsion subsheaf on a scheme is quasi-coherent.

We call $\mathcal F$ torsion-free when this kernel is zero and torsion
when the kernel is all of $\mathcal F$. These definitions apply to
arbitrary module sheaves here. The cited Divisors definitions and generic
section tests are stated within the quasi-coherent category; that
distinction is crucial in 7.4.4.

## 7.4.2: the generic embedding and arbitrary rank

Suppose now $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent and torsion-free. The preceding
comparison of 7.3.6 identifies
$$
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal R_X
\simeq\underline{\mathcal F_\eta}_X
$$
canonically. The definition of torsion-freeness therefore embeds
$\mathcal F$ into this constant sheaf. Choose a basis of the $K$-vector
space $\mathcal F_\eta$, indexed by a possibly infinite set $I$. It yields
an embedding $\mathcal F\hookrightarrow\mathcal R_X^{(I)}$.

The image generates the target as an $\mathcal R_X$-module: locally every
tensor is a finite sum of elements $s\otimes r$, hence a rational scalar
combination of images $s\otimes1$. This can equivalently be checked on
stalks, avoiding an unsupported statement about global sections of tensor
products. The basis-dependent embedding into a specified free module is
not canonical, although the embedding into the generic-fibre sheaf is.

For each nonempty affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, the generic stalk is
$\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)\otimes_AK$. Its dimension is the algebraic rank of
that module and does not depend on $U$. This cardinal is the rank of
$\mathcal F$. Rank zero includes the zero sheaf and the empty index set.
No finite rank, finite type, finite presentation, or finite free embedding
over $\mathcal O_X$ has been inferred.

## 7.4.3: rank one and the scope of the converse

A quasi-coherent torsion-free sheaf of rank one embeds in $\mathcal R_X$
by choosing a basis of its one-dimensional generic fibre. In particular,
an invertible sheaf has this property: locally it is a free rank-one
module over a domain, so is torsion-free, and its generic fibre has
dimension one.

Conversely, a **nonzero quasi-coherent** submodule $\mathcal H$ of
$\mathcal R_X$ is torsion-free and has rank one. Its generic fibre is a
$K$-subspace of $K$. If that fibre were zero, the generic embedding for
quasi-coherent torsion-free sheaves would make $\mathcal H=0$, a
contradiction. Hence its generic fibre is all of $K$.

The qualifications matter. The zero submodule has rank zero. Nor are
arbitrary subsheaves of $\mathcal R_X$ quasi-coherent. The local-ring
skyscraper construction below gives an example: on $\operatorname{Spec}
\mathbb Q[t]$, the kernel of $\mathcal O_X\to S_p(\mathcal O_{X,p})$
has zero stalk at $p$ and generic stalk $\mathbb Q(t)$. A quasi-coherent
sheaf with zero stalk at $p$ has zero generic stalk, since generic
localization is a further localization of the module at $p$. Thus the
kernel is a non-quasi-coherent subsheaf of $\mathcal O_X\subset\mathcal R_X$.
The intended nonzero quasi-coherent class is made explicit; this is not
advertised as a new printed-source error in the converse phrase.

## 7.4.4: two distinct failures and a valid tensor statement

The original assertion concerns arbitrary torsion-free module sheaves:
a pure tensor of two global sections should vanish only when one section
vanishes. This is false without further hypotheses.

Set $X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q[t]$, $K=\mathbb Q(t)$, and take
distinct closed points $p=(t)$ and $q=(t-1)$. For an
$\mathcal O_{X,p}$-module $N$, define
$$
S_p(N)(U)=\begin{cases}N,&p\in U,\\0,&p\notin U.\end{cases}
$$
Restrictions are identities between opens containing $p$, and zero if
the smaller open omits $p$. Scalar multiplication is induced by the
germ map $\mathcal O_X(U)\to\mathcal O_{X,p}$.
This is a sheaf: a cover of an open containing $p$ has a member containing
$p$, and compatibility identifies exactly one value in $N$; if the open
omits $p$, every section is zero. Its stalk is $N$ at $p$ and zero at
every other point, since the complement of the closed point is open.

Take $N=K$ and similarly construct $S_q(K)$. Both sheaves are torsion-free
by the stalk test in7.4.1, and each has a nonzero global section1. At any
point at least one of their stalks is zero, so their tensor sheaf is zero.
In particular $1\otimes1=0$ although neither section is zero.

The scalar action is through $\mathcal O_{X,p}\to K$, not through the
residue field $\kappa(p)$. This is pushforward from a one-point ringed
space with its local ring, **not** pushforward of a residue-field module
under the closed subscheme inclusion. Such a residue-field action would
make $t$ act as zero at $p$, whereas here it acts invertibly in $K$.

Even finite type alone does not repair the assertion. Replace $K$ by
$\mathcal O_{X,p}$ and $\mathcal O_{X,q}$. These remain torsion-free and
give the same disjoint-support counterexample. The germ map
$\mathcal O_X\to S_p(\mathcal O_{X,p})$ is surjective on every stalk:
identity at $p$ and a map onto zero elsewhere. Thus the target is a
quotient of the one-generated sheaf $\mathcal O_X$, hence of finite type.
No surjectivity on sections over every open is required or claimed.

A sufficient corrected assertion assumes both sheaves quasi-coherent and
torsion-free. Their global sections inject into their generic fibres by
7.4.2. If $s\otimes s'$ is zero, then $s_\eta\otimes s'_\eta=0$ over
the field $K$. A pure tensor of two nonzero vectors is nonzero in arbitrary
dimensions: choose a linear functional $\lambda$ with $\lambda(s_\eta)=1$;
applying $\lambda\otimes1$ gives $s'_\eta$. Therefore one generic germ,
and hence one global section, is zero. The converse is immediate.

This repairs two separate steps. Quasi-coherence permits generic-point
detection; torsion-freeness alone did not. Also arbitrary generic fibres
are vector spaces, not necessarily subspaces of the one-dimensional
field $K$. Already $\mathcal O_X^2$ has generic fibre $K^2$. The
vector-space argument repairs this rank-one inference without imposing
a spurious rank-one hypothesis. No minimality claim is made for the
quasi-coherent repair.

## 7.4.5: dominant direct images

Let $f:X\to Y$ be dominant with both schemes integral. Their generic
points correspond, and the induced map of function fields
$L=K(Y)\hookrightarrow K=K(X)$ is injective.
For quasi-coherent torsion-free $\mathcal F$,7.4.2 embeds it into the
constant sheaf $\underline V_X$ for $V=K^{(I)}$. Direct image is left
exact: sections of a kernel are exactly the sections killed by the map.
Hence $f_*\mathcal F\hookrightarrow f_*\underline V_X$.

Every nonempty open $W\subset Y$ contains its generic point. Its inverse
image is a nonempty open in the irreducible space $X$, so the sections
of $\underline V_X$ there are exactly $V$, with identity restrictions.
Thus $f_*\underline V_X=\underline V_Y$, considered as an $L$-vector
space by the field embedding. Every local ring of $Y$ embeds in $L$;
its action on $V$ is torsion-free. Stalkwise submodules are torsion-free,
proving the printed conclusion for $f_*\mathcal F$.

There is no quasi-coherence assertion for this direct image, and no
quasi-compactness, separation, finite type, or finite-rank assumption on
$f$ or $\mathcal F$. In particular the argument does not use the false
old global rational-pullback isomorphism of7.3.8.

### Independent weakening: quasi-coherence is unnecessary here

In fact the direct image of **any** torsion-free module sheaf is
torsion-free under these hypotheses. Let $y\in Y$, $0\ne a\in\mathcal
O_{Y,y}$, and $s\in(f_*\mathcal F)_y$ satisfy $as=0$. Represent $a$ by
a regular function on a nonempty neighborhood $W$ and $s$ by a section
of $\mathcal F$ on $f^{-1}W$. Shrinking $W$ makes the product section
zero. The rational value of $a$ is nonzero in $L$: local rings of the
integral scheme inject in that field. Its image in $K$ is nonzero.
Therefore its pullback has nonzero germ in every local ring
$\mathcal O_{X,x}$ for $x\in f^{-1}W$, since those rings embed in $K$.
Stalkwise torsion-freeness gives $s_x=0$ at every such $x$. A sheaf section
with all germs zero is zero, so the original germ $s$ is zero.
This is a separate derived argument, not a deletion of the printed
quasi-coherence hypothesis or an attribution of this proof to EGA.

## 7.4.6: proper support and the role of finite type

For quasi-coherent $\mathcal F$ on an integral scheme, rationalization
is the constant generic-fibre sheaf. Consequently
$$
\mathcal F\text{ is torsion}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal F_\eta=0.
$$
If $\mathcal F$ is also of finite type, its support is closed. Indeed,
at a zero stalk choose finitely many local generators. Each has zero
germ and hence vanishes near the point; intersect their finitely many
vanishing neighborhoods to make the whole sheaf zero. This is the
argument behind01B8–01BA. A closed subset of an irreducible space
contains the generic point exactly when it is the whole space.
Thus the printed three-way equivalence follows by adjoining
$\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)\ne X$.

There is a useful separate weakening: the same literal equivalence
holds without finite type, provided quasi-coherence remains. If
$\mathcal F_x=0$, take an affine neighborhood with associated module
$M$ and prime $\mathfrak p$ representing $x$. The generic module
$M\otimes_AK$ is a further localization of $M_{\mathfrak p}$, so it
is zero. Thus a nonzero generic stalk forces every stalk nonzero.
Conversely, a zero generic stalk excludes $\eta$ from the support.
It follows that
$$
\mathcal F\text{ torsion}\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\mathcal F_\eta=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)\ne X
$$
for arbitrary quasi-coherent $\mathcal F$. This localization proof is
not the printed closed-support proof with a missing assumption.

Without finite type, proper support can be dense. On
$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q[t]$ take the associated sheaf of
$M=\bigoplus_r\mathbb Q[t]/(r)$, over all monic irreducible polynomials.
Each element has finitely many nonzero coordinates and is killed by their
product, so $M$ is torsion. Its support consists of all closed points and
not the generic point. It is dense: every nonempty principal open contains
a rational closed point avoiding the finitely many roots of its defining
polynomial. Hence this sheaf does not vanish on any nonempty open.
Proper support must not be replaced by nowhere-dense support in the
weakened assertion. The finite-type non-quasi-coherent local-ring
skyscraper above also shows why quasi-coherence cannot simply be removed.

## 7.4.7: the published correction on reducible schemes

The original paragraph extends the torsion definitions to a reduced scheme
with finitely many irreducible components, then reuses the equivalence
with merely proper support from7.4.6. Take
$X=\operatorname{Spec}(k\times k)$ and
$\mathcal F=\widetilde{(1,0)(k\times k)}$. It is finite type and
quasi-coherent; its support is one of the two points, hence proper.
But $\mathcal R_X=\mathcal O_X$, so its rationalization map is the
identity. It is nonzero torsion-free, not torsion. This refutes that
unqualified reuse of proper support.

The authoritative later replacement, Err_III12, states the correct broader
local form. Let $X$ be reduced and suppose every point has an open
neighborhood with finitely many irreducible components. For a quasi-coherent
finite-type $\mathcal F$, torsion is equivalent to its support containing
**no irreducible component of $X$**.

Here is the full local proof. On an affine neighborhood
$U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with finitely many minimal primes
$\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n$, the preceding rational-sheaf
calculation identifies its rational ring with
$\prod_i A_{\mathfrak p_i}$. Reducedness makes each factor the function
field of the corresponding component. The product is finite, so for
$\mathcal F|_U=\widetilde M$,
$$
M\otimes_A\prod_i A_{\mathfrak p_i}
\simeq\bigoplus_i M_{\mathfrak p_i}.
$$
Thus the rationalization vanishes exactly when the stalk at every
component generic point of $U$ is zero. These affine neighborhoods cover
$X$; generic points of their components are precisely the generic points
of the global components meeting the neighborhood. The criterion therefore
is globally that every component generic stalk vanish. This uses local
finite-component neighborhoods, not an unjustified globally finite list.

For a quasi-coherent module, nonzero stalk at a generic point of a component
forces nonzero stalk at all its specializations: on an affine neighborhood
of such a specialization, generic localization is a further localization.
Hence a component generic point belongs to the support exactly when the
whole component does. This proves the component-exclusion formulation.
Finite type additionally makes the support closed. A closed set containing
no component is nowhere dense here: in each finite-component neighborhood
its complement meets every component generically and is dense. Conversely,
a closed nowhere-dense set cannot contain a component, since locally that
component has a nonempty open part disjoint from the other finitely many
components. Equivalently $\mathcal F$ vanishes on a dense open subset.

Without finite type retain the generic-point/component-exclusion condition
for quasi-coherent sheaves, but not the nowhere-dense reformulation. The
dense proper-support torsion example above already separates them on an
integral scheme. This is an independent qualification of the stronger
support language, not a change to the printed finite-type replacement.
