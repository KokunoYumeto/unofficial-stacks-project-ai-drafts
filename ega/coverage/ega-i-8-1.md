# EGA I §8.1: what Stacks already supplies

This is a coverage comparison, not five new Stacks theorems. The first three
items follow from existing Stacks material. The last two are candidate ways of
assembling that material for EGA's subsequent discussion of Chevalley schemes;
no claim of their absence from the whole Stacks Project is made.

The comparison uses the [French §8 source at a fixed commit](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/ega-fr/blob/6b38875842e3723b619d4aeeda9ed260a4f94f7c/source/ega1/ega1-8-fr.tex),

## Coverage decisions

| EGA item | What it says | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I.8.1.1 | Three tests for one local ring to dominate another | Covered by existing local-map results and the proper-ideal criterion; no new theorem needed |
| I.8.1.2 | Prime ideals correspond to the embedded prime localizations of a subring of a field | Covered by localization and contraction; `L(A)` is source notation, not a new theorem |
| I.8.1.3 | A local overring has a unique center on the base ring and dominates its localization there | Covered by the existing induced local map between prime localizations |
| I.8.1.4 | When two local subrings have a common local dominator | Candidate combined statement; follows from existing components; not admitted as new root source |
| I.8.1.5 | When the intersection of two localization families equals that of their generated ring | Candidate combined statement; not admitted as new root source |

All rings below are commutative and unital. In the last four items the subrings
sit inside one specified field `K`. Equalities mean equality of subrings there,
not merely isomorphism. No hypothesis says that `K` is the fraction field of
each subring.

## Three correspondences that do not require duplicate theorems

**I.8.1.1.** For local rings `A ⊂ B`, the equality
`m_B ∩ A = m_A` and the inclusion `m_A ⊂ m_B` are exactly the equivalent
conditions in [Stacks 07BJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07BJ).
They also hold exactly when `m_A B` is a proper ideal: inclusion puts this
ideal inside `m_B`; conversely every proper ideal of the local ring `B` is
contained in its unique maximal ideal. The resulting proper ideal
`m_B ∩ A` contains the maximal ideal of `A`, so they are equal. Existence of a
maximal ideal containing a proper ideal is supplied by
[00E0](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E0).
This works without an ambient field. Reflexivity and transitivity of domination
follow by contraction; antisymmetry follows from the underlying inclusions.
The field-subring definition is already in
[00I9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00I9).

**I.8.1.2.** Put `L(A) = {A_p : p ∈ Spec(A)}`, with each localization embedded
in `K`. The embedding sends `a/s` to the quotient in `K`, using
[00CP](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00CP), and is injective because `A`
is a domain. By [00E3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E3) its maximal
ideal is `p A_p` and contracts to `p`. If two embedded localizations are equal,
their unique maximal ideals are equal, so contraction recovers the same prime.
Surjectivity onto `L(A)` is its definition. This family is not all local
overrings of `A`: `L(Q) = {Q}` even inside `Q(t)`.

**I.8.1.3.** The quantifier matters: `A ⊂ M` is equivalent to the existence of
**some** `p` for which the local ring `M` dominates `A_p`, not domination at an
arbitrarily fixed prime. Necessarily `p = m_M ∩ A`. Conversely, every element
of `A \ p` is outside `m_M` and hence is a unit in `M`, by
[00E9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E9). Localization therefore embeds
`A_p` in `M` and the inclusion is local. This is the induced local map
`R_p → S_q` already constructed in the paragraph following
[07BI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07BI), with `S=M` and `q=m_M`;
that paragraph is outside the tagged definition itself. Contraction proves
uniqueness. For a fixed `p`, mere containment does not suffice: inside `Q(t)`,
`M=Q[t]_(t)` contains `A=Q[t]` but does not even contain `A_(t-1)`.

## Two candidate combined statements

The following proofs establish the statements mathematically; they do not
establish that Stacks lacks an equivalent result elsewhere. Their potential
value is the particular comparison needed by EGA, not mathematical novelty.

### Common local dominators (I.8.1.4)

Let `M,N ⊂ K` be local subrings and let `P` be the subring generated by them.
These are equivalent:

1. A prime of `P` contracts to `m_M` and `m_N`.
2. `m_M P + m_N P` is proper in `P`.
3. A local subring of `K` dominates both `M` and `N`.

A prime as in (1) contains the ideal in (2). Given (2), choose a maximal ideal
`r` containing it. Its contraction to `M` is proper and contains `m_M`, so
equals `m_M`; the same holds for `N`. This proves (1), and `P_r ⊂ K` is the
common dominator in (3). Finally, a common dominator `D` contains `P`, and
`m_D ∩ P` supplies (1).

EGA calls such a pair *allied*. This relation is not transitive. In `Q(t)`,
`Q[t]_(t)` and `Q[t]_(t-1)` each dominate `Q`, but have no common dominator:
the ideal generated by their maximal ideals contains `t` and `t-1`, hence `1`.

### Comparing localization families (I.8.1.5)

For subrings `A,B ⊂ K`, write `C` for their generated subring. These conditions
are equivalent:

1. For every local subring `D ⊂ K` containing `C`, the localizations of `A`
   and `B` at the contractions of `m_D` are equal.
2. For every `r ∈ Spec(C)`, `A_(r∩A) = B_(r∩B)`.
3. Every allied pair from `L(A)` and `L(B)` consists of equal subrings.
4. `L(A) ∩ L(B) = L(C)`.

Conditions (1) and (2) are equivalent by taking `D=C_r` in one direction and
contracting `m_D` to `C` in the other. Condition (3) is equivalent to (1):
use a common dominator for an allied pair, and use the unique localizations
dominated by `D` for the converse.

For (2) ⇒ (4), first observe that `L(A) ∩ L(B) ⊂ L(C)` holds without (2).
Indeed, if `D=A_p=B_q`, put `r=m_D∩C`. Then `C_r ⊂ D`. Every denominator
in `A \ p` lies outside `r`, so `D=A_p ⊂ C_r`, giving equality.
Now assume (2), fix `r`, and put `D=A_(r∩A)=B_(r∩B)`. We have
`C ⊂ D ⊂ C_r`, with the last inclusion local. An element of `C \ r` lies
outside the maximal ideal of `D`, so is invertible there. Thus `C_r ⊂ D`,
and `C_r=D` belongs to both families. Conversely, (4) identifies every `C_r`
with one member of each family. Uniqueness of the dominated localization
from I.8.1.3 identifies those members as `A_(r∩A)` and `B_(r∩B)`, proving (2).

The equality in (4) is not automatic. For `A=Q[t]`, `B=Q`, and `K=Q(t)`, the
intersection on the left is empty, whereas `L(C)` contains `Q(t)`.

## Scope and next work

The first three correspondences are settled at the stated pins. A bounded
search found no equivalent named common-dominator or localization-family
statement in the root TeX sources, but searches are not absence proofs. The
last two items remain in the candidate queue for comparison with the rest of
EGA §8 and its existing scheme-theoretic counterparts before any root-source
addition. This note does not change EGA translations, Stacks tags, earlier
errata, the sequential semantic ledger, or any PDF. It is not Lean verification.
