# Pulling back rational functions: the correction to EGA I 7.3.8

This independent comparison is the source-ordered EGA I 7.3.8 proof dossier.
It preserves the superseded assertion and authoritative replacement separately.
It does not
assign an official Stacks tag, alter an EGA edition, claim machine-checked
formalization, or declare the EGA integration complete.

<a id="ega-i-738-versions"></a>

## The two historical assertions are different

The original EGA I paragraph asserted a global isomorphism of pulled-back
rational-function sheaves when both schemes have finitely many irreducible
components and the morphism maps their component generic points surjectively.
That assertion is false. The errata in EGA II, printed pp. 221–222, replace the
paragraph: for a dominant morphism of integral schemes there is a canonical
map, and the map is an isomorphism at the generic stalk. A generic-stalk
isomorphism is not a global sheaf isomorphism.

These are separately bound source versions. The pinned French Chapter I
transcription preserves the superseded assertion. The later French errata
transcription contains the replacement. The pinned English discovery text
follows the replacement and explicitly notes the change. Its difference from
the older French paragraph is therefore not a newly discovered translation
error. The separate printed-page verification is evidence for the replacement;
it must not be attributed retrospectively to an older source-admission receipt.

Here $\mathcal R_X$ denotes the sheaf obtained from regular functions on
topologically dense open subsets, as in the preceding comparison of
EGA I 7.3.1–7.3.4. We write $\mathcal K_X$ for the Stacks meromorphic sheaf,
which instead inverts regular sections, meaning nonzerodivisors locally.
The distinction matters on nonreduced schemes. On an integral scheme both
are the constant sheaf of its function field; the dense-open identification
is [Tag 01RV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RV), and the meromorphic
identification is [Tag 01X5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01X5).

## The corrected construction

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a dominant morphism of integral schemes. Denote their
generic points by $\eta_X,\eta_Y$ and their function fields by
$K=k(X),L=k(Y)$. There is a canonical morphism
$$
\tau:f^*\mathcal R_Y\longrightarrow\mathcal R_X
$$
of $\mathcal O_X$-algebras. In particular it is a morphism of
$\mathcal O_X$-modules, as asserted in the replacement. Its stalk at
$\eta_X$ is an isomorphism. No finite-type, Noetherian, quasi-compactness,
quasi-separatedness, or separatedness assumption is required.

To construct the map, take nonempty affine opens
$U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ and
$V=\operatorname{Spec}B\subset Y$ such that $f(U)\subset V$.
Dominance gives $f(\eta_X)=\eta_Y$ and an injection $B\hookrightarrow A$
for every such pair, by
[Tag 0CC1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CC1).
The induced embedding $L\hookrightarrow K$ is the map on generic local
rings, so it does not depend on the choice of affine neighborhoods.

Over $V$ the rational-function sheaf is the associated $B$-module
$\widetilde L$, and over $U$ it is the associated $A$-module
$\widetilde K$. For example, every nonempty distinguished open in either
integral affine has the same fraction field, so these identifications agree
with restrictions, not just with sections on the full affine.
The affine pullback formula in
[Tag 01I9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01I9) identifies
$f^*\mathcal R_Y|_U$ with $\widetilde{A\otimes_B L}$.
Put $S=B\setminus\{0\}$, viewed in $A$ by the injection. Then
[Tag 00DK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00DK) gives
$$
A\otimes_B L\simeq S^{-1}A\longrightarrow K,
\qquad a\otimes(b/s)\longmapsto a f^\sharp(b)/f^\sharp(s).
$$
The last map is the localization inclusion. Its associated-sheaf map is
the required map on $U$.

These local maps glue. Indeed, their formulas use the same field embedding
$L\hookrightarrow K$. Given two affine pairs and a point in the intersection
of their source opens, choose an affine target neighborhood of its image
inside the intersection of the two target opens. Choose an affine source
neighborhood of the point inside both source opens and the inverse image of
that smaller target neighborhood. On this refinement the two formulas agree.
Such refinements cover the source intersection, proving agreement and hence
unique gluing. This uses affine refinements of intersections; it does not
assume that intersections of affine opens themselves are affine.

At the generic point the pullback-stalk formula, including the tensor-stalk
identity of [Tag 01CB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01CB), gives
$$
(f^*\mathcal R_Y)_{\eta_X}
\simeq K\otimes_L L\longrightarrow K,
\qquad k\otimes l\longmapsto kl.
$$
This is an isomorphism. It is exactly the stalk conclusion of the replacement.

## What can be proved beyond the replacement?

The construction also proves two useful facts, stated here as derived
comparison results rather than additional words in the printed correction:

1. $\tau$ is injective as a sheaf morphism.
2. $\tau$ is an isomorphism if and only if
   $f^{-1}(\eta_Y)=\{\eta_X\}$ as a set of points.

In the second condition, $f^{-1}(\eta_Y)$ is the **entire** generic fiber.
The condition is not merely a bijection between sets of component generic
points; it excludes nongeneric points of $X$ lying over $\eta_Y$.

For injectivity, the map on each affine pair is $S^{-1}A\hookrightarrow K$.
Its stalk at a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ is
$S^{-1}A_{\mathfrak p}\hookrightarrow K$, which is injective because $A$
is a domain and every denominator being inverted is nonzero. Thus all stalk
maps are injective. The stalk criterion in
[Tag 0H7H](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H7H) now proves the claim.
This does not infer injectivity from the generic stalk alone, and does not
assume that pullback preserves arbitrary injections.

For the isomorphism criterion put $C=S^{-1}A$. The prime correspondence for
localization, [Tag 00E3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E3), identifies
$$
\operatorname{Spec}C
\quad\text{with}\quad
\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A:
      \mathfrak p\cap S=\varnothing\}
=U\cap f^{-1}(\eta_Y).
$$
The equality holds because avoidance of every nonzero element of $B$ says
exactly that the contraction of $\mathfrak p$ to $B$ is zero.
Every nonempty $U$ contains $\eta_X$, represented by the zero prime.

If the entire generic fiber is $\{\eta_X\}$, the domain $C$ has only one
prime ideal, namely zero. It is a field: a nonzero nonunit would belong to a
nonzero maximal ideal. Since $A\subset C\subset K=\operatorname{Frac}A$,
this field must be $K$. The map on every such $U$ is therefore an
isomorphism, and so is $\tau$.

Conversely, if $\tau$ is an isomorphism, its sections on the affine $U$
give an isomorphism $C\to K$. Thus $\operatorname{Spec}C$ consists of the
zero prime alone. The compatible affine pairs cover $X$, so no nongeneric
point of $X$ lies above $\eta_Y$.
The scheme-theoretic generic fiber has this point-set fiber as its underlying
space by [Tag 01K1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01K1). In this case
its affine pieces are all $\operatorname{Spec}K$ with the compatible canonical
identifications, so the generic fiber itself is $\operatorname{Spec}K$.
No dimension formula or finiteness of $K/L$ is involved.

### Why the original isomorphism fails

Take $f:\mathbb A^1_k\to\operatorname{Spec}k$. Its source and target are
integral, and their singleton sets of generic points map bijectively.
Nevertheless
$$
f^*\mathcal R_Y=\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k},
\qquad \mathcal R_X=\widetilde{k(t)}.
$$
At the closed point $(t)$, the map is
$k[t]_{(t)}\hookrightarrow k(t)$ and is not surjective: $1/t$ is missing.
At the generic point it is an isomorphism. The whole generic fiber is all
of $\mathbb A^1_k$, not just its generic point.

The older proof's appeal to constancy of quasi-coherent
$\mathcal R_X$-modules cannot repair this. The pullback is initially an
$\mathcal O_X$-module. It need not carry the compatible
$\mathcal R_X$-module structure required by that constancy result. Here
such a structure on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}$ would make multiplication
by $t$ invertible at $(t)$, which it is not.

A dominant birational morphism does satisfy the derived criterion. In the
local calculation $C$ contains $L$ and lies in $K$; if $L=K$ under the
generic-field map, then $C=K$. Birationality is sufficient, not necessary:
for any extension of fields $L\subset K$, the morphism
$\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}L$ has $\tau$ an isomorphism.
This includes transcendental and infinitely generated extensions.

## What remains true with finitely many components?

Let $X,Y$ have finitely many irreducible components, without assuming they
are reduced. Suppose every component generic point of $X$ maps to a component
generic point of $Y$. Then there is still a canonical map
$$
f^{-1}\mathcal R_Y\longrightarrow\mathcal R_X,
\qquad
f^*\mathcal R_Y\longrightarrow\mathcal R_X,
$$
and the second map is an isomorphism at every component generic stalk of $X$.
Surjectivity onto all target component generic points is unnecessary.
This is a derived comparison, not the statement of the integral replacement.

To prove existence, first note that a dense open in a scheme with finitely
many components contains every component generic point. For each component,
remove the other finitely many components. The resulting nonempty open lies
in that component, and a dense open must meet it; an open meeting an
irreducible component contains its generic point.

Now let $W\subset V\subset Y$ be open with $W$ dense in $V$, and let
$U\subset X$ be open with $f(U)\subset V$. Every component generic point
of $U$ is a component generic point of $X$ contained in $U$, and its image
is a component generic point of $V$. Hence $f^{-1}(W)\cap U$ contains all
component generic points of $U$ and is dense there. Pullback of a regular
section on $W$ therefore represents a rational section on $U$.
An agreement on a further dense open remains an agreement on a dense open
after pullback by the same argument. Thus the map on rational classes is
well-defined and respects restrictions and ring operations. Sheafification
gives the first displayed map, and multiplication by $\mathcal O_X$ gives
the second. No target component not met by $X$ entered the construction.
For instance, the inclusion of one summand
$\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}k\amalg\operatorname{Spec}k$
misses a target generic point but still has the asserted rational pullback.

If $\xi$ is a component generic point of $X$ and $\eta=f(\xi)$, the
finite-component formula in Tag 01RV, followed by restriction away from the
other components, gives
$$
\mathcal R_{X,\xi}=\mathcal O_{X,\xi},
\qquad \mathcal R_{Y,\eta}=\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}.
$$
Consequently the relevant stalk map is
$$
\mathcal O_{X,\xi}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}}
\mathcal O_{Y,\eta}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{X,\xi},
$$
the multiplication isomorphism. These generic local rings need not be fields
when the schemes are nonreduced. This proof establishes existence and the
generic-stalk result, not a global injection or isomorphism.

## Embedded torsion separates rational and meromorphic pullbacks

Let
$$
A=k[t,e]/(e^2,te),\qquad X=\operatorname{Spec}A.
$$
As a $k$-vector space $A=k[t]\oplus ke$, so $e\ne0$. Its sole minimal
prime is $(e)$. At that prime $t$ is inverted and $e$ is killed, giving
$A_{(e)}=k(t)$. The dense-open rational sheaf is therefore
$\mathcal R_X=\widetilde{k(t)}$.

For $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ the rational pullback map is
$\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal R_X$. It kills $e$ and is not injective.
This is also a genuine stalk kernel at $\mathfrak m=(t,e)$: an element
outside $\mathfrak m$ has nonzero constant term $c$, and multiplication by
it sends $e$ to $ce\ne0$. Thus $e$ survives in $A_{\mathfrak m}$.
The embedded associated point is visible directly from
$\operatorname{ann}(e)=(t,e)$.

In contrast, the defining map $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal K_X$ is
injective because it inverts nonzerodivisors. Moreover this particular
morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is flat, so its meromorphic pullback is
defined by [Tag 02OU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02OU), case (7).
There is no contradiction: the two target sheaves are different.

There is an even sharper example using the same ring. Let
$g:X\to\mathbb A^1_k$ come from $k[t]\to A$.
The component generic point still maps to the generic point. Its rational
pullback is defined and is an isomorphism, since
$$
A\otimes_{k[t]}k(t)=k(t).
$$
But its meromorphic pullback is not defined in the sense of
[Tag 02OT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02OT): the nonzerodivisor
$t$ on the target pulls back to a zero divisor, since $te=0$ with $e\ne0$.
Even an isomorphism of rational pullbacks is not a substitute for the
regular-denominator condition.

The conditional bridge is
[Tag 0EMF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EMF). It requires both that
every weakly associated point be a component generic point and that every
quasi-compact open have finitely many irreducible components. Under those
hypotheses the meromorphic sheaf agrees with the generic-local-ring
decomposition, is quasi-coherent, and computes dense-open rational functions.
The embedded-torsion example fails the first hypothesis. Generic-point
mapping suffices for meromorphic pullback with a reduced source, as in
Tag 02OU(4), but not for an arbitrary nonreduced source.

## Disposition and source boundaries

The corrected EGA I 7.3.8 is covered by the explicit construction above and
existing Stacks dependencies. Its global injectivity and exact generic-fiber
criterion are separately identified derived conclusions. The finite-component
construction explains which part of the older argument survives, while the
affine-line and embedded-torsion examples refute the unwarranted extensions.
This comparison does not duplicate a root theorem or close unrelated gaps.
