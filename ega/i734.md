# The sheaf obtained from dense-open rational functions

This comparison treats EGA I 7.3.1–7.3.4, including the restriction statement
following 7.3.2 and the entire proof of 7.3.3. It gives an independently
worded derivation from existing Stacks results. It does not introduce an
official tag, alter a source edition, or claim formal verification or a
completed EGA integration.

## Which rational-function sheaf?

For an open subscheme $U$ of a scheme $X$, let $R(U)$ be the ring of regular
functions defined on topologically dense open subsets of $U$, where two
functions represent the same element if they agree on a further dense open.
The ring construction in
[Tag 01RU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RU) and the dense-open
colimit in [Tag 01RV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RV) use this
notion. We denote its sheafification by $\mathcal R_X$.

We reserve $\mathcal K_X$ for the sheaf of meromorphic functions in
[Tag 01X2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01X2), constructed by
inverting regular sections. Dense-open restriction and regular-denominator
localization need not give the same $\mathcal O_X$-algebra. In particular,
we will not use a meromorphic-sheaf theorem to add an unprinted hypothesis
to EGA's theorem or to suppress its nonreduced case.

## 7.3.1: restriction makes a presheaf of algebras

Let $V\subset U$ be open. If $W\subset U$ is dense and open, then
$W\cap V$ is dense in $V$: every nonempty open subset of $V$ is an open
subset of $U$ and therefore meets $W$. Restricting a representative on $W$
thus gives a representative on $V$.

The same observation applies to the dense open on which two representatives
agree. Consequently restriction is well-defined on rational classes and
gives a map
$$
\rho_{V,U}:R(U)\longrightarrow R(V).
$$
To add or multiply two classes, restrict their representatives to the
intersection of their dense domains and add or multiply the sections there.
The intersection is still dense and open. Replacing either representative
does not change the resulting class, since the old and new results agree
on a common further dense open. Ring identities follow after the same
common-domain restriction. Restriction preserves these operations and $1$.

A regular function on all of $U$ gives a rational class. This makes $R(U)$
a $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)$-algebra. The algebra compatibility of
$\rho_{V,U}$ is with the scalar restriction
$\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)\to\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_X)$: for $a$ regular on $U$
and $r\in R(U)$,
$$
\rho_{V,U}(ar)=a|_V\,\rho_{V,U}(r).
$$
This is the source's di-homomorphism assertion, not linearity over two
unrelated scalar rings. For $W\subset V\subset U$, actual restrictions
of sections compose, so
$\rho_{W,U}=\rho_{W,V}\circ\rho_{V,U}$; restriction to $U$ itself is the
identity. We have therefore obtained a presheaf of $\mathcal O_X$-algebras.
No reducedness, Noetherianity, separatedness, or finiteness of components
is needed for this construction.

The empty open is included: it has the zero ring of sections and rational
functions, and restriction to it is the unique ring map.

## 7.3.2: sheafification and its restriction to an open

Define $\mathcal R_X$ to be the sheaf of rings associated to this presheaf.
[Tag 0087](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0087) supplies sheafification
with algebraic structure. The compatible maps
$\mathcal O_X(U)\to R(U)$ induce a morphism of sheaves of rings
$$
\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow\mathcal R_X,
$$
and hence the required $\mathcal O_X$-algebra structure. Its underlying
module construction agrees with
[Tag 0089](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0089).
There is no assertion that this structural morphism is injective.

If $j:V\hookrightarrow X$ is an open immersion, the rational presheaf
constructed on $V$ is exactly the restriction of the one constructed on $X$.
Indeed, an open $T\subset V$ is open in $X$, its structure sheaf is the
same restriction, and the dense domains used in $R(T)$ are dense in $T$
in either construction.

Sheafification also respects this restriction. At a point of $V$, the
neighbourhoods contained in $V$ are cofinal among neighbourhoods in $X$.
Thus the two constructions have the same germs; the sections that are
locally represented by presheaf sections are the same, and their gluing
is the same. This identifies the sheaves and their scalar actions:
$$
j^{-1}\mathcal R_X=\mathcal R_V
\quad\text{as }\mathcal O_V\text{-algebras}.
$$
This argument accounts for the unnumbered restriction statement after the
definition, not only the numbered definition itself.

## 7.3.3: locally finite components

Suppose that the family of irreducible components of $X$ is locally finite.
Then $\mathcal R_X$ is quasi-coherent as an $\mathcal O_X$-module. For every
open $U\subset X$ meeting only finitely many components, the canonical map
from rational classes to sections is an isomorphism, and
$$
R(U)=\Gamma(U,\mathcal R_X)
\cong\prod_{\eta_i\in U}\mathcal O_{X,\eta_i},
$$
where the $\eta_i$ are the generic points of the components meeting $U$.
These factors are generic **local rings**, not necessarily fields.
The assertion includes schemes whose coordinate rings are nonreduced
or non-Noetherian.

### Reduction to finitely many components

Components of an open subscheme are exactly the traces of the global
components that meet it. A component meeting an open has dense irreducible
trace. If that trace were contained in a larger irreducible closed subset
of the open, taking closure in $X$ would contradict maximality of the
original component. Conversely, the closure of a component of the open
lies in a global component; taking its trace proves equality. This also
proves uniqueness of the correspondence.

By local finiteness, every point therefore has an open neighbourhood with
only finitely many components. If the underlying space of $X$ is locally
Noetherian, a Noetherian open neighbourhood has finitely many components
by [Tag 0052](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0052), so the stated
special case follows. This reasoning concerns the topology and does not
assert that an affine coordinate ring is Noetherian. The stronger scheme
Noetherian hypothesis in [Tag 0BA8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BA8)
is not needed here.

First work on a scheme with finitely many components and generic points
$\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n$. An open $U$ meets a component if and only if it
contains its generic point. Tag 01RV applied to $U$ identifies $R(U)$
with the product of the corresponding generic local rings. The
identification sends a rational class to its germs at those points.
It follows directly that restriction from $U$ to $V\subset U$ is the
coordinate projection that forgets the factors with $\eta_i\notin V$.
Thus the isomorphisms are canonical and compatible with restriction,
not merely abstract ring isomorphisms for separate opens.

### The full sheaf argument

On a finite-component scheme this product presheaf is already a sheaf.
To prove uniqueness, sections whose restrictions agree on an open cover
have the same coordinate at each $\eta_i$ in the covered open, since at
least one member of the cover contains $\eta_i$.

For existence, take a compatible family of tuples on a cover
$U=\bigcup_\alpha V_\alpha$. For each $\eta_i\in U$, choose a member
$V_\alpha$ containing it and take the $i$th coordinate of its tuple.
Another such choice gives the same coordinate: the intersection of the
two members still contains $\eta_i$, and compatibility applies there.
The resulting tuple on $U$ restricts to each given tuple. This proves
gluing, including arbitrary infinite open covers. Hence the presheaf
agrees with its sheafification.

Now take any open $U$ of the original scheme that meets finitely many
global components. Apply the preceding argument to $U$ itself and use
the restriction identity in 7.3.2. This proves the displayed section
formula without requiring $U$ to be affine or quasi-compact. We have
not replaced locally finite components by globally finitely many components.

### The affine localization argument and quasi-coherence

Choose an affine neighbourhood $V=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ meeting finitely
many components. By [Tag 00ES](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00ES),
these correspond to the finitely many minimal primes
$\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_n$ of $A$. Put
$$
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n A_{\mathfrak p_i}
 =\prod_{i=1}^n A_{\mathfrak p_i}.
$$
The equality of direct sum and product here is a finite one. The product
is an $A$-algebra by the diagonal localization map and coordinatewise
multiplication.

For $f\in A$, localization of the finite direct sum gives
$$
M_f=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n(A_{\mathfrak p_i})_f
   \cong\prod_{f\notin\mathfrak p_i}A_{\mathfrak p_i}.
$$
If $f\notin\mathfrak p_i$, its image is already a unit in
$A_{\mathfrak p_i}$ and that factor is unchanged. If
$f\in\mathfrak p_i$, its image is nilpotent by
[Tag 00EU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00EU), so inverting it
gives the zero ring and zero module. Only the nilpotence of this single
element is used; the maximal ideal is not asserted to be nilpotent.
There is no Noetherian ring hypothesis and no interchange of localization
with an infinite product.

The surviving factors are precisely the generic points in $D(f)$.
[Tag 00E3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E3) describes this
localization topology. By
[Tag 01HV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01HV),
$\widetilde M(D(f))=M_f$, with the usual localization restriction maps.
Our description of $R(D(f))$ has exactly those factors and restriction
maps: further localization either leaves a factor unchanged or deletes it.

Both sheaves consequently agree on the principal-open basis, compatibly
with restriction and the $A$-algebra operations. On an arbitrary open,
glue the matching principal-open sections; the sheaf axiom gives existence
and independence of the chosen basis cover. This produces the canonical
isomorphism
$$
\mathcal R_X|_V\cong\widetilde M.
$$
By [Tag 01I7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01I7), an associated
module sheaf is quasi-coherent. These affine neighbourhoods cover $X$,
so $\mathcal R_X$ is quasi-coherent. This completes the final affine
argument of the source's proof, rather than stopping after the section
formula or the sheaf axioms.

The source writes $\mathcal O_{X,\eta_i}=R(X_i)$ within that proof.
The factor used here is unambiguously $\mathcal O_{X,\eta_i}$. Replacing
$X_i$ there by its reduced induced subscheme would generally discard
generic nilpotents. We make no printed-erratum claim from this notation.

## Why this is not the general meromorphic sheaf

Let $k$ be a field and
$$
A=k[t,e]/(e^2,te),\qquad X=\operatorname{Spec}(A).
$$
The element $e$ is nonzero: its image in $A/(t)=k[e]/(e^2)$ is nonzero.
Every prime contains $e$, and $A/(e)=k[t]$, so the unique minimal prime
is $(e)$. The element $t$ lies outside that prime. Consequently $D(t)$
is dense, even though $te=0$ and $t$ is a zerodivisor in $A$.

At the generic point, inverting $t$ kills $e$ and inverting the nonzero
polynomials in $k[t]$ gives
$$
A_{(e)}=k(t).
$$
By the finite-component computation above,
$R(X)=\Gamma(X,\mathcal R_X)=k(t)$. The canonical map
$A\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal R_X)$ therefore kills the nonzero element $e$.

In contrast, $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal K_X$ is injective. Before
sheafification, localization at regular sections is injective: an element
that becomes zero is annihilated by a regular denominator, hence was zero.
Taking germs preserves these injections, and sheafification preserves
the germs. Thus the associated map of sheaves is injective as well.
An isomorphism between $\mathcal R_X$ and $\mathcal K_X$ compatible with
their $\mathcal O_X$-algebra structures would preserve the kernel of the
structural map, which is impossible in this example.

[Tag 0EMF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EMF) gives a meromorphic
comparison under additional hypotheses: every weakly associated point is
generic, and every quasi-compact open has finitely many components. The
first hypothesis is not present in EGA 7.3.3. It cannot be imported into
the source theorem or silently removed from the Stacks theorem.
For an integral scheme, the special identification in
[Tag 01X5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01X5) does make
$\mathcal K_X$ the constant function-field sheaf, and then it agrees
with $\mathcal R_X$. That special case does not settle the nonreduced
comparison above.

## 7.3.4: decomposition into reduced components

Suppose now that $X$ is reduced and has finitely many irreducible
components. Give each component $X_i$ its reduced closed subscheme
structure, uniquely provided by
[Tag 01J3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01J3), and write
$h_i:X_i\hookrightarrow X$ for the inclusion. Then
$$
\mathcal R_X\cong\prod_i h_{i,*}\mathcal R_{X_i}
\quad\text{as }\mathcal O_X\text{-algebras}.
$$

Here is a proof of the corollary; no additional proof is printed after its
statement in either pinned source. Each $X_i$ is integral. The generic
local ring $\mathcal O_{X,\eta_i}$ is a field by Tag 00EU, because $X$
is reduced. On an affine neighbourhood it is the localization at a
minimal prime; quotienting by that prime identifies it with the fraction
field of the corresponding reduced component. Thus it is canonically
the function field of $X_i$.

For any open $U\subset X$, the definition of direct image gives
$$
(h_{i,*}\mathcal R_{X_i})(U)=\mathcal R_{X_i}(U\cap X_i).
$$
If $U$ meets $X_i$, this is its function field
$\mathcal O_{X,\eta_i}$; otherwise it is the zero ring. Their finite
product is exactly the section formula for $\mathcal R_X(U)$ proved
above. Restrictions are the same coordinate projections on both sides.
The identifications are therefore compatible on all opens and give the
claimed sheaf isomorphism.

They also respect the scalar action: a section of $\mathcal O_X(U)$
acts after restriction to the reduced component and passage to its generic
field, which is its generic-germ action on the corresponding factor of
$\mathcal R_X(U)$. The isomorphism is thus an algebra isomorphism, not
only an isomorphism of modules or sets. For affine morphisms the same
direct-image compatibility is expressed in part (2) of
[Tag 01I9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01I9).

Reducedness cannot be deleted from this reduced-component decomposition.
For $X=\operatorname{Spec}(k[e]/(e^2))$, the only nonempty dense open is
$X$ itself, so $\mathcal R_X(X)=k[e]/(e^2)$. Its only reduced component
is $\operatorname{Spec}(k)$, whose rational sheaf has sections $k$.
The structural map to the former does not kill $e$, whereas the map to
the latter does. No $\mathcal O_X$-algebra isomorphism as in the
corollary is possible. This also illustrates why the generic factors of
7.3.3 must remain local rings until reducedness is imposed.

All formulas include the empty open and the empty scheme. The empty
product in rings is the zero ring, matching their sections.
