# Rational sections, regular functions, and reduced closures

An unofficial draft comparison of EGA I, §§7.2.5–7.2.7 with the Stacks project.

These three results explain when a partially defined section remains a section, how regular functions on a dense open are recovered from rational functions, and how the closure of a section detects whether it extends. The proofs below use existing Stacks results. This is an independently written exposition and comparison, not a new Stacks theorem, a proof-assistant formalization, or a claim that the EGA integration is complete.

## Conventions: extending a map is not always extending a section

A rational map from a scheme $X$ to a scheme $Y$ is represented by a morphism on a dense open subset of $X$. Two representatives give the same rational map if they agree on a dense open subset of $X$ contained in both domains. The ordinary domain of definition is the union of the domains of these representatives. This is the convention of [Stacks, Tag 0A1X](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1X).

When schemes carry a specified base, the following distinctions matter:

| Notation | Representatives allowed in taking the union |
|---|---|
| $D(\varphi)$ | All morphisms representing the ordinary rational map $\varphi$. |
| $D_S(\varphi)$ | Only representatives that are morphisms over $S$. |
| $D_X(\varphi)$, for $p:Y\to X$ | Only representatives $h:V\to Y$ with $p\circ h:V\to X$ equal to the inclusion. These are sections over their domains. |

These are open subsets, not assertions that an arbitrary collection of representatives glues. Under suitable reducedness and separatedness assumptions the relevant representatives do glue uniquely. Without those assumptions, even a domain equal to the whole source need not determine a unique morphism.

For an open $V\subset X$, write $j_V:V\hookrightarrow X$ for the inclusion.

EGA I, §7.1.2 defines a rational $S$-map by the existence of an $S$-morphism representative and then permits the shorter expression “rational map” when the base is understood. We keep that historical convention visible rather than silently interpreting every representative as a morphism over the base. Throughout this exposition, EGA's *prescheme* is expressed using the modern term *scheme*; separatedness is imposed only where it is stated.

The main tool is the following uniqueness principle. On a reduced scheme, a dense open is scheme-theoretically dense. Consequently, two morphisms over $S$ into an $S$-separated target that agree on a dense open agree everywhere on their common domain. The first assertion is [Tag 056D](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/056D), and the second is [Tag 01RH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RH). This is a relative statement: the two morphisms being compared must preserve the same base.

## EGA I, 7.2.5: a rational section retains its section identity over the base

Let $X$ be reduced, let $X$ and $Y$ be separated over a scheme $S$, and let $p:Y\to X$ be an $S$-morphism. Let $U\subset X$ be dense open and let $g:U\to Y$ satisfy $p\circ g=j_U$. Write $\varphi$ for the rational map represented by $g$.

Every $S$-morphism representative of $\varphi$ is a section of $p$ over its domain. In particular,

$$
D_S(\varphi)=D_X(\varphi),
$$

and there is a unique section on this open subset representing $\varphi$.

To prove the first assertion, take an $S$-morphism representative $h:V\to Y$. There is a dense open $W\subset U\cap V$ on which $h$ and $g$ agree. Both $p\circ h$ and the inclusion $V\to X$ are $S$-morphisms. They agree on $W$, so reducedness of $V$ and separatedness of $X/S$ give

$$
p\circ h=j_V.
$$

Conversely, any section of $p$ is an $S$-morphism because $p$ is over $S$. This proves the equality of the two domains.

For existence on their union, compare two such representatives on the overlap of their domains. They agree on a dense open of that overlap, hence agree everywhere there by reducedness and separatedness of $Y/S$. The morphisms therefore glue, and their section identities glue as well. The same argument proves uniqueness. The gluing step is supplied by [Tag 01JB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01JB); the closed-equalizer argument underlying uniqueness is [Tag 01KM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KM). This is the relative version of the mechanism in [Tag 0A1Y](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1Y), not an application of that tag's absolute separatedness hypothesis to a possibly non-separated scheme.

### Why the ordinary domain can be larger

Let $k$ be a field. Form the line with two origins $B$ by gluing two affine lines $B_1$ and $B_2$ along their common punctured line $\mathbf G_{m,k}$. The coordinate functions on the two charts agree on the overlap, and hence define a morphism

$$
q:B\longrightarrow\mathbf A^1_k
$$

which identifies the two origins. This is the construction in [Tag 01JD](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01JD).

Set $S=X=B$, take $Y=B_1$, and let $p:B_1\hookrightarrow B$ be the open immersion. The assumptions of the result hold: $X$ is reduced, $X\to S$ is the identity, and $Y\to S$ is an open immersion, so both structure morphisms are separated. Over $U=\mathbf G_{m,k}$, let $g$ be the evident section.

After identifying $\mathbf A^1_k$ with $B_1$, the morphism $q$ extends $g$ as an ordinary morphism on all of $B$. Thus $D(\varphi)=B$. But no section of $p$ can be defined at the origin belonging only to $B_2$: that point has no preimage under $p$. A section is defined on all of $B_1$, so

$$
D(\varphi)=B,
\qquad
D_S(\varphi)=D_X(\varphi)=B_1.
$$

In particular, the relative domain is $B_1$, not merely the punctured line. This example rules out the stronger assertion that every ordinary extension remains a section. It does not contradict the relative result proved above, nor does this comparison adjudicate a correction to the historical printed text.

## EGA I, 7.2.6: regular functions on a dense open

Let $X$ be a reduced scheme and let $U\subset X$ be dense open. Sending a section to its rational class gives a canonical bijection

$$
\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)
\longrightarrow
\{r\mid r\text{ is a rational function on }X,
\ U\subset D(r)\}.
$$

Here a rational function is a rational map to $\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$, as in [Tag 01RT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01RT). For every scheme $V$, morphisms $V\to\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$ correspond to elements of $\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_V)$ by [Tag 01JH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01JH).

For injectivity, let two sections on $U$ define the same rational function. Their associated morphisms agree on a dense open of $U$. Since $U$ is reduced and $\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$ is separated, the uniqueness principle makes them equal on $U$.

For surjectivity, let $r$ be defined at every point of $U$. The target $\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$ is separated because it is affine ([Tag 01KN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KN)). Thus [Tag 0A1Y](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A1Y) gives a unique representative of $r$ on its whole domain of definition. Restrict it to $U$ and use the correspondence with regular functions. The two constructions are inverse.

No integrality, Noetherianity, finite-type hypothesis, or separatedness of $X$ is needed.

### Comparing scalar functions with EGA's rational sections

EGA I, §§7.1.2–7.1.3 describes a rational function through rational sections of

$$
\mathbf A^1_X=X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf Z)}\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}
\longrightarrow X.
$$

A regular function $s$ on $V$ corresponds to the graph morphism $(j_V,s):V\to\mathbf A^1_X$. It is not true that every ordinary morphism into $\mathbf A^1_X$ has its first component equal to the inclusion into $X$. Nevertheless, these particular rational graphs have an exact domain comparison.

Let $\gamma$ be the ordinary rational map to $\mathbf A^1_X$ represented by such a graph, and let $r$ be its scalar rational function. If $h:V\to\mathbf A^1_X$ is any ordinary representative of $\gamma$, its second projection $s_h:V\to\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$ represents $r$. Replacing $h$ by $(j_V,s_h)$ produces an $X$-section on the same domain. On a dense open where $h$ equals the original graph, this new graph equals it too, so the new graph still represents $\gamma$.

Conversely, any representative $s:V\to\mathbf A^1_{\mathbf Z}$ of $r$ gives an $X$-section $(j_V,s)$ representing $\gamma$. Taking unions of these same domains yields

$$
D(\gamma)=D_X(\gamma)=D(r).
$$

This equality of domains holds even when $X$ is not reduced. Reducedness enters the uniqueness and the bijection of the preceding result. The graph construction is special to this product target; it does not remove the ordinary-versus-relative distinction for arbitrary targets in 7.2.5 or 7.2.7.

## EGA I, 7.2.7: the reduced closure criterion for extending a section

Let $Y$ be reduced, let $f:X\to Y$ be separated, let $U\subset Y$ be dense open, and let $g:U\to X$ be a section over $U$. Give the closed subset $\overline{g(U)}\subset X$ its reduced induced closed subscheme structure, and denote this subscheme by $Z$. Such a structure exists and is unique by [Tag 01J3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01J3).

Then $g$ extends to a section $Y\to X$ if and only if

$$
f|_Z:Z\longrightarrow Y
$$

is an isomorphism. If the extension exists, it is unique.

First examine the part lying over $U$. The morphism $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ is separated. Its section $g$ is therefore a closed immersion by [Tag 01KT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KT). Since $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$ and contains $g(U)$, taking closure and then restricting to this open gives

$$
\overline{g(U)}^{\,X}\cap f^{-1}(U)=g(U).
$$

This set-theoretic equality also identifies the closed subschemes of $f^{-1}(U)$: the restriction of $Z$ is reduced, and the image subscheme of $g$ is isomorphic to the reduced scheme $U$. They are therefore the same reduced closed subscheme with that support. In particular,

$$
Z\cap f^{-1}(U)=g(U)
$$

scheme-theoretically, and $f$ identifies this open of $Z$ with $U$.

Suppose now that $f|_Z$ is an isomorphism. Its inverse, followed by the inclusion $Z\hookrightarrow X$, is a section of $f$. The preceding identification shows that it restricts to $g$ over $U$.

Conversely, suppose that a section $h:Y\to X$ extends $g$. Separatedness of $f$ makes $h$ a closed immersion. Its image subscheme $H$ is reduced because it is isomorphic to $Y$. Since $h$ is a homeomorphism onto $H$ and $U$ is dense in $Y$, the subset $g(U)=h(U)$ is dense in $H$. As $H$ is closed in $X$, it follows that

$$
\overline{g(U)}^{\,X}=H
$$

as underlying spaces. More explicitly, closedness gives $\overline{g(U)}^{\,X}\subset H$, while density gives the reverse inclusion. The reduced closed structures then agree, so $Z=H$ as subschemes. Thus $h$ identifies $Y$ with $Z$ and $f|_Z$ is its inverse.

Finally, two global sections agreeing on $U$ are equal by the uniqueness principle applied over $Y$, since the source $Y$ is reduced and the target $X$ is separated over $Y$.

### No retrocompactness assumption is required

The proof used reduced induced closed structures, sections of separated morphisms, and density. It did not require $U\hookrightarrow Y$ to be quasi-compact. In the language of scheme-theoretic images, [Tag 056B](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/056B) identifies $Z$ with the scheme-theoretic image of $g$, since its source $U$ is reduced.

The nearby [Tag 0CNG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNG) is related but is not a single-tag substitute for the criterion just proved. It assumes that the open is retrocompact and concludes that the scheme-theoretic image is isomorphic to the base over that open. Here reducedness gives the needed equality over $U$ directly, and the criterion concerns an isomorphism over all of $Y$.

### What the hypotheses and the domain convention exclude

In this result, saying that the rational section is defined everywhere means $D_Y(g)=Y$, where only section representatives are allowed. They then glue uniquely by the same argument used above. If instead one takes the ordinary domain $D(g)$, the assertion can fail.

Indeed, in the doubled-origin example take $Y=B$, $X=B_1$, $f:B_1\hookrightarrow B$, and $U=\mathbf G_{m,k}$. The coordinate-collapse morphism $B\to B_1$ extends the ordinary rational map on all of $B$, but $f$ has no global section. The reduced closure is $Z=B_1$, and $Z\to B$ is not an isomorphism. Thus the ordinary-domain reading of the historical parenthetical is stronger than the valid section criterion. This is a distinction between precise readings of the source, not an allocated printed-source erratum.

Reducedness and separatedness also have real roles. If $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$, $X=Y$, $f=\operatorname{id}_Y$, and $U=Y$, the identity is a global section but its reduced closure is $Y_{\mathrm{red}}$, which is not isomorphic to $Y$. If separatedness is removed instead, take the coordinate-collapse morphism $f:B\to\mathbf A^1_k$ and one of its chart sections on all of $\mathbf A^1_k$. That section exists, but its image is dense and not closed in $B$. Its reduced closure is $B$, and $f$ is not an isomorphism.

## What this comparison establishes

The section-preservation argument, the correspondence for regular functions, and the reduced-closure criterion are consequences of existing Stacks results with the hypotheses stated above. Their presentation here does not require adding duplicate theorems to the Stacks source. The comparison also records exactly where an ordinary-domain interpretation would be stronger than the relative statement, and why the rational-function case nevertheless has equality of the relevant domains.

The next source unit is EGA I, §7.2.8. It is outside this dossier. No completeness claim about later sections, volumes, publication, or machine-checked proofs follows from these three results.
