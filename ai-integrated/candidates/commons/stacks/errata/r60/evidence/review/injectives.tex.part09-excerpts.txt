1651: For $\alpha = 0$ we set $K_0^\bullet = 0$. For $\alpha > 0$
1652: we proceed as follows:
1653: \begin{enumerate}
1654: \item If $\alpha = \beta + 1$ and $K_\beta^\bullet = M^\bullet$
1655: then we choose $K_\alpha^\bullet = K_\beta^\bullet$.
1656: \item If $\alpha = \beta + 1$ and $K_\beta^\bullet \not = M^\bullet$
1657: then $M^\bullet/K_\beta^\bullet$ is a nonzero acyclic complex.
1658: We choose a subcomplex $N_\alpha^\bullet \subset M^\bullet/K_\beta^\bullet$
1659: as in Lemma \ref{lemma-acyclic-quotient-complexes-bounded-size}.
1660: Finally, we let $K_\alpha^\bullet \subset M^\bullet$
1661: be the inverse image of $N_\alpha^\bullet$.
1662: \item If $\alpha$ is a limit ordinal we set
1663: $K_\alpha^\bullet = \colim_{\beta < \alpha} K_\beta^\bullet$.
1664: \end{enumerate}
1665: It is clear that $M^\bullet = K_\alpha^\bullet$ for a suitably large
1666: ordinal $\alpha$. We will prove that
1667: $$
1668: \Hom_{K(\mathcal{A})}(K_\alpha^\bullet, I^\bullet)
1669: $$
1670: is zero by transfinite induction on $\alpha$. It holds for $\alpha = 0$
1671: since $K_0^\bullet$ is zero. Suppose it holds for $\beta$ and
1672: $\alpha = \beta + 1$. In case (1) of the list above the result is clear.
1673: In case (2) there is a short exact sequence of complexes
1674: $$
1675: 0 \to K_\beta^\bullet \to K_\alpha^\bullet \to N_\alpha^\bullet \to 0
1676: $$
1677: Since each component of $I^\bullet$ is injective we see that we obtain
1678: an exact sequence
1679: $$
1680: \Hom_{K(\mathcal{A})}(K_\beta^\bullet, I^\bullet) \leftarrow
1681: \Hom_{K(\mathcal{A})}(K_\alpha^\bullet, I^\bullet) \leftarrow
1682: \Hom_{K(\mathcal{A})}(N_\alpha^\bullet, I^\bullet)
1683: $$
1684: By induction the term on the left is zero and by assumption on $I^\bullet$
1685: the term on the right is zero. Thus the middle group is zero too.
1686: Finally, suppose that $\alpha$ is a limit ordinal. Then we see that
1687: $$
1688: \Hom^\bullet(K_\alpha^\bullet, I^\bullet) =
1689: \lim_{\beta < \alpha} \Hom^\bullet(K_\beta^\bullet, I^\bullet)
1690: $$
1691: with notation as in
1692: More on Algebra, Section \ref{more-algebra-section-hom-complexes}.
1693: These complexes compute morphisms in $K(\mathcal{A})$ by
1694: More on Algebra, Equation
1695: (\ref{more-algebra-equation-cohomology-hom-complex}).
1696: Note that the transition maps in the system are surjective
1697: because $I^j$ is injective for each $j$. Moreover, for a limit
1698: ordinal $\alpha$ we have equality of limit and value
1699: (see displayed formula above). Thus we may apply
1700: Homology, Lemma \ref{homology-lemma-ML-over-ordinals}
1701: to conclude.
1702: \end{proof}
1703: 
1704: \begin{lemma}
1705: \label{lemma-functorial-homotopies}
1706: Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a Grothendieck abelian category.
1707: Let $(K_i^\bullet)_{i \in I}$ be a set of acyclic complexes.
1708: There exists a functor $M^\bullet \mapsto \mathbf{M}^\bullet(M^\bullet)$
1709: and a natural transformation
1710: $j_{M^\bullet} : M^\bullet \to \mathbf{M}^\bullet(M^\bullet)$
1711: such that
1712: \begin{enumerate}
1713: \item $j_{M^\bullet}$ is a (termwise) injective quasi-isomorphism, and
1714: \item for every $i \in I$ and $w : K_i^\bullet \to M^\bullet$
1715: the morphism $j_{M^\bullet} \circ w$ is homotopic to zero.
