6550: \label{lemma-ss-converges-trivial}
6551: Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category. Let $(K^\bullet, F)$ be a
6552: filtered complex of $\mathcal{A}$. Assume

6555: $H^n(F^pK^\bullet) = 0$ for $p \geq p_0(n)$,
6556: \item for every $n$ there exists $p_1(n)$ such that
6557: $H^n(F^pK^\bullet) \to H^n(K^\bullet)$ is an isomorphism
6558: for $p \leq p_1(n)$.
6559: \end{enumerate}
6560: Then
6561: \begin{enumerate}
6562: \item the spectral sequence associated to $(K^\bullet, F)$ is bounded,
6563: \item the filtration on each $H^n(K^\bullet)$ is finite,
6564: \item the spectral sequence associated to $(K^\bullet, F)$ converges
6565: to $H^*(K^\bullet)$.
6566: \end{enumerate}

6569: \begin{proof}
6570: Fix $n$. Using the long exact cohomology sequence associated to
6571: the short exact sequence of complexes
6572: $$
6573: 0 \to F^{p + 1}K^\bullet \to F^pK^\bullet \to \text{gr}^pK^\bullet \to 0
6574: $$
6575: we find that $E_1^{p, n - p} = 0$ for $p \geq \max(p_0(n), p_0(n + 1))$ and
6576: $p < \min(p_1(n), p_1(n + 1))$. Hence the spectral sequence is bounded
6577: (Definition \ref{definition-bounded-ss}). This proves (1).
6578: 
6579: \medskip\noindent
6580: It is clear from the assumptions and
6581: Definition \ref{definition-filtration-cohomology-filtered-complex}
6582: that the filtration on $H^n(K^\bullet)$ is finite. This proves (2).
6583:

6585: Next we prove that the spectral sequence weakly converges to
6586: $H^*(K^\bullet)$ using
6587: Lemma \ref{lemma-filtered-complex-ss-converges}.
6588: Let us show that we have equality in (\ref{equation-on-top-bigraded}).
6589: Namely, for $p + r > p_0(n + 1)$ the map
6590: $$
6591: d : F^pK^{n} \cap d^{-1}(F^{p + r}K^{n + 1}) \to F^{p + r}K^{n + 1}
6592: $$
6593: ends up in the image of $d : F^{p + r}K^n \to F^{p + r}K^{n + 1}$
6594: because the complex $F^{p + r}K^\bullet$ is exact in degree $n + 1$.
6595: We conclude that $F^pK^{n} \cap d^{-1}(F^{p + r}K^{n + 1}) =
6596: d(F^{p + r}K^n) + \Ker(d) \cap F^pK^n$. Hence for such $r$ we have
6597: $$

6600: $$

6602: (\ref{equation-at-bottom-bigraded}) we use that for $p - r + 1 < p_1(n)$

6604: $$
