2272: \begin{lemma}
2273: \label{lemma-good-quotient}
2274: Let $(A, \text{d})$ be a differential graded algebra.
2275: Let $M$ be a differential graded $A$-module. There exists a homomorphism
2276: $P \to M$ of differential graded $A$-modules with the following
2277: properties
2278: \begin{enumerate}
2279: \item $P \to M$ is surjective,
2280: \item $\Ker(\text{d}_P) \to \Ker(\text{d}_M)$ is surjective, and
2281: \item $P$ sits in an admissible short exact sequence
2282: $0 \to P' \to P \to P'' \to 0$ where $P'$, $P''$ are direct sums
2283: of shifts of $A$.
2284: \end{enumerate}
2285: \end{lemma}
2286: 
2287: \begin{proof}
2288: Let $P_k$ be the free $A$-module with generators $x, y$ in degrees
2289: $k$ and $k + 1$. Define the structure of a differential graded
2290: $A$-module on $P_k$ by setting $\text{d}(x) = y$ and $\text{d}(y) = 0$.
2291: For every element $m \in M^k$ there is a homomorphism
2292: $P_k \to M$ sending $x$ to $m$ and $y$ to $\text{d}(m)$.
2293: Thus we see that there is a surjection from a direct sum
2294: of copies of $P_k$ to $M$. This clearly produces $P \to M$
2295: having properties (1) and (3). To obtain property (2) note
2296: that if $m \in \Ker(\text{d}_M)$ has degree $k$, then there is a map
2297: $A[k] \to M$ mapping $1$ to $m$. Hence we can achieve (2) by adding
2298: a direct sum of copies of shifts of $A$.
2299: \end{proof}
