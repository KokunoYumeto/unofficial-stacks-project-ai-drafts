4583: \begin{lemma}
4584: \label{lemma-left-localization-limits}
4585: Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and let $S$ be a left multiplicative
4586: system of morphisms of $\mathcal{C}$. The localization functor
4587: $Q : \mathcal{C} \to S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$ commutes with finite colimits.

4590: \begin{proof}
4591: Let $\mathcal{I}$ be a finite category and let
4592: $\mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{C}$, $i \mapsto X_i$
4593: be a functor whose colimit exists. Then using
4594: (\ref{equation-left-localization-morphisms-colimit}),
4595: the fact that $Y/S$ is filtered, and
4596: Lemma \ref{lemma-directed-commutes} we have
4597: \begin{align*}
4598: \Mor_{S^{-1}\mathcal{C}}(Q(\colim X_i), Q(Y))
4599: & =
4600: \colim_{(s : Y \to Y') \in Y/S} \Mor_\mathcal{C}(\colim X_i, Y') \\
4601: & =
4602: \colim_{(s : Y \to Y') \in Y/S} \lim_i \Mor_\mathcal{C}(X_i, Y') \\
4603: & =
4604: \lim_i \colim_{(s : Y \to Y') \in Y/S} \Mor_\mathcal{C}(X_i, Y') \\
4605: & =
4606: \lim_i \Mor_{S^{-1}\mathcal{C}}(Q(X_i), Q(Y))
4607: \end{align*}
4608: and this isomorphism commutes with the projections
4609: from both sides to the set
4610: $\Mor_{S^{-1}\mathcal{C}}(Q(X_j), Q(Y))$ for each
4611: $j \in \Ob(\mathcal{I})$. Thus, $Q(\colim X_i)$ satisfies
4612: the universal property for the colimit of the functor
4613: $i \mapsto Q(X_i)$; hence, it is this colimit, as desired.

4931: \begin{definition}
4932: \label{definition-saturated-multiplicative-system}
4933: Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and let $S$ be a multiplicative system.
4934: We say $S$ is {\it saturated} if, in addition to MS1, MS2, MS3, we
4935: also have
4936: \begin{enumerate}
4937: \item[MS4] Given three composable morphisms $f, g, h$, if
4938: $fg, gh \in S$, then $g \in S$.
4939: \end{enumerate}
4940: \end{definition}
4941: 
4942: \noindent
4943: Note that a saturated multiplicative system contains all isomorphisms.
4944: Moreover, if $f, g, h$ are composable morphisms in a category and
4945: $fg, gh$ are isomorphisms, then $g$ is an isomorphism (because then $g$
4946: has both a left and a right inverse, hence is invertible).

4948: \begin{lemma}
4949: \label{lemma-what-gets-inverted}
4950: Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and let $S$ be a multiplicative system.
4951: Denote $Q : \mathcal{C} \to S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$ the localization functor.
4952: The set
4953: $$
4954: \hat S = \{f \in \text{Arrows}(\mathcal{C}) \mid
4955: Q(f) \text{ is an isomorphism}\}
4956: $$
4957: is equal to
4958: $$
4959: S' = \{f \in \text{Arrows}(\mathcal{C}) \mid
4960: \text{there exist }g, h\text{ such that }gf, fh \in S\}
4961: $$
4962: and is the smallest saturated multiplicative system containing $S$.
4963: In particular, if $S$ is saturated, then $\hat S = S$.
4964: \end{lemma}
4965: 
4966: \begin{proof}
4967: It is clear that $S \subset S' \subset \hat S$ because elements of
4968: $S'$ map to morphisms in $S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$ which have both left
4969: and right inverses. Note that $S'$ satisfies MS4, and that
4970: $\hat S$ satisfies MS1. Next, we prove that $S' = \hat S$.
4971: 
4972: \medskip\noindent
4973: Let $f \in \hat S$. Let $s^{-1}g = ht^{-1}$ be the inverse morphism
4974: in $S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$. (We may use both left fractions and right
4975: fractions to describe morphisms in $S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$, see
4976: Lemma \ref{lemma-multiplicative-system}.)
4977: The relation $\text{id}_X = s^{-1}gf$ in $S^{-1}\mathcal{C}$ means
4978: there exists a commutative diagram
4979: $$
4980: \xymatrix{
4981:  & X' \ar[d]^u & \\
4982: X \ar[ru]^{gf} \ar[r]^{f'} \ar[rd]_{\text{id}_X} &
4983: X'' &
4984: X \ar[lu]_s \ar[l]_{s'} \ar[ld]^{\text{id}_X} \\
4985: & X \ar[u]_v &
4986: }
4987: $$
4988: for some morphisms $f', u, v$ and $s' \in S$. Hence $ugf = s' \in S$.
4989: Similarly, using that $\text{id}_Y = fht^{-1}$ one proves that
4990: $fhw \in S$ for some $w$. We conclude that $f \in S'$. Thus
4991: $S' = \hat S$. Provided we prove that $S' = \hat S$ is a
4992: multiplicative system it is now clear that this implies that $S' = \hat S$
4993: is the smallest saturated system containing $S$.
4994: 
4995: \medskip\noindent
4996: Our remarks above take care of MS1 and MS4, so to finish the proof of the
4997: lemma we have to show that LMS2, RMS2, LMS3, RMS3 hold for $\hat S$.
4998: Let us check that LMS2 holds for $\hat S$. Suppose we have a solid diagram
4999: $$
5000: \xymatrix{
5001: X \ar[d]_t \ar[r]_g & Y \ar@{..>}[d]^s \\
5002: Z \ar@{..>}[r]^f & W
5003: }
5004: $$
5005: with $t \in \hat S$. Pick a morphism $a : Z \to Z'$ such that
5006: $at \in S$. Then we can use LMS2 for $S$ to find a commutative diagram
5007: $$
5008: \xymatrix{
5009: X \ar[d]_t \ar[r]_g & Y \ar[dd]^s \\
5010: Z \ar[d]_a \\
5011: Z' \ar[r]^{f'} & W
5012: }
5013: $$
5014: and setting $f = f' \circ a$ we win. The proof of RMS2 is dual to this.
5015: Finally, suppose given a pair of morphisms $f, g : X \to Y$ and
5016: $t \in \hat S$ with target $X$ such that $ft = gt$.
5017: Then we pick a morphism $b$ such that $tb \in S$. Then
5018: $ftb = gtb$ which implies by LMS3 for $S$ that there exists an $s \in S$
5019: with source $Y$ such that $sf = sg$ as desired. The proof of
5020: RMS3 is dual to this.
5021: \end{proof}
