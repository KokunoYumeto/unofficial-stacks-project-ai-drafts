218: A category with products and equalizers has limits, see
219: Categories, Lemma \ref{categories-lemma-limits-products-equalizers}.
220: \end{proof}
221: 
222: \noindent
223: Thus, if $(A, \text{d})$ is a differential graded
224: algebra over $R$, then there is an exact functor
225: $$
226: \text{Mod}_{(A, \text{d})} \longrightarrow \text{Comp}(R)
227: $$
228: of abelian categories. For a differential graded module $M$ the
229: cohomology groups $H^n(M)$ are defined as the cohomology of the
230: corresponding complex of $R$-modules. Therefore, a short exact
231: sequence $0 \to K \to L \to M \to 0$ of differential graded modules
232: gives rise to a long exact sequence
233: \begin{equation}
234: \label{equation-les}
235: H^n(K) \to H^n(L) \to H^n(M) \to H^{n + 1}(K)
236: \end{equation}
237: of cohomology modules, see
238: Homology, Lemma \ref{homology-lemma-long-exact-sequence-cochain}.
239: 
240: \medskip\noindent
241: Moreover, from now on we borrow all the terminology used for
242: complexes of modules. For example, we say that a differential
243: graded $A$-module $M$ is {\it acyclic} if $H^k(M) = 0$ for
244: all $k \in \mathbf{Z}$. We say that a homomorphism $M \to N$
245: of differential graded $A$-modules is a {\it quasi-isomorphism}
246: if it induces isomorphisms $H^k(M) \to H^k(N)$ for all $k \in \mathbf{Z}$.
