1316: \medskip\noindent
1317: Moreover, from now on we borrow all the terminology used for
1318: complexes of modules. For example, we say that a differential
1319: graded $\mathcal{A}$-module $\mathcal{M}$ is {\it acyclic} if
1320: $H^k(\mathcal{M}) = 0$ for all $k \in \mathbf{Z}$. We say that a
1321: homomorphism $\mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}$
1322: of differential graded $\mathcal{A}$-modules is a {\it quasi-isomorphism}
1323: if it induces isomorphisms $H^k(\mathcal{M}) \to H^k(\mathcal{N})$
1324: for all $k \in \mathbf{Z}$. And so on and so forth.
