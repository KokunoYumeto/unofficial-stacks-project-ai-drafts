## 1. The defining triangle and actual comparison maps

For a sequence K_1→K_2→… with maps f_n put S=⊕_n K_n and let
ι_n:K_n→S be its coprojections. Define u:S→S by the exact formulas

 u ι_n=ι_n-ι_{n+1}f_n.

Each column has two components, so the universal property of the
coproduct defines u even if the individual K_n are not compact.
A homotopy colimit is a choice of distinguished triangle

 S --u--> S --v--> C --w--> S[1].                       (1.1)

Its structural maps are i_n=vι_n, and v u=0 gives
i_{n+1}f_n=i_n. TR1 gives existence as soon as S exists.
Two choices with this same u admit a map of triangles whose first two
maps are identities, by TR3. Applying representable Hom gives maps of
long exact sequences; the five-lemma argument shows that the third map
induces bijections on every representable Hom, so is an isomorphism.
This is uniqueness up to a possibly nonunique isomorphism compatible
with both v and w, as stated in the source.

For a morphism of sequences a_n:K_n→L_n satisfying
a_{n+1}f_n=g_n a_n, let A=⊕a_n:S_K→S_L. The two maps in the
square are both A, and the column equation gives A u_K=u_L A.
TR3 supplies a map a:C_K→C_L with

 a v_K=v_L A,        w_L a=A[1]w_K.

After precomposition with ι_n this says a i_n=j_n a_n.
The source's printed a i_n=j_n has different domains on its two sides.
Existing unit MC-STK-ERR-0881 supplies precisely the missing a_n;
no new operation is needed for that reported defect.

For completeness, differences of two such completions ε:C_K→C_L
satisfy ε v_K=0 and w_L ε=0. Exactness of Hom applied to (1.1)
gives ε=t w_K for some t:S_K[1]→C_L. If η:C_L→C_M is another
such difference, write η=s w_L. Then ηε=s w_L ε=0.
Thus these differences form the square-zero ideal already proved as
DERIVED-UNDER-001. This is a receiving application of that result,
not a new underclaim. It does not assert a canonical choice of a.

## 2. Maps out of a telescope and their ambiguity

Apply Hom(-,L) to (1.1). The relevant exact segment is

 Hom(S[1],L) --u[1]^*--> Hom(S[1],L) --w^*-->
 Hom(C,L) --v^*--> Hom(S,L) --u^*--> Hom(S,L).

The coproduct property gives Hom(S,L)=∏_n Hom(K_n,L).
Under this exact identification,

 u^*((x_n))=(x_n-x_{n+1}f_n).

The same formula on shifted objects identifies u[1]^* with the
difference map for the tower Hom(K_n,L[-1]), using the exact shift
adjunction Hom(K_n[1],L)=Hom(K_n,L[-1]). If Δ denotes that product
difference map, the exact segment becomes

 0→coker Δ_{Hom(K_n,L[-1])}→Hom(C,L)→lim Hom(K_n,L)→0.  (2.1)

The left injection sends a family z_n:K_n[1]→L to z w, where
z:S[1]→L has those components. A change by an element of im Δ
does not change z w because u[1]w=0.
The right map sends b:C→L to (b i_n).
Thus every compatible family lifts, and its lifts form a torsor
under the displayed cokernel; no functorial choice of a lift follows.
The source's R^1 lim notation denotes this cokernel for abelian-group
towers. This proves the exact maps in the displayed source sequence
and the receiving More on Algebra lemma without a new count.

## 3. Passing to a subsequence: all four maps

Let n_1<n_2<… be the given subsequence. Write

 t_{q,p}=f_{q-1}⋯f_p:K_p→K_q for q>p,   t_{p,p}=id,
 T=⊕_i K_{n_i},          g_i=t_{n_{i+1},n_i},
 u_T κ_i=κ_i-κ_{i+1}g_i,

where κ_i:K_{n_i}→T. Retain S,u from Section 1.
The two telescope triangles and forward comparison have this form:

 T --u_T--> T --v_T--> C_T --w_T--> T[1]
 | b        | a        | φ          | b[1]
 v          v          v            v
 S ---u---> S ---v---> C ---w-----> S[1].

The reverse comparison has first map d, second map c and third map ψ.
Define all components, including the initial segment before n_1, by

 a κ_i=ι_{n_i},
 b κ_i=Σ_{n_i≤q<n_{i+1}}ι_q t_{q,n_i},
 c ι_j=κ_i t_{n_i,j}, where i is the least index with j≤n_i,
 d ι_j=κ_i if j=n_i, and zero otherwise.                (3.1)

Each expression defines a map from a coproduct by finitely many
components on each source summand. The least index in c exists since
the strictly increasing natural-number subsequence is unbounded.
At j=n_i the transition composite in c is the identity.

Telescoping each finite sum proves

 u b κ_i=ι_{n_i}-ι_{n_{i+1}}t_{n_{i+1},n_i}
         =a u_T κ_i.

For the reverse square, if j is not a selected index, j and j+1
have the same least selected upper endpoint and c u ι_j=0=u_T dι_j.
If j=n_i, then
c u ι_j=κ_i-κ_{i+1}g_i=u_T dι_j.
Hence u b=a u_T and c u=u_T d, exactly the two required squares.
TR3 gives φ and ψ with all triangle compatibilities.

The identities c a=id_T and d b=id_T hold on every κ_i:
the finite interval [n_i,n_{i+1}) contains exactly one selected index,
namely n_i. These are the first two maps of the composite of triangles
from the T triangle to itself. Consequently ψφ is an isomorphism.
The printed φψ at original 10278 reverses these domains.

For the other composite, define h:S→S on each summand as follows.
Let i be the least index with j≤n_i. Set

 h ι_j=Σ_{j≤q<n_i}ι_q t_{q,j}.                          (3.2)

The sum is empty, hence zero, when j=n_i.
This includes every positive j before n_1.
The last summand is K_{n_i-1}, with transition
f_{n_i-2}⋯f_j; there is no K_{n_i} summand.

The first exact identity is obtained by telescoping:

 u h ι_j=ι_j-ι_{n_i}t_{n_i,j}=(id_S-a c)ι_j.

For the second, when j<n_i the difference of (3.2) at j and j+1
leaves exactly ι_j, so h u ι_j=ι_j=(id_S-b d)ι_j.
When j=n_i, the first sum is zero and the second has upper endpoint
n_{i+1}; therefore

 h u ι_{n_i}
 =-Σ_{n_i<q<n_{i+1}}ι_q t_{q,n_i}
 =ι_{n_i}-bκ_i
 =(id_S-b d)ι_{n_i}.

Thus the two source homotopy equations hold exactly:

 u h=id_S-a c,               h u=id_S-b d.              (3.3)

The printed h_j includes its upper endpoint. This is a real defect.
For example, work in D(Ab), let every K_j=Z[0] and every f_j=id,
and choose n_i=2i. At j=3 the printed h has column ι_3+ι_4.
Its image under u is ι_3-ι_5, whereas (id_S-a c)ι_3=ι_3-ι_4.
These maps of degree-zero free abelian groups are different.
The corrected formula gives hι_3=ι_3 and satisfies (3.3).

Now θ=φψ is the endomorphism on C. Its triangle's first two
components are b d and a c. Let e=id_C-θ. Then

 e v=v(id_S-a c)=v u h=0,
 w e=(id_S-b d)[1]w=h[1]u[1]w=0.

Exactness gives e=t w for some t:S[1]→C; hence e²=t w e=0.
It follows that θ=id_C-e is invertible, with inverse id_C+e.
This supplies the source's omitted small argument and shows why the
printed ψφ in original 10292 and 10294 must instead be φψ.
DERIVED-NEW-035 records these three reversed composite expressions.
DERIVED-NEW-036 records the wrong homotopy endpoint, with its zero
case and initial-segment indexing made explicit in the corrected formula.

Finally both ψφ and φψ are invertible. The two candidates
(ψφ)^(-1)ψ and ψ(φψ)^(-1) for an inverse to φ coincide: multiply
(ψφ)ψ=ψ(φψ) on the left and right by the respective inverses.
They are respectively a left and a right inverse, so φ is an isomorphism.
Its component equation φ v_T=v a gives
φ v_T κ_i=vι_{n_i}, which is precisely the source's required diagram.
No compactness, enhancement, infinite sum in an individual column,
or uniqueness of a TR3 completion was assumed.

## 4. Direct sums in the derived category, including uniqueness

Assume A has exact countable direct sums. For complexes K_i let
S be their termwise direct sum. Exactness gives
H^r(S)=⊕_i H^r(K_i): apply direct sums to the short exact sequences
defining cycles, boundaries and cohomology. In particular a direct
sum of quasi-isomorphisms is a quasi-isomorphism.

For maps α_i:K_i→L in D(A), choose roofs
K_i←s_i M_i→f_i L with s_i a quasi-isomorphism.
Their coproduct s:⊕M_i→S is a quasi-isomorphism, and the maps f_i
define f:⊕M_i→L. The roof f s^(-1) restricts to every α_i,
which proves existence of the required coproduct map.

Here is the uniqueness omitted in the source. Suppose γ:S→L
restricts to zero on every K_i. Represent it by f s^(-1) with
s:M→S a quasi-isomorphism. For each coprojection K_i→S,
the fraction calculus supplies a quasi-isomorphism t_i:N_i→K_i
and u_i:N_i→M with s u_i=ι_i t_i in K(A).
The zero restriction implies f u_i=0 in D(A).
The zero criterion for localization supplies a further
quasi-isomorphism q_i:N'_i→N_i with f u_i q_i=0 in K(A).
Replace t_i,u_i by their composites with q_i.

Let U:⊕N'_i→M have components u_i and let T=⊕t_i:⊕N'_i→S.
The component homotopies combine to give s U=T in K(A),
because maps and homotopies out of a termwise coproduct are
specified by maps and homotopies on its summands.
T and s are quasi-isomorphisms, so U is one.
Likewise the component nullhomotopies combine to f U=0 in K(A).
Consequently γ=f U(s U)^(-1)=0 in D(A).
This proves the full coproduct property without presuming that
derived Hom out of a coproduct is a product.

The same argument applies to any indexing set for which the
corresponding direct sums exist and are exact; the countable source
statement remains the one under review. This routine index-set
observation is not counted as another editorial result.

## 5. Exact sequential colimits and the telescope sequence

Assume sequential colimits exist and are exact in A.
The colimit of the finite partial sums gives a countable coproduct:
maps from the colimit to an object are precisely compatible maps from
the finite sums, equivalently a family of maps from their summands.
Finite sums are exact, and taking the assumed exact sequential colimit
proves countable sums are exact. The existing correction 0885 changes
only the source's plural verb.

For a sequence A_1→A_2→… set B_n=⊕_{1≤j≤n}A_j and
B'_n=⊕_{1≤j<n}A_j. Define β_n:B'_n→B_n by columns
ι_j-ι_{j+1}f_j and q_n:B_n→A_n by columns t_{n,j}.
Then q_n β_n=0, and q_n is split epic using the last summand.
The sequence 0→B'_n→B_n→A_n→0 is exact.
For a completely explicit verification, β_n is injective by successive
coordinates: its first coordinate is x_1 and its j-th coordinate is
x_j-f_{j-1}x_{j-1}. An element y of ker q_n has unique preimage with
x_1=y_1 and x_j=y_j+f_{j-1}x_{j-1} for 1<j<n.
The last-coordinate equation is precisely q_n(y)=0.
These calculations are matrix identities of finite biproduct maps,
so hold in any abelian category, without an element interpretation.

The transition maps B'_n→B'_{n+1} and B_n→B_{n+1} are inclusions;
the right transition is f_n. The column equations show these form
a morphism of short exact sequences. Their colimit is therefore

 0→⊕_n A_n --(1-f)--> ⊕_n A_n→colim_n A_n→0.           (5.1)

For a sequence of complexes, apply (5.1) in every degree.
The differentials commute with every map, so this is a short exact
sequence of complexes. Its distinguished triangle in D(A), together
with Section 4, identifies the actual termwise colimit and its
canonical stage maps as a homotopy colimit.
The source's exact sequential-colimit assumption is retained.

## 6. Homological functors and compact-source maps

Let H:D→A be homological, commute with countable coproducts, and
let A have exact sequential colimits. Apply H to (1.1).
Shift is an equivalence, hence preserves the existing coproduct S.
The exact segment is

 ⊕H(K_n) --(1-H(f))--> ⊕H(K_n)→H(C)→
 ⊕H(K_n[1]) --(1-H(f[1]))--> ⊕H(K_n[1]).

The last map is monic by (5.1); its first map's cokernel is
colim H(K_n). Thus the induced map colim H(K_n)→H(C) is an
isomorphism. Its components are H(i_n), so the asserted
identification is the actual canonical one.

Take A=Ab and H=Hom_D(P,-). It is homological by the triangle
axioms. If Hom_D(P,-) commutes with countable sums, the preceding
argument proves the source comparison

 λ:colim_n Hom_D(P,K_n)→Hom_D(P,C)

is bijective. It follows explicitly that every map P→C factors
through some i_n and that two stage maps giving the same map to C
become equal at a later common stage. Both assertions are the
element description of a sequential colimit of abelian groups.
No countability assertion about the individual Hom groups is needed.

## 7. The exact comparison defect without compactness

DERIVED-UNDER-024 gives a criterion for this one sequence and object P.
Do not assume P compact or that Hom(P,-) preserves any infinite sum.
Retain the actual triangle (1.1). For r=0,1 define abelian groups

 A_r=⊕_n Hom_D(P,K_n[r]),
 B_r=Hom_D(P,S[r]),
 Q_r=B_r/A_r.

The canonical map A_r→B_r is injective: if a finite sum of stage
maps gives zero in B_r, compose with each coordinate projection
S[r]→K_n[r] to show that every component is zero.
Let d_r:B_r→B_r be postcomposition by u[r].
Its restriction δ_r to A_r has the original finite-support columns
1-(f_n[r])_*; it is injective, by successive components.
It induces an endomorphism e_r:Q_r→Q_r.
These definitions retain all the actual maps, not only their kernels.

The triangle yields a short exact sequence

 0→coker d_0 --v_*--> Hom_D(P,C) --w_*--> ker d_1→0.    (7.1)

The square of exact sequences 0→A_0→B_0→Q_0→0 with vertical
maps δ_0,d_0,e_0 has kernel-cokernel exact sequence

 0→ker d_0→ker e_0 --∂--> coker δ_0
   →coker d_0→coker e_0→0.                             (7.2)

Here ∂ has an exact representative description. If q∈Q_0 is
killed by e_0, choose b∈B_0 lifting q. Then d_0(b) lies in A_0,
and ∂q is its class in coker δ_0. Changing b by an element of A_0
changes this representative by im δ_0. This proves well-definedness,
and the kernel and image assertions follow by lifting and subtracting
representatives in the two rows.

Since coker δ_0=colim Hom_D(P,K_n), its composite into (7.1) is
exactly λ, not a different comparison. Equations (7.1)–(7.2) give

 ker λ ≅ coker(ker d_0→ker e_0),                       (7.3)
 0→coker e_0→coker λ→ker d_1→0.                        (7.4)

In (7.3), the isomorphism is induced by ∂; in (7.4), the first
injection comes from the quotient of coker d_0 by im(coker δ_0),
and the last map is induced by w_*.
Thus λ is bijective exactly when

 ker d_0→ker e_0 is surjective, coker e_0=0, ker d_1=0.  (7.5)

In particular it suffices that e_0 is invertible and d_1 is injective.
It also suffices that just the two comparison maps A_r→B_r for
r=0,1 are surjective: then Q_0=Q_1=0 and d_1=δ_1 is injective.
This is weaker than requiring preservation of every countable sum.
The source's compactness assumption implies these conditions, and
its theorem remains unchanged.

The distinction is substantive even for familiar categories.
For a constant sequence K_n=X and f_n=id, define
ε:S→X by ε ι_n=id and σ:S→S by

 σ ι_n=-Σ_{1≤j<n}ι_j.

Finite telescoping gives σ u=id_S and
u σ=id_S-ι_1 ε, while εu=0 and ει_1=id_X.
The maps (σ,ε):S→S⊕X and (u,ι_1):S⊕X→S are inverse:
the two displayed identities give their products, with
σι_1=0 and εu=0 supplying the off-diagonal zeros.
Therefore u is identified with the split inclusion S→S⊕X,
whose distinguished cone triangle has third object X.
The comparison from (1.1) gives C≅X with every i_n=id_X.
Hence λ is bijective for every P.

This example also directly satisfies the weaker criterion:
σ preserves the finite-support subgroup A_r, and on Q_r its
products with e_r are identities because ι_1 ε factors through
one summand. Thus e_0 is invertible; d_1 is split monic with
left inverse induced by σ[1].
For a concrete noncompact P take ⊕_m Z[0] in D(Ab).
The identity map P→⊕_m Z[0] has infinitely many nonzero target
coordinates and therefore is not in ⊕_m Hom(P,Z[0]).
Nevertheless the constant-sequence comparison above holds for it.
This proves an actual application beyond the global compactness
hypothesis, without claiming a new result in the literature.
