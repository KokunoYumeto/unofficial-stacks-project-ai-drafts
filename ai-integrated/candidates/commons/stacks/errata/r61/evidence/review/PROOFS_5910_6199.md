## 1. Weak Serre subcategories and the exact comparison

Let B be the given weak Serre subcategory of the abelian category A.
For a complex X, write H^n(X) for its cohomology in A. By the definition
and characterization in current Homology 2091–2176, B is strictly full,
closed under kernels and cokernels of its internal morphisms and under
extensions, and its inclusion into A is exact. Finite biproducts belong
to B because they are extensions.

The class D_B(A) of complexes with all H^n in B is therefore additive
and preserved by every integer shift. If X direct-sum Y belongs to it,
put M=H^n(X) direct-sum H^n(Y). The two endomorphisms

\[
 e_X=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 e_Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}:M\longrightarrow M
\]

are morphisms between objects of B. Their kernels are respectively
H^n(Y) and H^n(X), which therefore belong to B. This proves the actual
summand assertion underlying the corrected phrase at original 5948;
it does not assume that B is closed under arbitrary A-subobjects.

For a distinguished triangle X -> Y -> Z -> X[1], the exact five-term
segment is

\[
 H^n(X)\longrightarrow H^n(Y)\longrightarrow H^n(Z)
       \longrightarrow H^{n+1}(X)\longrightarrow H^{n+1}(Y).
\]

If X,Y lie in D_B(A), the defining five-term condition puts H^n(Z)
in B. Rotation gives every other two-out-of-three case. Cohomological
upper or lower bounds are preserved under finite sums, summands,
shifts and triangles, proving all stated bounded versions as well.

Exactness of B -> A gives the equality of the corresponding kernels,
images, quotients and hence cohomology for any B-complex. Applying the
inclusion to the original cone matrix preserves its entries and signs.
It sends quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms and descends to the
exact comparison D(B) -> D_B(A), with the same assertion for +, -, b.
No full faithfulness is inferred at this stage.

## 2. Lifting a complex from the abelian quotient

Let the given C-complex have objects X^i of A and differentials d^i
in C. Let S be the morphisms of A with kernel and cokernel in B.
The roof calculus and its pushout/pullback stability are the ones in
current Homology 2235–2289. We retain each step of the source's
two-sided construction, including the finite sums and intersections.

For i>=0, write d^i=v(f^i)v(s^i)^{-1}, with s^i:Y^i -> X^i in S
and f^i:Y^i -> X^{i+1}. Starting with j^0=1 on X^0, form recursively

\[
 (X')^{i+1}
   =\operatorname{Coker}\big((j^is^i,-f^i):
             Y^i\longrightarrow (X')^i\oplus X^{i+1}\big).
                                                        \tag{2.1}
\]

Let a^i:(X')^i -> (X')^{i+1} and j^{i+1}:X^{i+1} -> (X')^{i+1}
be the two pushout maps. The minus sign in (2.1) gives the exact
identity a^i j^i s^i=j^{i+1} f^i. Since j^i s^i is in S, its pushout
j^{i+1} is in S. Induction proves this at every finite nonnegative
degree. Applying v yields

\[
 v(a^i)v(j^i)=v(j^{i+1})d^i.
\]

Take (X')^n=X^n and j^n=1 for n<=0, and transport the negative
differentials in C. This gives an isomorphism of C-complexes and
represents every nonnegative differential by an actual A-map. No
infinite pushout or limit has been used.

For the resulting negative differentials, write
d^i=v(t^{i+1})^{-1}v(g^i), with t^{i+1}:X^{i+1} -> Z^{i+1} in S
and g^i:X^i -> Z^{i+1}. Starting at j^0=1, form, by descending
induction,

\[
 (X')^i=X^i\mathbin{\times}_{Z^{i+1}}(X')^{i+1},\quad
 j^i:(X')^i\longrightarrow X^i,\quad
 a^i:(X')^i\longrightarrow (X')^{i+1},                    \tag{2.2}
\]

where the second map to Z^{i+1} is t^{i+1}j^{i+1}.
The pullback identity is g^i j^i=t^{i+1}j^{i+1}a^i. Pullback stability
of S makes j^i an S-map, and after v the identity becomes
d^i v(j^i)=v(j^{i+1})v(a^i). Keep the already constructed terms and
maps in degrees >=0. We now have A-maps in every degree, whose
successive composites vanish after v. These maps need not yet form
an A-complex; that is precisely what the next two steps achieve.

I_k=Im(d^{k-1}d^{k-2}) inside X^k. Exactness of v implies v(I_k)=0,
so I_k belongs to B. For n>0 retain the entire source expression

\[
 N^n=\sum_{0<k\le n}\operatorname{Im}
                  (I_k\longrightarrow X^n),\qquad
 \widetilde X^n=X^n/N^n,                                \tag{2.3}
\]

where the indicated map is the restriction of a_{n,k}. Set N^n=0
for n<=0. Each N^n belongs to B, since the sum is finite and B is
Serre. Moreover d^n(N^n) is contained in N^{n+1}, so all differentials
descend. For n>=0 the image of d^n d^{n-1} is contained in I_{n+1},
one of the retained summands in N^{n+1}. Thus these composites are

under v. This proves the first source replacement with its exact
range and without dropping the k=1 contribution from X^{-1}.

For the remaining step use the new objects tilde X and their induced
maps; denote their path composites by tilde a. Set Q^n=tilde X^n for
n>=0, and for n<0 keep the full finite intersection

\[
 Q^n=\bigcap_{n\le k<0}
       (\widetilde a_{k,n})^{-1}
       \ker(\widetilde d^{k+1}\widetilde d^k).             \tag{2.4}
\]

Equivalently, Q^n is the kernel of the map to the finite biproduct
of the images of tilde a_{k+2,n}, n<=k<0. Each such image belongs
to B, since v kills the consecutive two-map factor. Consequently
tilde X^n/Q^n lies in B and Q^n -> tilde X^n becomes an isomorphism
under v. If x denotes a generalized section of Q^n, every condition
at degree n+1 follows by composing the corresponding condition for
x with tilde d^n; categorically, the image of tilde d^n|_{Q^n} lies
in each of the kernels defining Q^{n+1}. Thus Q is preserved by
the differential. For n<0 its k=n condition says exactly that the
two-map composite starting at Q^n is zero. The composites starting
in nonnegative degree already vanished by (2.3). Hence Q is an
A-complex. The maps in (2.1)–(2.4), after v, give actual chain
isomorphisms connecting vQ to the original C-complex. This proves
essential surjectivity with no omitted boundary degree.

## 3. The left adjoint descends after the quotient

Keep the source hypothesis: u:C -> A is left adjoint to the exact
quotient v:A -> C and vu is naturally isomorphic to Id_C. Let
eta:Id_C -> vu and epsilon:uv -> Id_A be the actual unit and counit.
The latter natural isomorphism need not initially have been specified
as eta, so we first justify that eta itself is invertible.

Write T=vu, mu=v epsilon u, and choose the given natural isomorphism
alpha:T -> Id. Define natural endomorphisms of Id_C by

\[
 e=\alpha\eta,\qquad
 m=\alpha\mu\,T(\alpha^{-1})\alpha^{-1}.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

Naturality of eta and the monad unit identity mu eta_T=1 give

\[
 me=\alpha\mu T(\alpha^{-1})\eta
    =\alpha\mu\eta_T\alpha^{-1}=1.
\]

Natural endomorphisms of the identity commute: naturality of e at
the morphism m_X gives e_X m_X=m_X e_X for every X. Thus em=1 as
well. It follows that eta=alpha^{-1}e is invertible. The adjunction
triangle identity now gives the specific formula

\[
 v(\epsilon_X)=\eta_{vX}^{-1}.                            \tag{3.2}
\]

The left adjoint u is additive. One direct verification is that its
left-adjoint property preserves zero objects and finite coproducts;
in an abelian category these are biproducts, and the zero maps,
projections and diagonals are characterized by their biproduct
equations. Hence u preserves addition as defined by diagonal,
biproduct and codiagonal. It therefore acts termwise on complexes
and homotopies and preserves the cone matrix, with its minus sign.

It is not necessary, and is generally false, that u send every
C-quasi-isomorphism to an A-quasi-isomorphism. Let

\[
 T_A=D(A)/D_B(A),\qquad q:D(A)\longrightarrow T_A.
\]

If s is a quasi-isomorphism of C-complexes, then v u C(s) is
isomorphic to C(s) via eta^{-1}. Thus H^n(u C(s)) lies in B for
every n. It follows that q(u C(s))=0 and q(u s) is invertible.
The universal property of localization therefore gives an exact
functor

\[
 G:D(C)\longrightarrow T_A,\qquad G(Y)=q(uY),              \tag{3.3}
\]

where uY means the termwise construction before localization. The
localization proof is what makes (3.3) independent of a chosen
complex representative. Naturality of eta and epsilon on chain
maps extends to inverted arrows by multiplying their naturality
identities by inverses. We obtain natural isomorphisms

\[
 F G\xrightarrow{\eta^{-1}}\operatorname{Id}_{D(C)},\qquad
 G F\xrightarrow{q(\epsilon)}\operatorname{Id}_{T_A},      \tag{3.4}
\]

where F:T_A -> D(C) is induced by v. The second is invertible
because its cone has cohomology in B by (3.2). For arbitrary
X,Y in D(A), the inverse on Hom of F is the explicit map

\[
 a:vX\longrightarrow vY
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 q(\epsilon_Y)\,G(a)\,q(\epsilon_X)^{-1}.                 \tag{3.5}
\]

Applying F to (3.5), formula (3.2) and naturality of eta give a.
Conversely, naturality of q(epsilon) shows that (3.5) takes F(b)
back to b for every b:qX -> qY, including roofs. This proves full
faithfulness and essential surjectivity of F with the original maps.

For each of +,-,b perform the same construction on complexes whose
terms satisfy the indicated bound. Additivity of u preserves their
zero terms. An acyclic cone still maps to a complex with B-cohomology,
so (3.3) descends to D^*(C) -> D^*(A)/D_B^*(A). All identities
(3.2)–(3.5) remain within these bounded categories. One never assumes
that u preserves cohomological bounds on an arbitrary representative.

For a concrete check that the distinction is necessary, fix a prime p.
Let A be the abelian category of left modules over

\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf Z&0\\\mathbf F_p&\mathbf F_p\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Equivalently its objects are triples (M,N,phi), where M is an abelian
group, N an F_p-vector space and phi:M/pM -> N is F_p-linear. A
morphism is a pair (f,g) satisfying g phi=phi' bar f. Kernels and
cokernels are computed on the two components with the induced
structure map, as also follows by applying the two complementary
idempotents of R. The functor v(M,N,phi)=M is exact. Its left adjoint
is u(M)=(M,M/pM,1): a morphism u(M) -> (M',N',phi') is uniquely
determined by f:M -> M', its second component being phi' bar f.
Thus vu=Id, and the counit has components (1,phi).

The Serre kernel B consists of triples (0,N,0). Every counit has
kernel and cokernel in B; in the localization it identifies every
object with u(vX). The same pair-of-components adjunction proves
that the induced A/B -> Ab is an equivalence, so this is a concrete
instance of the source setup. The injection p:Z -> Z is sent by u
to (p,0), which has the nonzero kernel (0,F_p,0). More explicitly,
the acyclic complex Z --p--> Z -> Z/p in degrees 0,1,2 is sent to
a complex whose first component is that exact complex and whose
second is F_p --0--> F_p --1--> F_p. Its degree-zero cohomology is
(0,F_p,0), and all other cohomology is zero. It is not acyclic in A;
its cohomology lies in B and vanishes in the required quotient,
exactly as (3.3) asserts. No contribution has been removed.

## 4. Subcomplex replacements and all derived morphisms

Now assume precisely the hypothesis of original 6143–6150: B is
Serre in A, and every epimorphism X -> Y with Y in B admits a
B-subobject X' of X which still maps onto Y. Let X^bullet be
bounded above with H^i(X) in B, and retain arbitrary prescribed
B-subobjects B^i of X^i.

Choose C^i inside ker(d^i) in B surjecting onto H^i(X). Such a
choice is possible by applying the hypothesis to ker(d^i) -> H^i(X).
Keep the original formula

\[
 D^i=C^i+d^{i-1}(B^{i-1})+B^i.                           \tag{4.1}
\]

Every summand, and their finite sum, lies in B. The image d^i(D^i)
equals d^i(B^i), which is contained in D^{i+1}; hence D is a
subcomplex. The chosen C^i show that H^i(D) -> H^i(X) is epic.

Above the upper term bound set E^i=0. Descending inductively, put

\[
 V^{i+1}=(D^{i+1}+E^{i+1})\cap\operatorname{Im}(d^i).
\]

This object lies in B, because it is a subobject of a B-object.
Apply the hypothesis to the epimorphism
(d^i)^{-1}(V^{i+1}) -> V^{i+1} to obtain E^i inside that preimage,
with E^i in B and d^i(E^i)=V^{i+1}. Set Y^i=D^i+E^i. The formulas
give an actual subcomplex Y of X, containing each prescribed B^i,
with terms in B. Its cohomology remains surjective onto that of X.
For injectivity, the exact subobject identity is

\[
 \ker(d^i|_{Y^i})\cap\operatorname{Im}(d^{i-1}_X)
   =Y^i\cap\operatorname{Im}(d^{i-1}_X)
   =d^{i-1}(E^{i-1})=\operatorname{Im}(d^{i-1}_Y).        \tag{4.2}
\]

The first equality uses d^i d^{i-1}=0, the second is the inductive
choice, and the last follows from E^{i-1} contained in Y^{i-1}
together with the reverse containment for every Y-boundary.
Thus Y -> X is a quasi-isomorphism. This verifies the entire
subcomplex claim without replacing images by unspecified elements.

For faithfulness first take a chain map f:U -> V between bounded
above B-complexes whose derived image in A is zero. The fraction
calculus supplies a quasi-isomorphism s:V -> X into a bounded above
A-complex and a homotopy h^i:U^i -> X^{i-1} satisfying

\[
 (sf)^i=d_X^{i-1}h^i+h^{i+1}d_U^i.                      \tag{4.3}
\]

Take the prescribed subobjects
B^i=Im(h^{i+1})+Im(s^i). They belong to B. The construction gives
a quasi-isomorphism Y -> X through which s and h factor. Write
s':V -> Y for the resulting chain map. Since both s and Y -> X
are quasi-isomorphisms, so is s'. Equation (4.3) holds in Y, by
monicity of Y -> X, and makes s'f null-homotopic there. Hence f
is zero in D^-(B). For an arbitrary derived arrow represented by
f t^{-1}, vanishing after inclusion implies that f vanishes after
inclusion, because t is already a quasi-isomorphism. The preceding
argument applies, proving faithfulness for every derived arrow.

For fullness, represent any A-derived arrow U -> V by a right roof

\[
 U\xrightarrow{f}X\xleftarrow{s}V,\qquad s\text{ a quasi-isomorphism}.
                                                               \tag{4.4}
\]

Take B^i=Im(f^i)+Im(s^i). A replacement Y -> X containing these
images factors the roof into B-complexes. The factor s':V -> Y is
a quasi-isomorphism, and (s')^{-1}f' in D^-(B) maps to the original
arrow. This is the exact fullness argument abbreviated in the source.
Essential surjectivity follows from the construction with B^i=0.

## 5. DERIVED-UNDER-008: simultaneous factorization and consequences

Keep all hypotheses of section 4. For a fixed bounded above X with
B-cohomology, let P_X be its B-term subcomplexes Y for which Y -> X
is a quasi-isomorphism, ordered by inclusion. It is nonempty by
section 4. Given Y_1,...,Y_r, apply that construction with
B^i=sum_j Y_j^i. It yields Y containing every Y_j. Thus P_X is
filtered; as an inclusion poset it has no distinct parallel arrows
requiring equalization. Each inclusion Y -> Y' in P_X is itself
a quasi-isomorphism by two-out-of-three.

More strongly, any finite family of chain maps from bounded above
B-complexes into X factors simultaneously through one Y in P_X.
Any specified finite family of homotopies between those maps is
retained there as well. In degree i prescribe the sum of all map
images from degree i and all homotopy images from degree i+1.
Each is a quotient of a B-object, so this is a B-subobject of X^i.
The construction and the monicity argument in (4.3) prove the claim
with all equations unchanged.

In particular, for a fixed bounded above B-complex U, the canonical
maps induce the exact Hom formula

\[
 \mathop{\rm colim}_{Y\in P_X}
       \operatorname{Hom}_{K^-(B)}(U,Y)
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
       \operatorname{Hom}_{K^-(A)}(U,X).                  \tag{5.1}
\]

Here the colimit is realized by the group on the right: every chain
map factors by the image construction, and if two factorizations
give the same homotopy class in X, include both target subcomplexes
and the images of one witnessing homotopy in a common replacement.
They then agree in its homotopy category. This proves precisely
the equality relation in the filtered colimit. Sums are compatible
by passing to a common replacement and adding the factor maps, so
(5.1) is an isomorphism of abelian groups, not only a bijection.
The same argument proves its universal property for compatible
families of homomorphisms. Thus no preservation of a previously
unconstructed colimit in A is assumed.

The following diagram records the actual simultaneous replacement;
the diagonal inclusions and all maps shown are chain maps, and
the maps from Y_1,Y_2,Y to X are quasi-isomorphisms.

\[
 \begin{array}{ccccc}
 Y_1&\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow&Y&\longleftarrow\joinrel\rhook&Y_2\\[-2pt]
 &&\big\downarrow&&\\[-2pt]
 &&X&&
 \end{array}
\]

Both paths to X are the given subcomplex inclusions. The homotopy
operators, when prescribed,
factor term by term through this same Y with their original degree -1.

The equivalence of section 4 restricts to

\[
 D^b(B)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^b_B(A).                      \tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed, if the A-cohomology of a resulting Y vanishes below a,
exactness of B -> A gives the same vanishing in B. Replace Y,
within B, by its canonical truncation tau_{>=a}Y. Its degree-a
term is coker(d^{a-1}_Y), an object of B, its higher terms are the
original Y^i, and the map Y -> tau_{>=a}Y is a quasi-isomorphism.
The upper term bound is retained. Full faithfulness follows by
restricting the already proved D^- equivalence and the bounded
embeddings. This proves (5.2) without asserting that this canonical
truncation is a subcomplex of X.

For M,N in B, the comparison therefore identifies, for every n,

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(B)}(M[0],N[n])
   \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{D^b(A)}(M[0],N[n]).                 \tag{5.3}
\]

These are the derived-category Ext groups. Compatibility with
composition and shift follows from the original exact inclusion;
in particular their products are preserved. We do not use an
unproved additional identification with a different Ext presentation.

These are editorial consequences of the source construction. The
bounded restriction is also used in the existing Perfect proposition
below; it is not advertised as a result missing from the corpus.
The source body is not rewritten to incorporate (5.1)–(5.3).

## 6. Checked receiving implications

In current Adequate Modules 2013–2031, u is the associated adequate
module functor, v is restriction to the small Zariski site, and the
unit is an isomorphism. Crucially, the source explicitly says u is
not left exact in general. The adjunction and unit are described at
1737–1765; parasitic objects form the Serre kernel, as in 2040–2114.
Applying (3.2) to the actual adjunction shows that the cone of the
termwise counit has parasitic cohomology. Equations (3.3)–(3.5)
therefore give the inverse and both natural isomorphisms for the
equivalence asserted at 2164–2179, including its bounded versions.
The clarification preserves this receiving theorem and does not add
the false requirement that its u be exact. Only this dependency
implication is reviewed here, not every earlier adequate-module lemma.

For current Perfect Complexes 2564–2592 set
A=QCoh(O_X), B=Coh(O_X), with X Noetherian. The kernels, quotients
and extensions required for the Serre property are those in current
Coherent Sheaves 2448–2488: on a Noetherian affine, submodules and
quotients of finite modules are finite, and extensions of finite
modules are finite. The local exact module-to-quasi-coherent-sheaf
correspondence gives the stated sheaf operations.

For an epimorphism G -> F from a quasi-coherent sheaf to a coherent
one, Properties 3009–3033 writes G as a directed union of its
finite-type quasi-coherent submodules. These are coherent on a
Noetherian scheme. Their images form a directed union equal to F.
Choose a finite affine cover of X. On each affine, finitely many
generators of F are contained in one of the image submodules; by
directedness and the finiteness of the cover, one coherent submodule
of G has image all of F. This verifies the epimorphism hypothesis
used in section 4 with the receiving objects unchanged.

Consequently the concrete coherent subcomplex construction in
(4.1)–(4.2), and its simultaneous map/homotopy refinement (5.1),
apply to bounded above quasi-coherent complexes with coherent
cohomology. The bounded restriction (5.2) and groups (5.3) follow
for Coh inside QCoh. Current Perfect 2595–2631 already uses the
bounded restriction in its larger comparison with all O_X-modules.
That further comparison invokes its separate proposition-Noetherian;
we do not claim a new review of that proposition from this bounded
dependency check. No Perfect source change follows from UNDER-008.
