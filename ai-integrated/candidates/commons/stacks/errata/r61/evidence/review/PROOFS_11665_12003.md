## 1. Orthogonals and the two exact Hom criteria

For a full subcategory A of an additive category D, the original right
orthogonal A^⊥ has objects Y for which Hom_D(A,Y)=0 for every A∈A.
The original left orthogonal ^⊥A has objects Y for which
Hom_D(Y,A)=0 for every A∈A. They are full subcategories by definition.

Suppose D is triangulated and A is invariant under all shifts.
In the triangle
\[
 X\xrightarrow fY\xrightarrow gZ\xrightarrow hX[1]
\]
the natural exact Hom sequence contains
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(A[1],Z)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A,X)
 \xrightarrow{f_*}\operatorname{Hom}(A,Y)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A,Z).
\]
If Z∈A^⊥, both outer groups vanish. Conversely, suppose f_* is an
isomorphism for every A∈A. The exact sequence
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(A,X)\xrightarrow{f_*}\operatorname{Hom}(A,Y)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A,Z)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A[-1],X)
 \xrightarrow{f_*}\operatorname{Hom}(A[-1],Y)
\]
has its first and last arrows isomorphisms, hence Hom(A,Z)=0.
This proves 11682–11715. The equality notation in its statement means
the isomorphism induced by f, not an arbitrary abstract group
isomorphism.

For the dual statement fix a shift-invariant B. If X∈^⊥B, the
exact sequence
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(X[1],B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(Z,B)
 \xrightarrow{g^*}\operatorname{Hom}(Y,B)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(X,B)
\]
gives the required isomorphism. Conversely, apply the assumption to
B and B[1] in
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Z,B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(Y,B)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(X,B)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(Z[-1],B)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(Y[-1],B).
\]
The last arrow is the assumed isomorphism for B[1], under the shift
identifications Hom(Z[−1],B)=Hom(Z,B[1]). Thus Hom(X,B)=0. This explicitly
proves the omitted dual at 11717–11735.

Both orthogonals are strictly full because composition with an
isomorphism identifies the relevant Hom groups. They contain zero
and are closed under finite biproducts, by additivity of Hom.
For a retraction Y→Z→Y, the identity composite makes Hom(A,Y)
a retract of Hom(A,Z), and similarly for Hom(Y,A). Thus a retract
of an orthogonal object remains orthogonal. Shifts preserve
orthogonality because Hom(A,Y[j])=Hom(A[−j],Y) and the dual formula
retain the actual shift isomorphisms. The two exact-sequence
criteria show closure under cones; rotation gives two-out-of-three
closure. The full triangulated-subcategory criterion at 860–880
then supplies the inherited triangulated structure. This proves
11737–11758, including saturation in the source definition at
1758–1763.

The direct sums needed in that proof are finite biproducts. Arbitrary
coproduct closure of A^⊥ is not used or inferred. In contrast, ^⊥A
is closed under any existing coproducts by
Hom(⊕Y_i,A)=∏Hom(Y_i,A); A^⊥ is closed under any existing products
by Hom(A,∏Y_i)=∏Hom(A,Y_i). If every testing object A is compact,
A^⊥ is also closed under existing coproducts by the defining compact
comparison. These precise universal-property statements do not
strengthen the source hypotheses silently.

## 2. Existence of approximation triangles is stable under triangles

Let A be the source full triangulated subcategory, not necessarily
strictly full. Suppose X_i has a triangle
\[
 A_i\xrightarrow{a_i}X_i\xrightarrow{b_i}B_i
 \xrightarrow{\delta_i}A_i[1],\qquad A_i\in A,\quad B_i\in A^\perp
\]
for i=1,2, and let f:X_1→X_2 be the first map of the given
distinguished triangle on X_1,X_2,X_3.
By §1 the map
Hom(A_1,A_2)→Hom(A_1,X_2), α↦a_2α,
is an isomorphism. Define α by a_2α=fa_1. This specifies the exact
commutative square used in the source.

Apply the previously proved nine-object proposition, original
1031–1052 and PROOFS_0001_1140.md, to that square. Choose the middle
column to be the specified triangle X_1→X_2→X_3→X_1[1], and complete
the first column on α. The resulting rows are
A_i→X_i→Q_i→A_i[1], and the third column is the distinguished triangle
Q_1→Q_2→Q_3→Q_1[1]. The lower-right square has the anticommuting
sign required by the nine-object proposition; it is not replaced by
a commuting square. All other squares commute.

The first two rows are triangles on the original a_i. The triangle
morphism axiom gives (id_{A_i},id_{X_i},q_i) to the originally chosen
triangle, and the Hom exact sequences show q_i:Q_i→B_i is an
isomorphism. Therefore Q_1,Q_2 belong to the strictly full
orthogonal, and its cone closure gives Q_3∈A^⊥.
The object A_3 is the cone of α. Since A is a full triangulated
subcategory, there is an object A_3'∈A and an isomorphism
t:A_3'→A_3. Transport the third-row triangle along t: its first
map becomes A_3'→^tA_3→X_3, and its last map becomes
Q_3→A_3[1]→^{t[1]^{-1}}A_3'[1]. This is precisely a triangle
witnessing the source property P(X_3), without treating A as replete.

The property is invariant under shifts, using representatives in A
where necessary and the corresponding shift of its triangle with
the distinguished-triangle sign convention. Rotating the given
triangle therefore proves the other two cases of two-out-of-three.
The split triangle X_1→X_1⊕X_2→X_2→^0X_1[1] proves the biproduct
case.

For the dual property, start with triangles A_i→X_i→B_i→A_i[1],
where B_i∈B and A_i∈^⊥B. The isomorphism
Hom(B_1,B_2)→Hom(X_1,B_2), β↦βb_1,
specifies the unique β with βb_1=b_2f. Apply the same nine-object
construction to the square on X_i→B_i; equivalently use the opposite
triangulated category, whose translation is [−1]. Its cone column
lies in ^⊥B by §1, and the B cone is isomorphic to an object of B.
Transport along that isomorphism as above. Rotation and the split
triangle give all remaining cases. This proves 11813–11829 with
the actual dual maps.

## 3. Adjoints, saturation, and double orthogonals

Let i:A→D be a full triangulated inclusion. If it has right adjoint
v, the counit ε_X:i vX→X induces
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_A(A',vX)
   \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon_X\circ i(-)\ }
 \operatorname{Hom}_D(iA',X)
\]
as the adjunction bijection. Completing ε_X to a triangle and applying
§1 puts its cone in A^⊥.

Conversely choose, for every X, a triangle
A_X→^{a_X}X→B_X→A_X[1] as in the source condition (2).
Set vX=A_X. For f:X→Y let vf be the unique α with
a_Yα=fa_X. This uniqueness proves v(id)=id and v(gf)=vg vf.
The bijections Hom_A(A,A_X)→Hom_D(iA,X) are induced by a_X,
and are natural in both A and X by this defining identity.
They therefore give the required right adjunction, with a_X as
counit. The inclusion is fully faithful, so the unit
A→v iA is an isomorphism. Indeed its image under the adjunction
is id_{iA}; the triangle identities and faithfulness give its
two-sided inverse.

If A is strictly full, then A⊂^⊥(A^⊥). For
X∈^⊥(A^⊥), its approximation triangle has b_X=0.
The split-triangle lemma, applied after rotation, gives
A_X≅X⊕B_X[−1]. Since B_X[−1]∈A^⊥, the projection from A_X to
that summand is zero; composing it with the inclusion gives
id_{B_X[−1]}=0. Hence B_X=0 and a_X is an isomorphism, so X∈A.
Thus A=^⊥(A^⊥). Without strict fullness the same proof gives
equality with the strictly full isomorphism closure of A.
Saturation in the source's sense follows: any summand X of an object
isomorphic to an A object is in ^⊥(A^⊥), so is isomorphic to an
object of A. The other summand is treated by the same argument.
No splitting of arbitrary idempotents in D was assumed.

For j:B→D and a left adjoint w the unit η_X:X→j wX induces
Hom_B(wX,B)→Hom_D(X,jB), β↦j(β)η_X. Completing η_X backwards
gives a triangle A_X→X→j wX→A_X[1], and §1 places A_X in ^⊥B.
Conversely, such triangles define wX=B_X and wf as the unique β
with βb_X=b_Yf. Uniqueness proves the functor laws and the adjunction
naturality. If X∈(^⊥B)^⊥, then a_X=0; the triangle splits as
B_X≅X⊕A_X[1]. The inclusion of this last summand vanishes because
Hom(A_X[1],B_X)=0, so A_X=0. Strict fullness gives
B=(^⊥B)^⊥, and without it the equality holds after isomorphism
closure. This also proves saturation and the entire omitted dual
at 11900–11924.

Both adjoints are exact by the full exact-adjoint proof in
PROOFS_1844_2401.md §3. Its shift isomorphisms are induced by the
actual Hom adjunctions; no assertion of literal equality of chosen
objects replaces them.

## 4. The missing hypotheses in the summary proposition

DERIVED-NEW-042 concerns 11954–11963. As printed, A and B are merely
subcategories. Condition (3), the degree-zero orthogonality and
decomposition triangle for every object, does not force them to be
admissible triangulated subcategories.

An explicit counterexample has D=D^b(k) for a field k,
A=D^{≤0}(k), and B=D^{≥1}(k), both strictly full.
Hom_D(A,B)=0: use the natural truncation quasi-isomorphisms to a
bounded complex supported in degrees ≤0 for A and one supported in
degrees ≥1 for B. A bounded complex of vector spaces is a complex
of projectives that computes these derived Hom groups; its ordinary
chain maps to the second complex are zero by the disjoint degree
supports. Transporting through the stated quasi-isomorphisms gives
the asserted Hom vanishing for the original A and B.

For the decomposition of an arbitrary bounded complex X, use the
actual subcomplex A_X with terms X^n for n<0, ker(d_X^0) for n=0,
and zero for n>0. Its inclusion a_X:A_X→X has quotient complex
B_X=X/A_X. The quotient has zero terms below zero, term
X^0/ker(d_X^0) in degree zero, and terms X^n in degrees n>0.
The degree-zero differential of B_X is injective, so H^n(B_X)=0
for n≤0, while H^n(A_X)=0 for n>0. The actual short exact sequence
of these complexes gives a distinguished triangle A_X→X→B_X→A_X[1].
Thus printed condition (3) holds.

But k∈A while k[−1]∉A, and k[−1]∈B while k[−1][1]=k∉B.
Neither subcategory is invariant under all shifts, so neither is
triangulated and (1),(2) both fail. The failure is mathematical,
not just an omitted word in an otherwise valid proof.

Strict fullness is also not implied by printed (3), which tests
ambient Hom groups and membership of selected objects, not the
subcategory's complete morphism sets or isomorphism closure.
For example, in a nonzero derived category take the subcategory with
all objects but only identity and zero maps, and let B be its full
zero subcategory. The identity triangle X→X→0 verifies (3), yet
the first subcategory is not full. Hence the conclusion cannot
silently restore fullness.

The corrected premise is: A and B are **strictly full subcategories
invariant under all shifts**. It is unnecessary to assume their
cone closure separately. Here is the complete proof of the missing
implication under precisely this premise.

Assume (3). We already have B⊂A^⊥ and A⊂^⊥B.
If X∈A^⊥, the first map A_X→X of its decomposition triangle
vanishes. The split triangle gives B_X≅X⊕A_X[1].
By shift invariance and (3), Hom(A_X[1],B_X)=0; composing the
inclusion of A_X[1] with its projection forces its identity to
vanish. Thus A_X=0 and X≅B_X∈B. Since B is strictly full,
X∈B, proving B=A^⊥.

If X∈^⊥B, its map X→B_X vanishes, hence
A_X≅X⊕B_X[−1]. Shift invariance gives
Hom(A_X,B_X[−1])=0, so the projection to B_X[−1] is zero and
B_X=0. Strict fullness gives X∈A. Thus A=^⊥B.
Section 1 now shows that both categories are saturated and
triangulated. Section 3 applied to the original decomposition
triangles proves both (1) and (2). Conversely (1) supplies (3)
by the right-adjoint criterion, and (2) supplies it by the
left-adjoint criterion.

The source patch adds exactly the strict-fullness and shift
hypotheses and supplies this implication. It does not assume a
missing conclusion in place of proving it.

## 5. Functorial triangles and the exact morphism obstruction

DERIVED-UNDER-031 strengthens the statement at 11938–11950.
For an admissible pair let
\[
 A_X\xrightarrow{a_X}X\xrightarrow{b_X}B_X
 \xrightarrow{\delta_X}A_X[1]
\]
be the selected triangles, with A_X∈A, B_X∈B=A^⊥.
For any f:X→Y, the adjunction gives a unique
α_f:A_X→A_Y with a_Yα_f=fa_X. There is also a unique
β_f:B_X→B_Y with β_fb_X=b_Yf: the relevant exact sequence is
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(A_X[1],B_Y)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}(B_X,B_Y)\xrightarrow{b_X^*}
 \operatorname{Hom}(X,B_Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(A_X,B_Y),
\]
whose outer terms are zero.
The triangle morphism axiom gives a completion of the first square,
and uniqueness identifies its third map with β_f. Consequently
\[
 \delta_Y\beta_f=\alpha_f[1]\delta_X.
\]
The unique choices respect identities and composition. In
particular, two selected triangles on the same X have a **unique**
isomorphism inducing id_X: the unique maps in both directions
compose to identities by the same uniqueness. These constructions
give a natural triangle of functors i v→Id_D→j w→(i v)[1],
with the actual exact-adjoint shift identifications.
The conclusion is stronger than an unspecified isomorphism between
the third objects of two cones.

There is further exact information about arbitrary maps X→Y.
Using the original a,b,δ, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_{-1}(k,l)&=k\delta_X-\delta_Y[-1]\,l,\\
 \phi(h)&=a_Yh b_X,\\
 \rho(f)&=(\alpha_f,\beta_f),\\
 d_0(\alpha,\beta)&=\alpha[1]\delta_X-\delta_Y\beta.
\end{aligned}
\]
The domains and codomains form the following sequence, exact at
each of its three middle terms:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Hom}(A_X[1],A_Y)\oplus
 \operatorname{Hom}(B_X,B_Y[-1])
 \xrightarrow{d_{-1}}\operatorname{Hom}(B_X,A_Y)
 \xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\\
 \xrightarrow{\rho}
 \operatorname{Hom}(A_X,A_Y)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(B_X,B_Y)
 \xrightarrow{d_0}\operatorname{Hom}(B_X,A_Y[1]).
 \tag{5.1}
\end{gathered}
\]
No surjectivity onto the last group is asserted.

For exactness at the pair of Hom groups, the displayed triangle
morphism identity gives d_0ρ=0. Conversely if
α[1]δ_X=δ_Yβ, rotate each triangle twice, obtaining
B_X→^{δ_X}A_X[1]→^{−a_X[1]}X[1]→^{−b_X[1]}B_X[1]
and its Y counterpart. The first square, with maps β and α[1],
commutes. The triangle morphism axiom gives a map f[1] on the
third objects. Its two squares contain the same minus signs on
both sides, so shifting back gives
fa_X=a_Yα and b_Yf=βb_X. The uniqueness above identifies
ρ(f)=(α,β).

If ρ(f)=0, then fa_X=0. Exactness of Hom(−,Y) gives
g:B_X→Y with f=g b_X. Since b_Yf=0 and
Hom(B_X,B_Y)→Hom(X,B_Y) is injective, b_Yg=0.
The Hom(B_X,−) sequence for Y gives h:B_X→A_Y with g=a_Yh.
Thus f=φ(h). Conversely b_Xa_X=0 and b_Ya_Y=0 show
ρφ=0 by the defining uniqueness of α and β.

Finally φ(h)=0 means a_Yh b_X=0. The Hom(−,Y) sequence gives
r:A_X[1]→Y with a_Yh=rδ_X. Since A_X[1]∈A, its adjunction
bijection writes r uniquely as a_Yk for
k:A_X[1]→A_Y. Hence a_Y(h−kδ_X)=0.
The inverse-rotated triangle
B_Y[−1]→^{−δ_Y[-1]}A_Y→^{a_Y}Y→B_Y
gives l:B_X→B_Y[−1] with
h−kδ_X=−δ_Y[−1]l. Therefore h=d_{−1}(k,l).
Conversely the triangle composites
δ_Xb_X=0 and a_Yδ_Y[−1]=0 give φd_{−1}=0.
This proves every asserted exactness and keeps the rotation sign.

Consequently a compatible pair (α,β), namely d_0(α,β)=0, lifts to
a map X→Y. The set of its lifts is a torsor under the explicitly
defined quotient group
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(B_X,A_Y)/\operatorname{Im}(d_{-1}),
\]
acting by adding a_Yh b_X. Surjectivity of the action is exactness
at Hom(X,Y), and freeness is exactness at Hom(B_X,A_Y).
Thus the connecting maps δ_X,δ_Y determine both the obstruction
and the exact ambiguity; one may not replace the category by a
direct product merely because each object has two projections.
This is an editorial consequence of the original triangles, with
no novelty claim and no change to their objects or maps.

## 6. The quotient equivalences and their natural isomorphisms

Keep i:A→D and its exact right adjoint v, and j:B→D and its
exact left adjoint w. Then ker v=B:
if vX=0 the adjunction makes Hom(A,X)=0 for all A;
if X∈B then Hom_A(A,vX)=0 for all A, so taking A=vX shows
id_{vX}=0. Dually ker w=A.

Let q_B:D→D/B be the actual Verdier quotient. Since v is exact and
vanishes on B, its value on every morphism whose cone lies in B
is an isomorphism, by its triangle exactness. Thus the quotient
universal property defines an exact functor
\(\bar v:D/B\to A\) with \(\bar v q_B\cong v\).
The adjunction unit gives
\(\bar v q_B i\cong v i\cong Id_A\).
The counit ε_X=i vX→X has cone B_X∈B, so
q_B(ε_X):q_B i vX→q_BX is an isomorphism.
These isomorphisms are natural for the original arrows by the
counit identity; they are natural for inverses of quotient
denominators by multiplying that identity by their inverses.
They are therefore natural for every roof in D/B. This supplies
\(q_B i\bar v\cong Id_{D/B}\), the other quasi-inverse comparison.

For q_A:D→D/A, the exact left adjoint gives
\(\bar w:D/A\to B\) with \(\bar w q_A\cong w\).
Its counit gives \(\bar w q_A j\cong wj\cong Id_B\).
The original unit b_X:X→j wX has cone A_X[1]∈A, so its quotient
isomorphism gives \(Id_{D/A}\cong q_A j\bar w\).
Naturality for roofs follows by the same inverse calculation.
Thus both quotient equivalences in 11965–12003, and both descriptions
of the adjoints as quotient composites, are proved with their
actual comparison maps. Object choices give natural isomorphisms,
not an unproved assertion that independently chosen objects are
literally equal.
