## 1. The original tower, its shifts, and the invalid assertion

Let E be its given set of objects. Replacing E by all its integral shifts
preserves both assumptions. Detection is preserved because the old set
is included. To verify the factorization assumption for A[i] with A in
the old set, shift a map A[i]→⊕Y_n by −i, apply the old assumption to
Y_n[−i], and shift the resulting entire diagram by i. Its intermediate
objects are E_n[i] in the enlarged set. The shift equivalences preserve
coproducts by their universal property.

Keep exactly the source functor H:D^op→Ab and elements a_n. Put

\[
 X_1=\bigoplus_{(E,a),\,E\in\mathcal E,\ a\in H(E)}E,\qquad
 a_1=(a)_{(E,a)}\in H(X_1).
\]

At stage n define θ_n(f)=H(f)(a_n). Form the source coproduct K_{n+1}
of all pairs (E,k) with k:E→X_n and θ_n(k)=0, its evaluation

\[
 K_{n+1}\xrightarrow{e_n}X_n\xrightarrow{u_n}X_{n+1}
 \longrightarrow K_{n+1}[1].
\]

Every coordinate of H(e_n)(a_n) is zero. The comparison from H on the

H sequence provides a_{n+1} with H(u_n)(a_{n+1})=a_n. In particular,
θ_{n+1}u_{n*}=θ_n.

\[

## 2. The exact restricted-functor category and its countable coproducts

Let P be the full subcategory of objects isomorphic to coproducts of
objects in E. For Y∈D define hY=Hom_D(−,Y)|_P. Let A be the category
of additive functors P^op→Ab that take all coproducts in P to products,
with natural transformations. These are genuine products of abelian
groups; coproducts in A will not be identified with pointwise sums.

A is locally small. A natural transformation is determined by its
components at the set E: for any decomposition P=⊕E_i, naturality for
the inclusions identifies its component at P with the product of its
components at E_i. Hence the class of natural transformations injects
into the set ∏_{E∈E}Hom_Ab(F(E),G(E)).

Kernels and cokernels of a natural transformation in A, calculated
pointwise, still take coproducts to products. For kernels this follows
coordinate by coordinate. For cokernels the needed identity is

\[
 \operatorname{coker}\left(\prod_i F(P_i)\to\prod_i G(P_i)\right)
   \cong\prod_i\operatorname{coker}(F(P_i)\to G(P_i)).
\]

Indeed the product of the quotient maps is onto by choosing a
representative in every coordinate, and its kernel consists exactly
of coordinatewise images, which are the image of the product map.
The same coordinate argument identifies images and coimages. Thus A
is abelian, its exact sequences are pointwise exact, and evaluation
at any P∈P is exact. In particular, idempotents split in A, with image
and kernel calculated pointwise.

For each Y choose

\[
 P_Y=\bigoplus_{(E,f),\,f:E\to Y}E,\qquad p_Y:P_Y\to Y
\]

by evaluation. Hom(P,p_Y) is onto for every P∈P: lift each component
from E and combine them by the coproduct universal property. Complete
p_Y to a triangle C_Y→P_Y→Y→C_Y[1], choose an evaluation map
q_Y:Q_Y→C_Y with Q_Y∈P in the same way, and compose it with
C_Y→P_Y. The Hom exact sequence gives the pointwise exact presentation

\[
 hQ_Y\longrightarrow hP_Y\longrightarrow hY\longrightarrow0. \tag{2.1}
\]

All three functors are in A. For P_0∈P, hP_0 is representable on P,
so Yoneda identifies Nat(hP_0,F) with F(P_0).

We now prove the precise coproduct comparison used in the repair:

\[
 h\left(\bigoplus_{n\ge1}Y_n\right)
       \ \cong\ \coprod_{n\ge1}^{A}hY_n. \tag{2.2}
\]

First, assumption (2) implies that any countable family of maps
p_n:P_n→Y_n which is onto on Hom(E,−) for every E∈E has a coproduct
with the same property. For α:E→⊕Y_n write
α=(⊕β_n)γ as in the assumption. Lift β_n:E_n→Y_n through p_n, say to
\(\widetilde\beta_n:E_n\to P_n\). Then
(⊕\(\widetilde\beta_n\))γ is a lift of α. For a domain P∈P do this
on each coproduct component.

Apply this to the evaluation maps p_{Y_n} and also to q_{Y_n}.
Coproducts of distinguished triangles are distinguished in D. Here
is the needed argument without imposing an additional exactness
axiom on coproducts. For triangles A_i→B_i→C_i→A_i[1], choose a
distinguished triangle on ⊕A_i→⊕B_i with third object Z. The triangle
morphism axiom applied to the inclusions A_i→⊕A_i and B_i→⊕B_i gives
maps C_i→Z. They combine to c:⊕C_i→Z, and all three squares commute
on the actual inclusions. Applying Hom(−,W), the candidate coproduct
triangle has an exact sequence because it is the product of the
exact sequences for the individual triangles and products of abelian
groups are exact. The comparison with the distinguished triangle
on ⊕A_i→⊕B_i has identities on the adjacent A and B terms. The
five-lemma argument proves Hom(c,W) is an isomorphism for every W.
Yoneda implies c is an isomorphism, proving the assertion. Thus
the coproduct of the triangles C_{Y_n}→P_{Y_n}→Y_n→C_{Y_n}[1],
together with these two surjectivities, gives the exact presentation

\[
 h(\bigoplus Q_{Y_n})\longrightarrow
 h(\bigoplus P_{Y_n})\longrightarrow
 h(\bigoplus Y_n)\longrightarrow0. \tag{2.3}
\]

For an arbitrary F∈A, applying Nat(−,F) to the cokernel presentation
(2.3), then Yoneda and the product property of F, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Nat}(h(\bigoplus Y_n),F)
  &=\ker\left(F(\bigoplus P_{Y_n})\to F(\bigoplus Q_{Y_n})\right)\\
  &=\ker\left(\prod_n F(P_{Y_n})\to\prod_n F(Q_{Y_n})\right)\\
  &=\prod_n\ker\left(F(P_{Y_n})\to F(Q_{Y_n})\right)\\
  &=\prod_n\operatorname{Nat}(hY_n,F).
\end{aligned}
\]

The maps are restriction along the actual inclusions Y_n→⊕Y_n,
since both presentation diagrams commute with the inclusions of
P_{Y_n} and Q_{Y_n}. This proves the coproduct universal property,
including its canonical structure maps. It is not a compactness
assertion: evaluation at E need not preserve these coproducts.

The same proof works under the weaker condition that countable
coproducts preserve all maps which are onto on Hom(E,−) for every
E∈E. Conversely, (2.2) implies this condition. If all h(f_n) are
epimorphisms in A, their coproduct is an epimorphism: two maps agreeing
after it agree after every epimorphism h(f_n), hence on all summands.
By (2.2) this is h(⊕f_n), and its evaluation at E is onto because
epimorphisms in A are pointwise onto. This proves the exact equivalence
between that weaker condition and (2.2).

## 3. The split telescope sequence with all maps specified

Set T=H|_P and M_n=hX_n. All θ_n:M_n→T are epimorphisms in A.
To see this directly at P=⊕E_i, use
Hom(P,X_n)=∏Hom(E_i,X_n), H(P)=∏H(E_i), and the surjectivity established
in §1. The transition v_n=h(u_n) kills ker θ_n pointwise at E_i,
and therefore at P. Since θ_1 is a pointwise epimorphism, v_1
factors uniquely as

\[
 M_1\xrightarrow{\theta_1}T\xrightarrow{\sigma_2}M_2.
\]

Compatibility and surjectivity of θ_1 give θ_2σ_2=id_T. For n≥2 set
σ_{n+1}=v_nσ_n. Induction gives

\[
 \theta_n\sigma_n=1_T,\qquad v_n\sigma_n=\sigma_{n+1}.
\]

Write L_n=ker θ_n for n≥2. The isomorphism
T⊕L_n→M_n sends (x,z) to σ_nx+z; its inverse sends m to
(θ_nm,m−σ_nθ_nm). Since v_n kills L_n, in these actual coordinates
the transition is

\[
 T\oplus L_n\longrightarrow T\oplus L_{n+1},
       \qquad (x,z)\longmapsto(x,0). \tag{3.1}
\]

Let V=⊕^A_{n≥2}M_n, which exists by (2.2). The idempotent on V
induced on its n-th summand by σ_nθ_n has image C and complementary
image L. The coproduct universal property and the component
retractions show

\[
 C=\coprod_{n\ge2}^{A}T,\quad
 L=\coprod_{n\ge2}^{A}L_n,\quad V=C\oplus L.
\]

For example, maps from C to any F correspond exactly to families of
maps M_n→F that vanish on L_n, hence to families of maps T→F. This
also proves that the displayed coproducts exist; their existence is
not assumed from pointwise sums.

On C let j_n:T→C denote its n-th coproduct inclusion, let
s_Cj_n=j_{n+1}, and let ε:C→T be the fold, εj_n=id_T. Define
\[
 \ell_Cj_n=-\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}j_j,\qquad
 \ell_Cj_2=0.
\]
Each sum is finite, so the coproduct universal property defines an
actual morphism ℓ_C:C→C. Direct calculation on each inclusion gives

\[
 \ell_C(1-s_C)j_n=j_n,\qquad
 (1-s_C)\ell_Cj_n=j_n-j_2,\qquad \varepsilon j_2=1_T.
\]

Consequently
\[
 \ell_C(1-s_C)=1_C,\quad
 (1-s_C)\ell_C=1_C-j_2\varepsilon,\quad
 \varepsilon(1-s_C)=0.
\]
These identities prove the split exact sequence
0→C→^{1−s_C}C→^εT→0. In (3.1) the transition is zero on L, so the
tail telescope map t_V:V→V is (1−s_C)⊕id_L. Its left inverse is
ℓ_V=ℓ_C⊕id_L, and its cokernel is T with map induced by the θ_n.

Retain the initial object M_1, which need not split as T⊕ker θ_1.
Put b=j_2^Vv_1:M_1→V. Relative to M=M_1⊕V=hS, where S=⊕_{n≥1}X_n,
the actual telescope map t=h(1−s) is

\[
 t(x,y)=(x,t_Vy-bx).
\]

The target automorphism A_0(x,y)=(x,y+bx), whose inverse is
(x,y)↦(x,y−bx), satisfies
\[
 A_0t(x,y)=(x,t_Vy).
\]
Thus
\[
 \ell(x,y)=(x,\ell_V(y+bx))
\]
is a left inverse of t. Its cokernel is T. In fact the cokernel map
Θ:M→T is induced by the original θ_n; in these coordinates
Θ(x,y)=θ_1x+Θ_Vy, because Θ_Vb=θ_2v_1=θ_1. A section is
x↦(0,j_2^Vσ_2x). We have proved the split exact sequence in A

\[
 0\longrightarrow hS\xrightarrow{h(1-s)}hS
          \xrightarrow{\Theta}H|_P\longrightarrow0. \tag{3.2}
\]

Evaluating its left inverse at E proves exactly the source injectivity
Hom(E,S)→^{(1−s)_*}Hom(E,S). It does not imply that 1−s:S→S has a
left inverse in D: the natural transformation ℓ is only defined on
the restriction to P. Since E[−1] is also in the enlarged set, the
natural shift comparison gives injectivity on Hom(E,S[1]) as well.

## 8. Rejected shortcuts and retained scope

Coordinate projections of a map E→⊕X_n may all vanish without the
map vanishing when E is not compact. The attempted argument from
projections alone therefore does not prove the required injectivity.
Nor does the factorization hypothesis put each arbitrary β_n into
ker θ_n. Neither shortcut is used in §§2–4.

For reference, the direct finite-sum calculation from an arbitrary
factorization does prove a cokernel statement. Choose δ_n as in §4,
put B=⊕E_n and β|_{E_n}=ι_nβ_n,
\(\widetilde\beta|_{E_n}=\iota_nu_{n,1}\delta_n\), and
k=β−\(\widetilde\beta\). Then sk=0 and t_Dk=k. Define

\[
 g|_{E_n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\iota_ju_{j,1}\delta_n,\qquad
 d|_{E_n}=\delta_n.
\]

Every defining sum is finite, and direct telescoping gives
t_Dg=ι_1d−\(\widetilde\beta\), whence
β=ι_1d+t_D(k−g). After composition with γ, every α:E→S therefore has
the form ι_1z+t_Dη. This calculation alone neither proves that t_D
is injective on Hom(E,S) nor that every E→X lifts to S. The
restricted-functor argument supplies exactly these missing facts.
