## 1. Exact totalization, preserving both component conventions

The source uses commuting double-complex differentials and the full
total differential
\[
T(A)^n=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}A^{p,q},\qquad
D|_{A^{p,q}}=d_1^{p,q}+(-1)^p d_2^{p,q}.
\]
The two diagonal squares of \(D^2\) vanish because \(d_1^2=d_2^2=0\).
Its two mixed terms are
\((-1)^{p+1}d_2^{p+1,q}d_1^{p,q}\) and
\((-1)^pd_1^{p,q+1}d_2^{p,q}\); they cancel by the commuting-square
condition. Equality on every summand inclusion proves the equality
on the coproduct; no rearrangement of an infinite sum of morphisms
is required.

For the first convention, with outer degree \(q\), the source
shift is \([0,1]\). The exact functor comparison
\(\xi_A:T(A[0,1])\to T(A)[1]\) multiplies the summand
\(A^{p,q+1}\) by \((-1)^p\). This is the inverse of the
original \(\gamma\) in Homology, and its inverse has the same
component sign. On a horizontal differential, the source has
coefficient \(1\) and the target has coefficient \(-1\),
balanced by \((-1)^{p+1}=-(-1)^p\). On a vertical differential,
both sides have coefficient \((-1)^{p+1}\) before applying that
same sign. Thus this is an actual chain isomorphism, natural in all
bigraded chain maps.

If \(h^{p,q}:A^{p,q}\to B^{p,q-1}\) is an outer homotopy,
its total homotopy is \(H|_{A^{p,q}}=(-1)^ph^{p,q}\).
The four terms of \(DH+HD\) on this summand are
\[
(-1)^pd_1h+d_2h+(-1)^{p+1}hd_1+hd_2.
\]
The first and third cancel because each \(h^q\) is a horizontal
chain map. The other two are precisely \(f^{p,q}-g^{p,q}\).
For a split sequence of these outer complexes, totalizing the actual
sections and retractions gives a split sequence degree by degree.
The connecting map on \(C^{p,q}\) is
\[
\pi^{p+1,q}d_1s^{p,q}
+(-1)^p\pi^{p,q+1}d_2s^{p,q}
=(-1)^p\pi^{p,q+1}d_2s^{p,q}.
\]
The omitted first term is zero for a reason: the section commutes
with \(d_1\), and \(\pi s=0\). The remaining term is exactly
\(\xi_A T(\delta)\). Hence images of the distinguished split
triangles have the required boundary.

For the second convention, with outer degree \(p\), the shift is
\([1,0]\). The comparison \(T(A[1,0])\to T(A)[1]\) is the
identity on the reindexed summands. For an outer homotopy
\(h^{p,q}:A^{p,q}\to B^{p-1,q}\), use \(H=h\). The four
terms are
\[
d_1h+(-1)^{p-1}d_2h+hd_1+(-1)^phd_2.
\]
Here the two vertical terms cancel, and the horizontal pair gives
\(f-g\). The connecting map of a totalized split sequence is
\(\pi^{p+1,q}d_1s^{p,q}\): its vertical term vanishes because
the sections are vertical chain maps. These are the two exactness
claims at 3487–3532, with the full original comparisons retained.
They agree with the previously published Homology review, rather
than defining a replacement convention.

## 2. DERIVED-NEW-013 and DERIVED-UNDER-004

For fixed \(X^\bullet\in\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal A)\),
the variable \(Y^\bullet\) belongs to \(\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal B)\).
Consequently the domain at 3572 is \(\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal B)\).
For fixed \(Y^\bullet\), the domain of the functor varying
\(X^\bullet\), at 3574, is \(\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal A)\).
Those two source categories are interchanged in the printed proof.
The two theorem assertions at 3555–3568 already have the correct
domains. **DERIVED-NEW-013** repairs just the two domains in the proof.

Bilinearity supplies all needed identities: \(1_X\otimes h\)
is the outer homotopy for the first functor, the horizontal
chain-map identity follows from functoriality in the other variable,
and the five biproduct splitting equations are preserved by
\(X^p\otimes-\). The second variable-fixed construction uses
\(h\otimes1_Y\) and the same equations. Thus both functors into
the respective double-complex homotopy categories are exact with
their literal outer shifts. Composing with §1 proves the totalized
claims. The first totalized comparison has component \((-1)^p\)
on \(X^p\otimes Y^{q+1}\); the second has component identity
on \(X^{p+1}\otimes Y^q\). The direct receiving tensor lemmas in
More on Algebra, Cohomology and Cohomology on Sites therefore keep
their original conclusions and all flatness-related distinctions.

The same argument yields an editorial extension without requiring all
countable coproducts. Let \(\mathcal E\) be an additive category.
Inside either of the two homotopy categories of double complexes,
take the full subcategory on those actual double complexes \(A\)
for which every displayed diagonal coproduct
\(\bigoplus_{p+q=n}A^{p,q}\) exists. This full subcategory is
triangulated, and totalization on it is exact with the same comparisons.

Here is the closure and exactness proof. Diagonal sums of \(A[0,1]\)
and \(A[1,0]\) are reindexings of the next diagonal sum of \(A\);
the inverse shifts work in the same way. For a first-convention cone
of \(f:A\to B\), the terms are
\(B^{p,q}\oplus A^{p,q+1}\), so its diagonal sum exists and is
canonically the finite biproduct \(T(B)^n\oplus T(A)^{n+1}\).
For the second convention they are
\(B^{p,q}\oplus A^{p+1,q}\), with the same conclusion. These
identifications follow from the coproduct universal property, without
discarding or identifying any labelled summands. The zero object and
finite direct sums also remain in the subcategory. The already proved
triangulated-subcategory criterion therefore applies.

In the first convention the isomorphism from the totalized outer
cone to \(C(T(f))\) is identity on each \(B^{p,q}\) and
\((-1)^p\) on each \(A^{p,q+1}\). The totalized outer-cone
differential has upper-right entry \((-1)^pf\), source-part
horizontal differential \(d_1\), and source-part vertical
differential \(-(-1)^pd_2\). After the stated map, these become
respectively \(f\), \(-d_1\), and \(-(-1)^pd_2\), the three
entries required by the original cone of \(T(f)\). The changing
sign between horizontal source indices accounts for \(-d_1\).
The inclusion square is identity, and the last square is the exact
comparison \(\xi_A\) on the source shift, with the retained
negative projection. In the second convention the comparison is
identity on both sets of summands: the outer cone already has
source-part differential \(-d_1+(-1)^{p}d_2\) at new index
\(p\), which is \(-d_1-(-1)^{p+1}d_2\) at its old source
index. Its upper-right entry is \(f\). Thus totalization preserves
distinguished cone triangles and is exact in both cases.

For a fixed tensor factor, restrict the variable-complex homotopy
category to those objects for which the tensor double complex has
these diagonal coproducts. Bilinearity and the same cone and shift
calculation prove closure and exactness on that full triangulated
subcategory. In particular, when both factors are bounded on the same
side, every diagonal has finite support and finite biproducts suffice.
For lower bounds \(p\geq a,q\geq b\), a fixed diagonal satisfies
\(a\leq p\leq n-b\); for upper bounds it satisfies
\(n-b\leq p\leq a\). These are the actual finite index intervals.

This is **DERIVED-UNDER-004**, propagating the diagonal-coproduct
calculation in **HOMOLOGY-UNDER-009** to exact functors and their
domains. Existence of the sums remains essential. The Homology
counterexample in finite-dimensional vector spaces, with identity
horizontal pairs in bidegrees \((2i,-2i),(2i+1,-2i)\), has no
degree-zero diagonal coproduct. It is excluded by the precise domain
just defined. Also that domain need not be closed under arbitrary
outer homotopy equivalences: the same example is outer contractible.
No claim of strict fullness is made. No source theorem is silently
replaced by this editorial extension.

## 3. Cohomology, localization and bounded models

For a termwise split short exact sequence, its connecting morphism is
\(\delta^n=\pi^{n+1}d_B^ns^n\). On a cycle, its image in
\(H^{n+1}(A)\) is the snake-lemma boundary obtained by lifting
along \(s^n\), applying \(d_B^n\), and retracting along
\(\pi^{n+1}\). Independence follows because a different lift
adds an \(A^n\)-component and therefore changes the result by an
\(A\)-boundary. The general abelian-category assertion is the
same pullback/kernel calculation in the referenced Homology lemma,
not an assumption that a morphism from every test object can be lifted.
Consequently \(H^0\) is homological and the source's long sequence
has boundary \(H^i(\delta)\) with its displayed sign.

**OCC-01499 / P02-E0027** and French **DERIVED-016** correctly
request deletion of the unfinished reference placeholder at 3651;
**MC-STK-ERR-0827** already does this, retaining both actual Homology
references. French **DERIVED-017** matches **MC-STK-ERR-0828**,
the insertion of “by” at 3702. The functor being named is the unique
factorization of the original \(H^0\).

The shifted kernel of \(H^0\) is exactly the acyclic complexes.
For a distinguished triangle, the long sequence proves closure under
cones and both shifts, and additivity proves closure under summands.
The cone of a map is acyclic precisely when its cohomology maps are
isomorphisms. Thus the localization denominators are exactly the
quasi-isomorphisms, its kernel is exactly the acyclic subcategory,
and its canonical shift comparison is identity. The quotient and
bounded-subcategory constructions established earlier apply with
these exact objects.

The size warning in the next remark is substantive. In the cited
example, kernels and cokernels of an equivariant abelian-group map
inherit each ordinal-indexed operator, and extending the shorter
operator list by zeros gives the necessary common index. Finite
biproducts do the same. This verifies the abelian structure used
there. For the extensions of \(Z\) by \(Z\), an extension
isomorphism has underlying matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}\).
If the nonzero operator of one extension occurs at ordinal \(\alpha\)
and that of another at \(\beta\ne\alpha\), intertwining the
operator at \(\alpha\) would require the product of that
invertible matrix with
\(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}\) to be zero.
It is not zero. Thus the distinct ordinals yield distinct extension
classes. The use of their identification with derived Hom is the
cited later Ext lemma, whose complete source-order review remains
later; no new set-sized Hom claim is made for this large category.

For a Grothendieck abelian category the source invokes the specific
K-injective existence theorem in Injectives. Its statement and the
start of its construction were read here; its entire transfinite proof
was not reaudited. The receiving comparison is exact: if \(I\)
is K-injective and \(s:M\to N\) is a quasi-isomorphism, its
acyclic cone and all shifts have zero Hom into \(I\). The long
Hom sequence makes
\(\operatorname{Hom}_K(N,I)\to\operatorname{Hom}_K(M,I)\)
bijective. Every roof \(N\leftarrow M\to I\) therefore has
a unique representative \(N\to I\); common refinements prove
that this identification is independent of the roof. Composing with
the selected quasi-isomorphism \(L\to I\) gives exactly the
Hom comparison at 3746–3747, and its right side is a set.

The bounded models are obtained with their actual boundary objects:
\[
(\tau_{\leq b}K)^i=
\begin{cases}K^i&i<b,\\\ker d_K^b&i=b,\\0&i>b,\end{cases}
\quad
(\tau_{\geq a}K)^i=
\begin{cases}0&i<a,\\\operatorname{coker}d_K^{a-1}&i=a,\\K^i&i>a.
\end{cases}
\]
Their differentials are the original ones or their induced kernel
and cokernel maps. Choose \(a<b\) outside the nonzero cohomology
range. The two truncations commute on each degree, including the
distinct boundary degrees, so their composite is the original
\(N\) in the four-corner diagram. All four maps induce the
identity on the surviving cohomology and zero-to-zero outside it.

For bounded-below complexes a left fraction \(s^{-1}f\) can have
its common target replaced by its lower truncation, since that target
is quasi-isomorphic to the bounded-below denominator source. This
gives fullness. If the fraction is zero, a further denominator kills
\(f\); lower-truncate its target again to give the same equality
inside the bounded category. Additivity reduces equality of arbitrary
fractions to this zero test, proving faithfulness. For bounded-above
complexes use right fractions and upper truncation of their common
source. For bounded complexes perform the bounded-below argument
inside the bounded-above category; lower truncation preserves that
upper bound. This supplies all three equivalences, with the kernel
and essential surjectivity already verified.

## 4. The canonical delta-functor and truncation triangles

For the original short exact sequence with maps \(a,b\), put
\(q=(b,0):C(a)\to C\). Its kernel is identified with
\(C(1_A)\) by \((x,y)\mapsto(a(x),y)\). The differential
under that identification is
\(\begin{psmallmatrix}d_A&1\\0&-d_A\end{psmallmatrix}\).
The homotopy \(H^n(x,y)=(0,x)\) has
\(dH+Hd=(x,y)\), so the kernel is contractible. The exact
cohomology sequence makes \(q\) a quasi-isomorphism.

For a commuting map of short exact sequences \((f,g,h)\), the
canonical cone map in degree \(n\) is
\(c^n=\begin{psmallmatrix}g^n&0\\0&f^{n+1}\end{psmallmatrix}\).
It satisfies \(q'c=hq\), \(p'c=f[1]p\). Hence, in the
derived category,
\[
(-p'(q')^{-1})h=-p'c q^{-1}=f[1](-pq^{-1}).
\]
This proves the actual boundary naturality, including the shift.
The signed cone triangle and the isomorphism \(q\) prove the
distinguishedness. This is a further checked receiving use of
DERIVED-NEW-007. All bounded variants preserve the cone's one shift
and finite sum. French **DERIVED-018** matches **MC-STK-ERR-0829**,
adding “above” at 3968 without changing this construction.

When all three vertical maps are quasi-isomorphisms, this same
naturality gives the claimed triangle isomorphism. In the split case,
the earlier homotopy inverse \(S=(s,-\delta)\) satisfies
\(qS=1\), \(Sq\simeq1\), and \(-pS=\delta\). Thus the
canonical boundary is exactly the image of the original split
boundary. **OCC-01500 / P02-E0028**, French **DERIVED-019**, and
French **DERIVED-020** match **MC-STK-ERR-0830**, **0831**,
the two singular-sequence copyedits.

For the first truncation triangle, write
\(U=K/\tau_{\leq a}K\). The natural map
\(U\to\tau_{\geq a+1}K\) has kernel concentrated in degrees
\(a,a+1\), with both terms \(\operatorname{im}d_K^a\) and
differential identity under the canonical image/coimage
identification. Hence the kernel is acyclic and the map is a
quasi-isomorphism. The canonical delta-functor triangle transports
across that precise map. Applying the same construction to
\(\tau_{\leq a+1}K\) leaves only the quotient cohomology
\(H^{a+1}(K)[-a-1]\). For the lower truncations, the kernel of
\(\tau_{\geq a}K\to\tau_{\geq a+1}K\) has term
\(K^a/\operatorname{im}d^{a-1}\) at \(a\), term
\(\operatorname{im}d^a\) at \(a+1\), and the induced
surjection between them. Inclusion of its kernel
\(H^a(K)[-a]\) is a quasi-isomorphism. This proves the third
triangle and its final shift \([-a+1]\), with all original
quotient contributions retained.

## 5. DERIVED-UNDER-005: an explicit bound and derived morphisms

The induction at 4154–4160 applies its one-step case after shifting
the cohomological cutoff. Its full form is the following. Let
\(f_j:K_j\to K_{j+1}\), \(0\leq j<n\), be morphisms in
\(D(\mathcal A)\), and fix an integer \(b\). Assume
\[
H^i(K_0)=0\quad(i>b),\qquad H^{b-j}(f_j)=0\quad(0\leq j<n).
\]
Then the composite factors through
\(\tau_{\leq b-n}K_n\to K_n\). The intermediate complexes
need not have any boundedness assumption. This includes arbitrary
derived morphisms, not only the chain maps in the source statement.

We prove the needed one-step assertion without assuming a representative
exists on the original pair of complexes. If \(X\) has cohomology
zero above \(m\), represent \(f:X\to Y\) by a roof
\(X\xleftarrow{s}Z\xrightarrow{g}Y\). The map
\(\tau_{\leq m}Z\to Z\) is a quasi-isomorphism. The restricted
chain map factors through \(\tau_{\leq m}Y\), because its
degree-\(m\) component carries cycles to cycles. The composite
\(\tau_{\leq m}Z\to H^m(Y)[-m]\) equals the canonical
projection to \(H^m(Z)[-m]\) followed by \(H^m(g)\).
If \(H^m(f)=0\), then \(H^m(g)=H^m(f)H^m(s)=0\).
Thus that composite is zero as a chain map. Applying the exact
representable Hom sequence to the canonical triangle
\[
\tau_{\leq m-1}Y\to\tau_{\leq m}Y\to H^m(Y)[-m]
\]
gives the required lift \(X\to\tau_{\leq m-1}Y\).

These truncation constructions are natural on the derived category.
On complexes their kernel/cokernel definitions are functorial and
preserve quasi-isomorphisms by the displayed cohomology formulas.
They also preserve homotopies: at the upper cutoff restrict the last
homotopy component to the kernel; at the lower cutoff project its
first nonzero target component to the cokernel. The boundary equations
are exactly the original \(f-g=dh+hd\), restricted or projected.
The universal property of localization therefore supplies the induced
functors and their canonical maps used above.

Starting with \(u_0:K_0\to\tau_{\leq b}K_0\), the inverse
of the canonical quasi-isomorphism, suppose the first \(j\) maps
factor as \(u_j:K_0\to\tau_{\leq b-j}K_j\) followed by
the canonical inclusion. Apply the one-step assertion at
\(m=b-j\) to
\[
\tau_{\leq b-j}K_j\longrightarrow K_j\xrightarrow{f_j}K_{j+1}.
\]
Its degree-\(b-j\) cohomology map vanishes by hypothesis.
It therefore factors through \(\tau_{\leq b-j-1}K_{j+1}\).
Composing that factorization with \(u_j\) constructs \(u_{j+1}\).
Induction proves the stated result. For the source's \(n=2\)
step this is the application at cutoff \(-1\), equivalently the
cutoff-zero case on complexes shifted by \([-1]\). It is an
implicit shift in the source, not a failure of its conclusion.

The dual assertion says that for arrows
\(K_n\to\cdots\to K_0\), with \(H^i(K_0)=0\) below
\(a\) and \(H^{a+j}(K_{j+1}\to K_j)=0\), the composite
factors through \(K_n\to\tau_{\geq a+n}K_n\).
To obtain it, reverse arrows and degrees: a cochain complex in
\(\mathcal A^{op}\) associated to \(K\) has degree \(i\)
object \(K^{-i}\) and differential the opposite of the original
map \(d_K^{-i-1}\). Cohomology in degree \(i\) is the opposite
of \(H^{-i}(K)\); quasi-isomorphisms and their roofs are reversed,
and upper truncation becomes lower truncation under this operation.
Applying the just-proved factorization with bound \(-a\) and
reversing it back gives exactly the claimed arrow and indices.

Now suppose additionally that \(H^i(K_n)=0\) for \(i<a\)
and \(n>b-a\). The receiving truncation
\(\tau_{\leq b-n}K_n\) is acyclic, hence zero in the derived
category. The composite is zero. In particular, for a complex with
cohomology in \([a,b]\), a composite of \(b-a+1\) endomorphisms
that each act by zero on all cohomology is zero. These endomorphisms
form an ideal, since cohomology is an additive functor. Its
\((b-a+1)\)-st power is therefore zero. If \(u\) is one of
them, the complete finite formula
\[
(1-u)^{-1}=1+u+u^2+\cdots+u^{b-a}
\]
follows by multiplying on either side and retaining the terminal
term \(-u^{b-a+1}=0\). No global dimension or existence of
projective/injective resolutions is used for this consequence.

This is **DERIVED-UNDER-005**. It is a proved corollary and extension
of the original factorization argument, not a claim of a new theorem
in the literature. Its source theorem stays unchanged.
