## 1. Products and the defining triangle

For a tower with transition maps f\_{n+1}:K\_{n+1}→K\_n, let
P=∏\_n K\_n with projections π\_n and define Δ:P→P by

 π\_n Δ=π\_n-f\_{n+1}π\_{n+1}.

This is a map specified by its product components, not an assumption
that the K\_n have elements. A homotopy limit is a distinguished triangle

 C --j--> P --Δ--> P --w--> C[1].                       (1.1)

Existence follows by completing Δ to a triangle and rotating.
Two such triangles have an isomorphism respecting j, Δ and w,
by TR3 and the two-isomorphisms criterion for maps of triangles.
This need not choose a unique map between their first objects.

Suppose A is abelian with exact countable products.
The termwise product of complexes has
H^p(∏K\_n)=∏H^p(K\_n): apply exact products to the short exact
sequences for kernels, images and cohomology. Thus products preserve
quasi-isomorphisms. For maps L→K\_n in D(A), choose right roofs
L→f\_n M\_n←s\_n K\_n, where s\_n is a quasi-isomorphism.
Then s=∏s\_n is a quasi-isomorphism, and the maps f\_n give
f:L→∏M\_n. The right roof s^(-1)f gives the desired product map.

Here is the uniqueness omitted in the source.
Put P=∏K\_n termwise, and suppose γ:L→P has zero composite with
every projection. Write γ=s^(-1)f with s:P→M a quasi-isomorphism.
For each projection p\_n:P→K\_n, right fraction calculus gives
u\_n:M→N\_n and a quasi-isomorphism t\_n:K\_n→N\_n with
u\_n s=t\_n p\_n in K(A). Since u\_n f is zero in D(A), the zero
criterion for localization allows postcomposition by a further
quasi-isomorphism N\_n→N'\_n to make u\_n f zero in K(A).
Use these refined u\_n and t\_n.

Form U:M→∏N'\_n and T=∏t\_n:P→∏N'\_n.
Coordinate homotopies combine to U s=T and U f=0 in K(A),
by the degreewise product property. T and s are quasi-isomorphisms,
so U is one. It follows that γ=(U s)^(-1)U f=0.
This proves the product property in D(A) without assuming it in
the uniqueness argument.

With enough injectives and merely existing countable products,
a tower whose individual objects lie in D^+(A) also has a derived
product: choose a bounded below injective representative I\_n
for each object. Every I\_n is K-injective, and the full Hom-complex
product argument in PART14 proves ∏I\_n is K-injective and
represents the product in D(A). No common lower bound is required.
Its difference map gives (1.1). This proves the source's second
existence criterion without adding exactness of products.

## 2. Maps into a homotopy limit

Use the shifted boundary k=-w[-1]:P[-1]→C. With the source
rotation convention this is the first arrow of the distinguished
triangle P[-1]→C→P→P. Applying Hom(T,-) gives the exact segment

 Hom(T,P[-1]) --Δ[-1]\_\*--> Hom(T,P[-1]) --k\_\*-->
 Hom(T,C) --j\_\*--> Hom(T,P) --Δ\_\*--> Hom(T,P).

Representable Hom preserves products. Thus Δ\_\* is precisely the
difference map on ∏Hom(T,K\_n), and the preceding map is the
difference map on ∏Hom(T,K\_n[-1]).
Consequently

 0→coker Δ\_{Hom(T,K\_n[-1])}→Hom(T,C)
   →lim\_n Hom(T,K\_n)→0.                               (2.1)

The injection sends a family b:T→P[-1] to k b=-w[-1]b,
with its indicated sign. The right map is given by all π\_n j.
The cokernel is the R^1 lim of this abelian-group tower, by the
explicit two-term complex from More on Algebra.
In particular compatible maps T→K\_n lift to T→C, and their
possible lifts form a torsor under the left group.
Nothing here requires exact products in the underlying abelian
category when the needed product already exists in D.

## 3. Pro-morphisms: exact finite sums and independence

Write t\_{r,s}:K\_s→K\_r for the original transition composite,
with t\_{r,r}=id. Let g denote transitions in a second tower L.
A pro-morphism may be represented by strictly increasing indices
m\_n and maps a\_n:K\_{m\_n}→L\_n satisfying
g\_{n+1}a\_{n+1}=a\_n t\_{m\_n,m\_{n+1}}.
To obtain such a representation, choose successive representatives
of the inverse-limit-of-colimits definition of a pro-morphism.
At stage n+1, choose an index larger than m\_n and large enough that
the required equality with the n-th representative holds. That
equality is available after a sufficiently late transition by the
definition of equality in the filtered colimit. Earlier equalities
then follow by composition. This also permits m\_n≥n.

The source's two product maps have exact components

 π\_n a'=a\_n π\_{m\_n},
 π\_n a''=Σ\_{m\_n≤r<m\_{n+1}}a\_n t\_{m\_n,r}π\_r.             (3.1)

The sum in each component is finite. Telescoping proves

 π\_n a''Δ\_K
 =a\_n π\_{m\_n}-a\_n t\_{m\_n,m\_{n+1}}π\_{m\_{n+1}}
 =π\_n Δ\_L a'.

Thus Δ\_L a'=a''Δ\_K. TR3, after rotation, gives a map C\_K→C\_L
and a map of triangles with product components a' and a''.
This verifies every transition factor in original 10539.
The spelling correction in unit 0886 changes none of them.

The construction induces well-defined maps on kernel and cokernel
of the abelian-group difference maps. Here are the full comparison
homotopies, including the omitted representation-independence step.
If m\_n is enlarged to M\_n≥m\_n and
b\_n=a\_n t\_{m\_n,M\_n}, define a map H:∏K\_r→∏L\_n by

 π\_n H=Σ\_{m\_n≤r<M\_n}a\_n t\_{m\_n,r}π\_r.

The same finite telescoping gives

 a'-b'=HΔ\_K,             a''-b''=Δ\_L H.                (3.2)

For the second equality, expand π\_n H-g\_{n+1}π\_{n+1}H.
Compatibility converts the second sum into one with coefficients
a\_n t\_{m\_n,r}; subtracting the two intervals leaves the interval
[m\_n,m\_{n+1}) minus [M\_n,M\_{n+1}), exactly a''-b''.
Thus enlargement does not change the maps on ker Δ or coker Δ.
Two representatives of the same pro-map have a common increasing
enlargement where their component maps agree; (3.2) proves their
maps on these groups agree.

For a second pro-map b\_n:L\_{ℓ\_n}→N\_n, the composite uses
indices m\_{ℓ\_n} and components b\_n a\_{ℓ\_n}.
The degree-zero product map is b'a'. In degree one, expand b''a'':
its outer interval ℓ\_n≤q<ℓ\_{n+1} and inner intervals
m\_q≤r<m\_{q+1} partition
[m\_{ℓ\_n},m\_{ℓ\_{n+1}}). Compatibility converts each coefficient
to b\_n a\_{ℓ\_n}t\_{m\_{ℓ\_n},r}.
Hence its degree-one map is exactly the composite's map.
For an identity represented by transitions t\_{n,m\_n},
compare to m\_n=n in (3.2). The induced maps on kernels and
cokernels are identities.
This proves functoriality and invariance under a pro-isomorphism.

Apply this to the towers Hom(T,K\_n) and Hom(T,K\_n[-1]).
A pro-isomorphism gives isomorphisms on both outer terms of (2.1).
The map of triangles supplies a commuting map of its short exact
sequences, so the map Hom(T,C\_K)→Hom(T,C\_L) is an isomorphism
for every T. Yoneda then makes C\_K→C\_L an isomorphism.
This proves the asserted receiving More on Algebra result using
the same a',a'' as the source, including any TR3 completion.
It does not assert that the resulting map itself is uniquely chosen.

## 4. The truncation tower and choice of its comparison

Let T\_n=τ\_{\\geq -n}K and assume its product in D(A) exists.
Let C be its homotopy limit. The canonical maps c\_n:K→T\_n
are compatible, so (2.1) supplies c:K→C with
π\_n j c=c\_n.
For each fixed p, put N\_p=max(1,-p). The cohomology tower has
H^p(T\_n)=0 for n<N\_p and H^p(T\_n)=H^p(K) for n≥N\_p,
with identity transitions on the latter factors.

For m≥N\_p, the composite
H^p(K)→H^p(C)→H^p(T\_m)=H^p(K) is the identity.
Thus H^p(c) is an isomorphism if and only if the second map is
an isomorphism: when either is invertible the other is its inverse.
The second map is independent of c. Any isomorphism of choices of
the triangle preserves these projections, so the criterion is also
independent of the chosen homotopy limit.
This proves original 10574–10585. Existing unit 0887 removes
the redundant connector without changing this argument.

The comparison c is one possible lift, not a canonical choice
deduced from compatible stage maps. In particular, showing that a
specific chain map γ supplies one such lift proves the criterion
for every lift, by the preceding argument; it does not prove that
all lifts are literally equal.

## 5. The split injective tower and the actual chain map

Assume countable products and enough injectives in A.
Use the strictly commuting diagram of original Lemma
lemma-special-inverse-system: maps τ\_{\\geq -n}K→I\_n are
quasi-isomorphisms and the maps f\_{n+1}:I\_{n+1}→I\_n are
termwise split epimorphisms. Each I\_n is bounded below with
injective terms, hence K-injective.

Fix a degree p and choose sections s\_{n+1}^p of f\_{n+1}^p.
Put C\_1^p=I\_1^p and C\_n^p=ker f\_n^p for n≥2.
The successive chosen decompositions identify
I\_n^p with the finite sum of C\_1^p,…,C\_n^p, keeping the
transition map as the projection dropping its last component.
The termwise limit is therefore I^p=∏\_n C\_n^p, with the
actual projections λ\_n^p:I^p→I\_n^p from those decompositions.
The differentials on the I\_n induce a unique differential on I
commuting with all λ\_n; its square is zero after every projection,
so is zero. Thus this is a complex and the termwise limit.

Let P^p=∏\_n I\_n^p and let Δ^p have components
x\_n-f\_{n+1}^p x\_{n+1}.
Define a section σ^p:P^p→P^p recursively by

 (σ^p y)\_1=0,
 (σ^p y)\_{n+1}=s\_{n+1}^p((σ^p y)\_n-y\_n).              (5.1)

Every component is a finite expression in the projections of y,
so (5.1) defines a morphism in A by the product property.
It satisfies Δ^p σ^p=id exactly.
The compatible sequences are exactly im λ^p, which proves

 0→I^p --λ^p--> P^p --Δ^p--> P^p→0                    (5.2)

is split exact. To identify the other splitting mentioned in
the source, let ρ^p:P^p→∏C\_n^p take from x\_n its C\_n^p
component. Then ρ^p λ^p=id, ρ^p σ^p=0, and
λ^pρ^p+σ^pΔ^p=id: subtract σ^pΔ^p x from x to get a
compatible sequence, whose C\_n coordinates are precisely ρ^p x.

The splittings need not be chain maps, and are not asserted to be.
The λ and Δ are chain maps and give a termwise split exact
sequence of complexes. Its triangle in K(A) and D(A) is
I→P→P→I[1]. The product-of-K-injectives argument from PART14
identifies P as the derived product of the I\_n and makes it
K-injective. Closure under triangles then makes I K-injective.
Equivalently PART15's split inverse-system argument proves this.
The displayed triangle identifies I with holim I\_n and, through
the stage quasi-isomorphisms, with holim τ\_{\\geq -n}K.

The strictly compatible composite maps K→τ\_{\\geq -n}K→I\_n
give a unique actual chain map γ:K→I by the termwise limit property.
It represents a lift c\_γ:K→holim τ\_{\\geq -n}K with the prescribed
stage maps. Hence γ is a quasi-isomorphism exactly when c\_γ
is an isomorphism. Section 4 makes this equivalent to any chosen
comparison c being an isomorphism.
This justifies the source's conclusion without identifying all
possible c with a single predetermined map in its displayed diagram.
There is no new source-defect count for that choice convention.

No assertion that γ is a quasi-isomorphism has yet been made
under countable products alone. Its obstruction is computed next.

## 6. The exact recovery obstruction without exact products

DERIVED-UNDER-025 concerns the actual truncation tower T\_n,
assuming only that its product P and homotopy limit C exist
in D(A). Exactness of products in A and enough injectives
are not assumed.
Write A\_p=H^p(P) and δ\_p=H^p(Δ).
The triangle gives a short exact sequence in A

 0→C\_p→H^p(C) --z\_p--> Z\_p→0,
 C\_p=coker δ\_{p-1},             Z\_p=ker δ\_p.            (6.1)

The first injection is induced by the shifted boundary of (1.1);
thus its sign is the one specified in Section 2.
All these kernels and cokernels exist since A is abelian.
There is no replacement of A\_p by a product of cohomology objects.

For m≥N\_p the projection P→T\_m induces
q\_p:Z\_p→H^p(K). This map is independent of m:
on Z\_p the equations δ\_p=0 say that adjacent projections
are related by the cohomology transition maps, which are
identities after N\_p.
The canonical product map κ:K→P has components c\_n.
The equality Δκ=0 lets H^p(κ) factor as
s\_p:H^p(K)→Z\_p, and q\_p s\_p=id.
Define B\_p=ker q\_p and

 E\_p=ker(q\_p z\_p:H^p(C)→H^p(K)).

Pull back the epimorphism z\_p along B\_p→Z\_p in (6.1).
Its kernel remains C\_p and the pullback object is E\_p, so

 0→C\_p→E\_p→B\_p→0                                    (6.2)

is exact. These are explicit objects with the indicated maps,
independent of the choice of c.
For any lift c with the required stage maps,
(q\_p z\_p)H^p(c)=id. Consequently

 H^p(K)⊕E\_p→H^p(C),  (x,e)↦H^p(c)x+e                (6.3)

is an isomorphism. Its inverse sends y to
((q\_p z\_p)y, y-H^p(c)(q\_p z\_p)y).
This writes out the actual splitting without suppressing the defect.
It depends on the chosen c; E\_p itself does not.

It follows that every such c is an isomorphism in D(A) exactly when

 coker δ\_{p-1}=0 and ker(q\_p:ker δ\_p→H^p(K))=0
 for every integer p.                               (6.4)

Indeed (6.2) makes these conditions equivalent to E\_p=0, and
(6.3) then makes every H^p(c) invertible.
Conversely invertibility of c makes the kernel in (6.3) zero,
forcing both outer objects in (6.2) to vanish.
This is a proved necessary-and-sufficient criterion, not an
assumption that the missing product exactness holds.
Under Section 5's hypotheses it is also the exact criterion
for the specific γ:K→I to be a quasi-isomorphism.

## 7. Exact products, stabilization, and removal of an unused hypothesis

DERIVED-UNDER-026 assumes exact countable products in A,
but does not assume enough injectives.
Section 1 supplies products and homotopy limits for every
countable tower in D(A). For any such tower K\_n its triangle
has the cohomology exact sequence

 0→coker(Δ:∏H^{p-1}(K\_n)→∏H^{p-1}(K\_n))
 →H^p(holim K\_n)
 →ker(Δ:∏H^p(K\_n)→∏H^p(K\_n))→0.                     (7.1)

All maps are induced by the original projections, transitions
and boundary of the chosen triangle. This follows from (1.1)
and H^p of the actual product, as proved in Section 1.

Here is the needed vanishing for any eventually isomorphic tower
B\_n in A, with f\_{n+1} isomorphisms for n≥N.
Write t\_{r,s}:B\_s→B\_r for its unchanged transition composites.
Define σ:∏B\_n→∏B\_n by finite component formulas

 (σ y)\_N=0,
 (σ y)\_n=Σ\_{n≤j<N}t\_{n,j}y\_j,                         n<N,
 (σ y)\_n=-Σ\_{N≤j<n}(t\_{j,n})^(-1)y\_j,                 n>N. (7.2)

Every inverse in the last formula is of a transition within
the isomorphism range. Direct cancellation gives
(σy)\_n-f\_{n+1}(σy)\_{n+1}=y\_n, also at n=N.
Thus Δσ=id. Its kernel is B\_N by the map with components
t\_{n,N} for n<N and (t\_{N,n})^(-1) for n≥N.
The inverse is projection at N. These exact formulas retain
the initial finite part and all transition factors.

Apply this to the truncation tower's H^p. Its nonzero tail
starts at N\_p=max(1,-p). The preceding calculation makes
C\_p=0 in (6.1) and identifies Z\_p with H^p(K) through
the very map q\_p, so B\_p=0 as well.
Section 6 therefore proves every comparison

 K→holim\_n τ\_{\\geq -n}K

is an isomorphism, without enough injectives.
This is the asserted strengthening of the intermediate recovery
claim in original 10670–10685. Enough injectives remain necessary
as an assumption of the source's separate construction of the I\_n;
retaining them, Section 5 now gives the stated K-injective resolution.
The source theorem is not replaced by a stronger existence claim.

The same proof gives a more general convergence assertion.
If for a fixed p the towers H^{p-1}(K\_n) and H^p(K\_n)
are eventually isomorphic, (7.1) identifies H^p(holim K\_n)
with the eventual H^p(K\_n), by the actual stable projection.
In fact for this one p it suffices that the first difference map
in (7.1) is epic and that the kernel of the second projects
isomorphically to the chosen stable value.
If E→K\_n are compatible and induce isomorphisms on H^p
for all sufficiently large n for each p, then any lift
E→holim K\_n is an isomorphism: the compatibility makes the
cohomology transitions eventually isomorphic in every degree,
and the stable projection composite is exactly the given isomorphism.
The stabilization index may depend on p; no uniform bound is used.

Existing unit MC-STK-ERR-0882 makes n the explicit product index
in original 10681 while keeping the condition p≥-n.
For fixed p it is precisely the product over n≥N\_p used here.
No bound on the fixed degree p is substituted for the tower index.

## 8. The source's omitted dual statements

The source says that the duals of its three colimit lemmas could
be stated here. They follow with the following exact hypotheses.
These supplementary proofs are not counted as source errors.

First, assume all countable inverse limits in A exist and the
inverse-limit functor on towers is exact.
The inverse limit of the finite products ∏\_{j≤n}A\_j, with the
projection transitions, is the countable product by its universal
property. Finite products are exact, so exact inverse limits
make countable products exact.
For a tower A\_n, consider the finite split exact sequence

 0→A\_n→∏\_{j≤n}A\_j→∏\_{j<n}A\_j→0.

The first map has components t\_{j,n}; the second has components
x\_j-f\_{j+1}x\_{j+1} for j<n. Its kernel is the image of the
first map, and it is split epic: prescribe x\_n=0 and recursively
solve x\_j=y\_j+f\_{j+1}x\_{j+1} backwards. The sequence maps to
the preceding one by the projections on the products and
the transition A\_n→A\_{n-1}. Its exact inverse limit is

 0→lim A\_n→∏A\_n --Δ--> ∏A\_n→0.                        (8.1)

For a tower of complexes, apply (8.1) termwise. The short exact
sequence yields a distinguished triangle in D(A), and Section 1
identifies its two products as derived products. Thus the
termwise inverse limit is a homotopy limit.
Exact countable products alone are not substituted for exactness
of the inverse-limit functor in this statement.

Finally let D have countable products and let Q satisfy
⊕Hom\_D(E\_n,Q)≅Hom\_D(∏E\_n,Q) for every countable family.
For any inverse tower with limit triangle (1.1), applying
Hom\_D(-,Q) gives

 Hom(P,Q) --Δ^\*--> Hom(P,Q)→Hom(C,Q)→
 Hom(P[-1],Q) --Δ[-1]^\*--> Hom(P[-1],Q).

Under the stipulated direct-sum identifications, both difference
maps have the finite-support columns 1-f\_{n+1}^\* and are injective.
Their cokernel in the first row is colim Hom(K\_n,Q).
Hence the canonical map colim Hom(K\_n,Q)→Hom(C,Q) is an
isomorphism. The injection proof is by successive finite-support
coordinates, exactly as in PART16. This proves the final dual
statement without invoking any hypothetical exact inverse-limit
functor on D.
