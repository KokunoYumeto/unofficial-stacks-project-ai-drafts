## 1. Cofinal computing classes and the composition comparison

For the source's class I, let C_X be the full subcategory of X/S whose
targets lie in I. Every object s:X -> Y has an arrow to an object of
C_X: choose the asserted denominator t:Y -> I_Y and compose ts.
Two objects of C_X have a common successor in X/S; refine its target
once more into I. Parallel arrows can first be equalized in X/S and
then refined into I. Thus C_X is filtered. The same procedure applied
to two objects under a fixed s proves that the corresponding comma
category is nonempty and connected. This proves cofinality, including
the connectedness condition.

Every transition u between its targets is in S, since Q(u) is the
quotient of two invertible structure maps and S is saturated. Therefore
F(u) is an isomorphism by the source's second hypothesis. Choose one
index i. For any j choose a common successor k with arrows a:i -> k,
b:j -> k, and set c_j=F(a)^{-1}F(b):F(X_j) -> F(X_i).
Further common refinements and equalization show this is independent
of both choices. These maps form a cocone; c_i=1. Their defining
equalities give the essential-constancy witness at index i. Thus the
source's word “constant” means canonically isomorphic to a constant
diagram, rather than literal equality of its objects and arrows.
When X belongs to I, choose i=id_X. The cocone identifies the
canonical map F(X) -> RF(X) with the identity. This proves the
computing assertion as well as existence. In the opposite category,
the identical common-predecessor and equalization construction proves
the left-derived criterion for P.

For composition retain the original Q_A, Q_B and F'=Q_B F. Let
c_{s}:F'(X_s) -> RF'(Q_A X) be the right-derived cocone, and write
eta^G_Y:G(Y) -> RG(Q_B Y). The maps

\[
 G(F(X_s))\xrightarrow{\eta^G_{F(X_s)}}RG(F'(X_s))
        \xrightarrow{RG(c_s)}RG(RF'(Q_A X))                 \tag{1.1}
\]

form a cocone, by naturality of eta^G. The universal property of
R(GF)(Q_A X) therefore gives the source's comparison t_X. The only
interchange of RG and a colimit is justified by essential constancy:
applying any functor to a witness, its section identity and its eventual
factorization identity gives another such witness. Hence the image
diagram has value RG(RF'(Q_A X)); no preservation of arbitrary
filtered colimits is being assumed.

For f:X -> Y choose its denominator squares as in the preceding
review. Naturality of eta^G and the cocone identities for RF' make
the composites of (1.1) on these squares equal. Equality on every
colimit structure map proves naturality of t. Denominators are sent
to isomorphisms by all involved derived functors, so the equality also
holds for every localized fraction. The iterated original shift
comparisons commute on each map (1.1), and thus on t. This supplies
the omitted transformation check without identifying Q_A with F or
suppressing Q_B.

For the left version, use the projections LF'(Q_A X) -> F'(X_s),

Applying a functor to the pro-object witness justifies the analogous

## 2. Bounded and unbounded definitions at the same object

For X in K^+(A), the category X/Qis^+(A) is a full subcategory of
X/Qis(A). Every index X -> X' can be followed by the lower truncation
quasi-isomorphism X' -> X'', for X'' bounded below. Common successors
and equalizations in the larger filtered category followed by this
construction prove cofinality, including connected comma categories.
Thus the two indexing diagrams agree after cofinal restriction.

There is also a target-category issue to verify. The bounded diagram
takes values in the fully faithfully embedded D^+(B). If it is
essentially constant there, its witness remains a witness in D(B).
Conversely, if its value V exists as an essentially constant object
in D(B), the witness makes V a retract of F(X'') for one bounded-below
complex X''. Cohomology in every degree below that complex's lower
bound is then a retract of zero, hence zero. Consequently V belongs
to D^+(B). Fullness puts its cocone and witness maps in that category

cofinality of the source categories. The identity-denominator maps
are preserved, so the assertion about computing objects follows.
Reversing arrows, using upper truncations and D^-(B), proves both
left-derived assertions.

## 3. Resolutions by a prescribed class and the acyclic-class criterion

For the source's left-resolution construction, retain its induction
data in degrees j>=n. Write Z_P^n=ker(d_P^n), Z_K^n=ker(d_K^n).
The induction hypothesis includes an epimorphism Z_P^n -> Z_K^n.
The actual object used at the next step is

\[
 E=K^{n-1}\mathbin{\times}_{K^n}Z_P^n
  =K^{n-1}\mathbin{\times}_{Z_K^n}Z_P^n,                 \tag{3.1}
\]

because d_K^{n-1} lands in Z_K^n. Its projection to K^{n-1} is
epic by stability of epimorphisms under pullback in an abelian category.
Choose the given class epimorphism P^{n-1} -> E. Its two projections
give alpha^{n-1} and d_P^{n-1}. They make the square commute,
alpha^{n-1} epic, and d_P^n d_P^{n-1}=0.

The image of E -> Z_P^n is the inverse image of im(d_K^{n-1})
under Z_P^n -> Z_K^n. Indeed (3.1) is the pullback of d_K^{n-1};
factor that map as an epimorphism followed by its image inclusion
and pull back. The image is therefore exactly the kernel of the
epimorphism Z_P^n -> H^n(K). Since P^{n-1} -> E is epic,
quotienting by im(d_P^{n-1}) makes H^n(P) -> H^n(K) an isomorphism.
The kernel of E -> Z_P^n is Z_K^{n-1}. Pulling the epimorphism
P^{n-1} -> E back along this kernel shows that
Z_P^{n-1} -> Z_K^{n-1} is epic. Higher cohomology is unchanged.
These are all clauses of IH_{n-1}. The initial zero terms at n=a+1
satisfy the induction hypotheses because K vanishes above a.
Successive descending choices yield the entire requested complex;
each degree and differential is fixed at a finite stage. Applying
this construction to tau_{<=a}K proves part (2), with the stated
quasi-isomorphism into the original K.

The right-resolution construction is its exact dual. More explicitly,
replace the pullback by the pushout

\[
 K^{n+1}\mathbin{\amalg}_{K^n}\operatorname{coker}(d_I^{n-1})
 =K^{n+1}\mathbin{\amalg}_{\operatorname{coker}(d_K^{n-1})}
                         \operatorname{coker}(d_I^{n-1}),       \tag{3.2}
\]

and embed (3.2) into a chosen class object I^{n+1}. The inductive
monomorphism of cokernels makes K^{n+1} -> (3.2) monic by stability
of monomorphisms under pushout. The maps from K^{n+1} and I^n give
the next component and differential. The dual kernel/image argument
above proves the cohomology and cokernel induction conditions.
Start with zero terms below a and proceed upward. For cohomology
bounded below first use K -> tau_{>=a}K. These constructions require
neither projectivity/injectivity nor closure of the class under sums.

Now impose the hypotheses of lemma-subcategory-right-acyclics. The
class I is nonempty by its embedding property. The sequence
0 -> P -> P -> 0 -> 0 for P in I shows 0 is in I. The same
cokernel condition with P=0 shows closure under isomorphism.
Class resolutions form a cofinal full subcategory of denominators:
resolve the target of each denominator, then refine common successors
and equalizations as in section 1.

Let A in I and A[0] -> I^bullet be a quasi-isomorphism with I^n in I
and I bounded below. Choose n0<0 with I^n=0 below n0. Put
B^n=im(d^{n-1}) and Z^n=ker(d^n). Below degree zero, B^n=Z^n.
Starting with B^{n0}=0, the sequences
0 -> B^n -> I^n -> B^{n+1} -> 0 for n0<=n<0 and the class
hypothesis show that all these B's, including B^0, belong to I,
and that their F-images are exact sequences. Next
0 -> B^0 -> I^0 -> I^0/B^0 -> 0 puts I^0/B^0 in I. The
quasi-isomorphism identifies A with Z^0/B^0, giving
0 -> A -> I^0/B^0 -> B^1 -> 0. Thus B^1 is in I and its
F-sequence is exact. Continue with
0 -> B^n -> I^n -> B^{n+1} -> 0 for n>=1.

Splicing these exact F-sequences shows that H^n(F(I))=0 for n!=0
and that the actual map F(A) -> H^0(F(I)) is an isomorphism. At
degree zero this follows by composing F(I^0) -> F(I^0/B^0) with
the kernel identification F(A)=ker(F(I^0/B^0) -> F(B^1)); it is
the map induced by the original A -> I^0. Hence F(A)[0] -> F(I)
is a quasi-isomorphism. The resulting constant cocone proves A
computes RF. Reversing all sequences and using the quotient class P
proves lemma-subcategory-left-acyclics with its stated hypotheses.

## 5. Leray's lemma and DERIVED-UNDER-007

A bounded complex of right F-acyclic objects computes RF. For a
one-term complex this is the definition and shift invariance. For
support [a,b], the source's termwise split sequence

\[
0\to\sigma_{\ge a+1}A^\bullet\to A^\bullet
                      \to\sigma_{\le a}A^\bullet\to0          \tag{5.1}
\]

gives a distinguished triangle already in the homotopy category.
F preserves its termwise splittings, so this is a triangle to which
the earlier computing-object two-out-of-three lemma applies. Induction
proves both existence of RF and its canonical comparison isomorphism.

For a bounded-below A^bullet with RF(A) defined, fix q and put
P=sigma_{<=q}A, T=sigma_{>=q+1}A. There is a termwise split sequence
0 -> T -> A -> P -> 0 with its actual inclusion, projection, and
connecting map. Suppose only that A^n is right F-acyclic for n<=q.
Then P is bounded and computes RF by the preceding argument. RF(T)
exists by two-out-of-three. The complexes T and F(T) are zero below
q+1, so section 4 gives H^j(RF(T))=0 for j<=q; the same vanishing
holds for H^j(F(T)) by its terms. The comparison of the two triangle
cohomology sequences now proves

\[
 H^j(F(A))\xrightarrow{\sim}H^j(RF(A))\quad(j<q),
 \qquad H^q(F(A))\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow H^q(RF(A)). \tag{5.2}
\]

There is an exact description of the possible cokernel in degree q.
Let beta:H^q(F(P)) -> H^q(RF(P)) be the known isomorphism,
delta_F:H^q(F(P)) -> H^{q+1}(F(T)) the connecting map, and
gamma=H^{q+1}(eta_T):H^{q+1}(F(T)) -> H^{q+1}(RF(T)).
The other connecting map satisfies delta_R beta=gamma delta_F,
with the signs fixed by the same original split sequence. Set

\[
 O_q=\operatorname{im}(\delta_F)\cap\ker(\gamma)
                  \ \subset H^{q+1}(F(T)).                    \tag{5.3}
\]

Using beta to identify the middle terms, H^q(F(A)) is
ker(delta_F) and H^q(RF(A)) is ker(gamma delta_F). The map from
the latter to O_q is the restriction of delta_F, and its kernel is
the former. Its image is the intersection in (5.3): pulling the
epimorphism H^q(F(P)) -> im(delta_F) back along that intersection
proves this image assertion in an arbitrary abelian category.
Consequently

\[
0\to H^q(F(A))\xrightarrow{H^q(\eta_A)}H^q(RF(A))
                                      \longrightarrow O_q\to0.\tag{5.4}
\]

If F is left exact, gamma is an isomorphism and O_q=0. To prove
this without assuming RF exists on any additional object, let T be
zero below c=q+1 and use the cofinal denominators T -> L with L
zero below c. For each such L, left exactness gives
H^c(F(L))=F(H^c(L)), and the quasi-isomorphism identifies this
with F(H^c(T)). Applying H^c to the essential-constancy witness
shows H^c(RF(T)) has that value. The same kernel computation gives
H^c(F(T))=F(H^c(T)), and the induced map is precisely gamma.
Thus (5.2) extends to an isomorphism for all j<=q when F is left
exact. This is an actual comparison proof, not a presumption that
F commutes with all cohomology.

The finite-prefix statement, (5.3)–(5.4), and this left-exact refinement
are DERIVED-UNDER-007. Their hypotheses preserve the original bounded
below A and the given existence of RF(A), while requiring acyclicity
only through the specified degree q. Taking q=i+1 for every i when
all terms are acyclic proves the source's full Leray lemma exactly.
The construction does not erase the differential out of degree q:
H^q(F(A)) still uses F(A^{q+1}), even when that term is not assumed
acyclic.

## 6. DERIVED-NEW-021: the enough-acyclics proof and its counterexample

The proposition's statement is valid for an arbitrary additive F.
The induction at original 5856–5877 is not valid at that generality:
its short exact sequences of image objects are triangles in D(A),
whereas the earlier computing lemma applies to the exact functor
K(A) -> D(B) and triangles in K(A). A nonsplit short exact sequence
need not give such a triangle there. Applying F to that sequence
cannot be justified before the desired exactness has been proved.

Here is an explicit counterexample to the claimed acyclicity of all
images, including the proposition's enough-acyclics hypothesis. Fix
a prime p. For an abelian group M let D(M) be its largest divisible
subgroup and set

\[
 F(M)=M[p]/D(M)[p].                                      \tag{6.1}
\]

The sum of all divisible subgroups is divisible: any element is a
finite sum, and its division by any positive integer is obtained
by dividing each summand. Thus this sum defines D(M). Every group
homomorphism maps divisible subgroups into divisible subgroups, so
it induces the displayed quotient map. These induced maps preserve
composition, identities and addition, making (6.1) an additive functor.
In particular D(M direct-sum N)=D(M) direct-sum D(N), by projection
and by inclusion, and (6.1) preserves that biproduct with its maps.

Every group A embeds in a divisible group, without using injective
resolutions: choose a free abelian group P surjecting onto A with
kernel N. The inclusion P -> Q tensor_Z P is injective because P
is a sum of copies of Z. It induces an injection

\[
 A=P/N\longrightarrow (\mathbf Q\otimes_{\mathbf Z}P)/N.
\]

The target is divisible, since a quotient of a divisible group is
divisible. Section 3 therefore resolves every bounded-below complex
by a bounded-below complex of divisible groups. F vanishes on every
divisible group, so it sends all these complexes and every transition
between them to zero. Section 1, applied to this class of complexes,
proves RF is everywhere defined on D^+(Ab) and is the zero functor.
Thus M is right F-acyclic exactly when F(M)=0. Divisible groups and
Z are acyclic, whereas F(Z/p)=Z/p is nonzero, so Z/p is not.

Consider the original cochain complex, in degrees 0 through 3,

\[
 I^\bullet:
 \mathbf Z\xrightarrow{\,p\,}\mathbf Z
 \xrightarrow{m\mapsto m/p+\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z
 \xrightarrow{\,p\,}\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z.                   \tag{6.2}
\]

Its first map is injective. The kernel of the middle map is pZ,
equal to the preceding image. Its image is (p^{-1}Z)/Z, precisely
the kernel of multiplication by p on Q/Z. The final map is surjective
because Q/Z is divisible. Hence (6.2) is acyclic. Every term is
right F-acyclic, and the category has enough such objects, as just
proved. Nevertheless the source's J^1=im(d^1) is isomorphic to Z/p
and is not right F-acyclic. Thus the stated image induction fails
under its own hypotheses. F(I^bullet) is still zero and hence acyclic;
the counterexample refutes the proof's intermediate claim, not the
proposition's conclusion.

The correct proof is shorter and preserves the claimed generality.
An acyclic bounded-below complex I^bullet is isomorphic to zero in
D^+(A). RF is defined at zero with value zero: the denominator
category has the terminal zero index and F(0)=0. Denominator
invariance therefore makes RF(I^bullet) defined with value zero.
Because all its terms are right F-acyclic, the already proved Leray
lemma gives the actual isomorphism

\[
 F(I^\bullet)\xrightarrow{\sim}RF(I^\bullet)=0.             \tag{6.3}
\]

This is not circular. Leray's lemma requires existence only at the
one complex in question, supplied here by its quasi-isomorphism to
zero; it does not use the enough-acyclics proposition.

For a quasi-isomorphism between two bounded-below complexes of
acyclic objects, its cone is bounded below and acyclic. Its terms
are finite biproducts of acyclic objects: those are acyclic by the
earlier direct-sum computing result applied to the degree-zero
complexes. Additivity identifies F of this cone with the cone of
the image map, including the original minus sign on the shifted
first component. Equation (6.3) makes that image map a quasi-isomorphism.
The source's class resolutions and section 1 now prove existence
everywhere and computation by every such complex. Reversing arrows
and using the upper-bounded version of Leray gives the left-derived
assertion. This is the proposed replacement of the faulty paragraph.
