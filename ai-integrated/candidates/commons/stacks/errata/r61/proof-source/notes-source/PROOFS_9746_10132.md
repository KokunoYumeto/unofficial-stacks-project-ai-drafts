## 1. Why a K-injective complex computes every right derived functor

Let I be K-injective and let s:I→N be a quasi-isomorphism in K(A).
Precomposition gives a bijection Hom_K(N,I)→Hom_K(I,I). Thus there
exists a unique r:N→I with r s=id_I in K(A). It is also a quasi-isomorphism:
H(r) is inverse to H(s). Therefore id_I is a final object of I/Qis,
including the uniqueness required for finality, not only the existence
of a retraction.

For an arbitrary functor F on K(A), the system sending s:I→N to F(N)
has value F(I) at that final object and is an essentially constant
ind-object. Indeed the unique arrows to the final object provide its
cocone, and the Hom colimit from any object is the Hom to F(I).
In the source's exact-functor setting this proves I computes RF.
If K is isomorphic in D(A) to I, the isomorphism K→I is represented
by an actual map in K(A), since maps to a K-injective complex agree
in K and D. Its induced cohomology maps are isomorphisms. Hence the
computation transfers to K by that quasi-isomorphism.

If every complex maps by a quasi-isomorphism to a K-injective complex,
the computation criterion gives RF everywhere. A quasi-isomorphism
s:I→J between K-injectives is an isomorphism already in K:
the unique retraction r satisfies r s=id_I, and precomposition by s
is injective on Hom_K(J,J), so (s r)s=s=id_J s implies s r=id_J.
Every functor F therefore takes this map to an isomorphism. This proves
the two source lemmas without imposing injectivity on individual terms.

## 2. The split inverse system and compatible primitives

Let …→I_2→I_1 be the source tower, with K-injective stages, split
surjections in each degree, and existing termwise limit I.
For an acyclic M let D_n=Hom^•(M,I_n), using the full differential

 D_n^r=∏_a Hom(M^a,I_n^{a+r}),
 (d f)^a=d_{I_n}^{a+r}f^a-(-1)^r f^{a+1}d_M^a.

Every D_n is acyclic by the all-shift K-injectivity calculation in PART14.
The transition maps on D_n^r are surjective: lift each component through
the chosen split epimorphism of I terms. Representable Hom and products
preserve limits, and the differentials agree after each projection.
Consequently Hom^•(M,I)=lim_n D_n as actual complexes of abelian groups.

Here is an explicit proof of its degree-zero exactness. A compatible
cycle z=(z_n) admits primitives h_n∈D_n^{-1}. Choose h_1 first.
Given a compatible primitive through stage n, choose any k∈D_{n+1}^{-1}
with d k=z_{n+1}. Its discrepancy t_n(k)-h_n is a cycle in D_n^{-1},
where t_n is the transition. Acyclicity gives y_n∈D_n^{-2} with
d y_n=t_n(k)-h_n. Lift y_n to y∈D_{n+1}^{-2} by surjectivity and put
h_{n+1}=k-d y. Then d h_{n+1}=z_{n+1} and t_n(h_{n+1})=h_n.
The resulting compatible primitive proves H^0(lim D_n)=0, and hence I
is K-injective. Shifting this argument proves exactness in every degree.

This is exactly the use of the degree -2 system in the source's invocation
of Homology's Mittag-Leffler lemma. It does not assume that inverse
limits are exact in A. In particular it proves I is K-injective, but
does not prove that an unrelated compatible map K→I is a quasi-isomorphism.
The source explicitly keeps that latter issue open for a later lemma.

## 3. The exact obstruction for a tower without split maps

DERIVED-UNDER-021 records a stronger conclusion of the preceding argument.
Retain K-injective stages and existing termwise limits, but allow arbitrary
transition maps. For each acyclic M use D_n as above and put
Z_n^r=ker(d:D_n^r→D_n^{r+1}). For a tower A_n of abelian groups write

 Δ_A:∏_n A_n→∏_n A_n,  (x_n)↦(x_n-t_n x_{n+1}),
 C^1(A_n)=coker(Δ_A).

This defines the obstruction groups directly; no exactness of a limit
functor is assumed. The exact sequences
0→Z_n^{-1}→D_n^{-1}→Z_n^0→0 and exactness of products of abelian groups
give, by the kernel-cokernel sequence for the vertical maps Δ, a natural
identification

 Hom_K(M,I)
 ≅ ker(C^1(Z_n^{-1})→C^1(D_n^{-1})).                    (3.1)

Explicitly, for z=(z_n)∈lim Z_n^0 choose primitives h_n with d h_n=z_n.
Their discrepancies e_n=h_n-t_n h_{n+1} lie in Z_n^{-1}.
Changing the primitives by cycles changes (e_n) by Δ_Z of those cycles,
and changing z by the boundary of a compatible h changes its class by zero.
Moreover e=Δ_D h, so its class maps to zero in C^1(D_n^{-1}).
Conversely, if a cycle family e represents an element of that kernel,
write e=Δ_D h. Then (d h_n) is a compatible cycle family. If the
class of e is zero in C^1(Z_n^{-1}), subtract the corresponding cycle
family from h to make it compatible, so z is a boundary. These operations
prove both the bijection and its naturality, including the exact map.

Thus I is K-injective precisely when the right side of (3.1) vanishes
for every acyclic M. This is an explicit obstruction, not an assertion
that arbitrary towers work. A sufficient condition is that for every
acyclic M the tower D_n^{-2} is Mittag-Leffler. Its quotient tower
Z_n^{-1}=im(D_n^{-2}→D_n^{-1}) is then Mittag-Leffler too.

For completeness, a countable Mittag-Leffler tower A_n has C^1(A_n)=0.
Let B_n be the eventual image in A_n; Mittag-Leffler makes it an attained
image, and B_{n+1}→B_n is surjective by choosing a stage large enough
for both images to have stabilized. The quotient tower A_n/B_n is
pro-zero. On a pro-zero tower Q, the expression
y_n=Σ_{k≥n}t_{n,k}x_k is a finite sum at each fixed n and satisfies
Δ_Q y=x. On a surjective tower B, solve Δ_B y=x recursively, starting
with any y_1 and lifting y_n-x_n to y_{n+1}.
Given x in ∏A_n, solve first in the quotient, lift a solution to ∏A_n,
then correct its remaining error in ∏B_n. This proves Δ_A is surjective.
Applied to Z_n^{-1}, it makes the right side of (3.1) zero.

This sufficient condition includes some nonsplit towers. For K-injective
I_0,J let every stage be I_0⊕J and every transition diag(id,0).
It is not termwise surjective where J is nonzero, but every Hom tower
has stable image the I_0 summand, and the termwise limit is I_0.
The actual projection and inclusion identify that limit and its
K-injectivity. No novelty is claimed for this criterion or obstruction.

The source's exact-adjoint preservation lemma is also valid.
For v left adjoint to u with v exact, the degreewise adjunction carries
chain maps and homotopies to one another, so
Hom_K(B)(M,uI)=Hom_K(A)(vM,I).
An acyclic M remains acyclic under v: exactness preserves the kernel-image
short exact sequences defining its cohomology. Therefore the last Hom
group is zero. The existing article correction MC-STK-ERR-0884 is complete.

## 4. Repairing the initial cutoff in the replacement lemma

The dimension function d is permitted to have value infinity. The limit
condition n+d(K^n)→-infinity as n→-infinity implies finiteness far to the
left, but permits finitely many infinite values in any remaining bounded
range. Replacing only σ_{\geq0}K does not necessarily remove them.
Choose instead an integer a such that d(K^n)<infinity for every n<a.
Resolve σ_{\geq a}K by the source's bounded below construction to a
termwise monomorphic quasi-isomorphism into M, with M^n=0 for n<a
and d(M^n)=0 for n≥a.

The class of d-zero objects contains zero. To check this from the stated
axioms, choose A with d(A)=0 by axiom (1) and apply axiom (3) to
0→A→A→0→0; it gives d(0)≤max(-1,0)=0.
Thus the cited bounded resolution lemma is applicable.

Splice the initial map into

 …→K^{a-2}→K^{a-1}→M^a→M^{a+1}→…,

using the composite K^{a-1}→K^a→M^a at the join. Its square with the
next differential is zero by the chain-map equation. The map from K
is the identity below a and the resolution map at and above a.
The short exact sequences with subcomplex σ_{\geq a} and common
quotient σ_{\leq a-1} show it is a quasi-isomorphism. Equivalently the
quotient of the monomorphic map is the acyclic quotient of that bounded
resolution. All its d-values are now finite, d is zero for n≥a, and
the far-left limit condition is unchanged.

DERIVED-NEW-033 changes the source's fixed cutoff 0 to this cutoff a.
Here is a complete example of the defect in the original finite-maximum
argument. In abelian groups set d(G)=0 for divisible G and infinity
otherwise. Finite sums and quotients of divisible groups are divisible.
Consequently axioms (2) and (3) hold: the only nonautomatic case of (3)
has both its first and middle groups divisible, hence its quotient is.
Every group embeds into a divisible group. One direct construction adjoins
roots to all its elements by a pushout of the monomorphism
⊕_{(g,m)}Z→⊕_{(g,m)}Z whose components are multiplication by m≥1,
with the map from the first sum to G sending its generator to g.
Pushout preserves that monomorphism, so G embeds in the resulting group,
where every original g has an m-th root. Iterating countably and taking
the union embeds G in a divisible group.
Thus axiom (1) holds as well.
Let K be Z in degree -1 and zero elsewhere. It meets the far-left
condition, but the source's resolution of σ_{\geq0}K leaves it unchanged,
so the printed maximum ξ is infinite. The corrected cutoff removes
this term before the finite-maximum argument. The lemma remains true.

## 5. Elementary replacements and a quantitative stabilization bound

After Section 4 assume all d-values are finite and zero in sufficiently
high degrees. For n with d(K^n)>0 choose a monomorphism j:K^n→M
with d(M)=0. Use the pushout

 M'=coker((j,-d_K^n):K^n→M⊕K^{n+1}).

The new differential into M is j d_K^{n-1}; the next differential is
m↦[m,0]; the differential out of M' is [m,x]↦d_K^{n+1}x.
The vertical map K^{n+1}→M' is x↦[0,x].
The identity [j y,0]=[0,d_K^n y] proves the chain-map equation, and
the relation is killed by the outgoing differential because d_K²=0.
All other components are unchanged.

Both changed vertical components are monomorphisms. The quotient complex
is M/j(K^n) in degrees n and n+1, with identity differential: the second
quotient M'/K^{n+1} is canonically M/j(K^n). It is contractible.
Hence this elementary replacement is a quasi-isomorphism.
The defining short exact sequence for M' and the d axioms give exactly

 d((K')^n)=0,
 d((K')^{n+1})≤max(d(K^n)-1,d(K^{n+1})).

If there are no positive d-values, the construction stops.
Otherwise ξ=max{n+d(K^n):d(K^n)>0} is a finite integer. Its maximum
is attained at finitely many indices because weights tend to -infinity
to the left and d is zero to the right. An elementary step cannot
increase ξ. At a maximal-weight index n, any new positive weight at
n+1 is at most ξ, and the old positive value at n disappears.
With ξ fixed, the positive integer
Σ_{n+d(K^n)=ξ}(ξ-n) strictly decreases on every such step:
a new maximal index can only replace n by n+1, or coincide with an
already maximal index. Thus finitely many steps decrease ξ.
Repeating either terminates or makes ξ tend to -infinity.

DERIVED-UNDER-022 gives a bound that also directly proves stabilization.
Let d_j be the finite dimensions immediately after the initial splice.
For each integer m put

 N_m=Σ_{j:d_j>0, j+d_j≥m}d_j.                            (5.1)

This is a finite sum. During the algorithm let the same expression,
formed from the current dimensions, be its current mass above level m.
A replacement with n+d(K^n)≥m decreases that mass by at least one.
Indeed it removes d(K^n); if its recipient was already counted, the
recipient increases by at most d(K^n)-1, and if it was not counted,
its new counted value is at most d(K^n)-1. A replacement of lower weight
cannot increase the mass: it cannot create a weight ≥m, and cannot
increase a recipient already of weight ≥m.

A step changing a degree at least m has n+1≥m and d(K^n)≥1, hence
n+d(K^n)≥m. Therefore at most N_m elementary replacements affect the
entire tail in degrees ≥m. This proves the source's required local
stabilization with an explicit bound.

Define L degree by degree by the eventual unchanged terms and differentials.
Every component of K→L is the finite composite of the actual replacement
maps affecting that component. Compatibility holds because the chain
equations hold at every finite stage and eventually stabilize.
Cohomology at degree r depends only on degrees r-1,r,r+1, which stabilize
after finitely many steps; the finite composite to that stage is a
quasi-isomorphism, so K→L induces an isomorphism on H^r.
Every surviving d-value is zero, since an eventual positive value would
give a fixed weight while ξ tends to -infinity. No infinite colimit
or exactness of an infinite limit is used.

## 6. Finite cohomological dimension and the cone bound

Let F be left exact, with enough right acyclic objects, and suppose R^nF=0.
If n=0, left exactness identifies R^0F with F, so F is the zero functor.
Its derived functor is zero on every complex, and all conclusions follow.
In the remainder take n≥1.

The bounded below derived functor exists by enough acyclics.
For a monomorphism A→A' with A' right acyclic and quotient C, the long
exact sequence gives R^{r+1}F(A)≅R^rF(C) for r≥1.
Starting with r=n and iterating shows R^rF=0 for all r≥n.
Define

 d(A)=max({0}∪{r:R^rF(A)≠0}).

Then 0≤d(A)≤n-1. Additivity gives the direct-sum axiom for d.
For 0→A→B→C→0 and r>max(d(A)-1,d(B)), the two adjacent terms
R^rF(B) and R^{r+1}F(A) in the long exact sequence vanish, so
R^rF(C)=0. This proves the third d axiom. The enough-acyclics
assumption supplies the first. The corrected replacement lemma therefore
maps every complex to a complex of right acyclic objects.
The admitted parentheses in MC-STK-ERR-0879 give this actual maximum.

Let L→M be a quasi-isomorphism between complexes of right acyclic objects.
Fix a cohomological degree i and put c=i-n-1. Let
Q=C(σ_{\geq c}L→σ_{\geq c}M). The source uses stupid truncations.
They agree with L,M in degrees at least c, so their cohomology agrees
in degrees greater than c and vanishes below c. The long exact cone
sequence therefore makes H^q(Q) zero except possibly for q=c-1,c.

Apply the bounded below hypercohomology spectral sequence to Q.
Its second page has terms R^pF(H^q(Q)), with 0≤p≤n-1 and q∈{c-1,c}.
Every possible total degree p+q lies in

 [c-1,c+n-1]=[i-n-2,i-2].                               (6.1)

The filtration is bounded in each degree, so the same support holds
for RF(Q). In particular both H^{i-1}(RF(Q)) and H^i(RF(Q)) vanish.
The triangle now proves an isomorphism on H^i from RF(σ_{\geq c}L)
to RF(σ_{\geq c}M). Both are computed termwise by F because their
terms are right acyclic and they are bounded below. Since i>c,
their H^i is that of F(L) and F(M). Thus F(L)→F(M) is a quasi-isomorphism.
The computation criterion constructs RF on all of D(A), and every
complex of right acyclic objects computes it.

DERIVED-NEW-034 tightens the printed upper endpoint i-1 to i-2 in
original 10048. The printed interval is a true but insufficient estimate:
it does not exclude H^{i-1}(RF(Q)), whose vanishing is needed for the
claimed isomorphism. Formula (6.1) proves the missing vanishing rather
than replacing it by an assumption.

## 7. Canonical truncation maps, their endpoints, and finite windows

For E∈D(A), the functor just constructed agrees with bounded below
derivation on D^+(A). In particular it sends D^{≥a} into D^{≥a}:
represent the input by a complex zero below a and use the bounded below
acyclic resolution with the same lower bound.
Apply RF to τ_{\leq a}E→E→τ_{\geq a+1}E. The long exact sequence
proves H^i(RF(τ_{\leq a}E))→H^i(RF(E)) is an isomorphism for i≤a.
It is also a monomorphism for i=a+1. This proves the statement with
the existing parentheses correction MC-STK-ERR-0878.

For the second source comparison write E as a complex L of right
acyclic objects. For any integer c, RF(σ_{\geq c}L) is computed by
σ_{\geq c}F(L). The quantifier on c makes the existing unintroduced
cutoff explicit; P02-E0079 is repaired this way, preserving the true
general statement instead of replacing c throughout by one special value.

Set q=b-n. There are the two source sequences

 H^q(L)[-q]→τ_{\geq q}L→τ_{\geq q+1}L,
 0→im(d_L^{q-1})[-q]→σ_{\geq q}L→τ_{\geq q}L→0.

For either left term T[-q], RF(T[-q]) has cohomology only in degrees
[q,q+n-1]=[b-n,b-1]. Their long exact sequences show that both maps
RF(σ_{\geq q}L)→RF(τ_{\geq q}L) and
RF(τ_{\geq q}L)→RF(τ_{\geq q+1}L) induce isomorphisms in degrees ≥b.
The map RF(σ_{\geq q}L)→RF(L) is the actual inclusion
σ_{\geq q}F(L)→F(L), also an isomorphism in degrees ≥b since n≥1.
All these maps commute with L→τ_{\geq q+1}L.
Thus that canonical map induces the asserted isomorphism

 H^i(RF(E))→H^i(RF(τ_{\geq b-n+1}E)),  i≥b.

If the cohomology support of E lies in [a,b], the first comparison
gives no RF cohomology below a and the second gives none at or above b+n.
This proves support in [a,b+n-1], including unbounded endpoints.

DERIVED-UNDER-023 records the finite-window consequence. For every i,
the canonical zigzag

 E→τ_{\geq i-n+1}E←τ_{\leq i}τ_{\geq i-n+1}E

becomes isomorphisms on H^i after applying RF.
Hence H^i(RF(E)) is canonically determined by the actual truncation
window [i-n+1,i], retaining all its extension data, not merely the list
of cohomology objects. The upper-truncation comparison is monomorphic
at its next degree as already shown. The lower-truncation comparison
E→τ_{\geq c}E is epimorphic on H^{c+n-2}(RF): its triangle has first
term RF(τ_{\leq c-1}E), supported at most in degree c+n-2, so the next
cohomology of that term is zero. It is an isomorphism in degrees ≥c+n-1.

The left-derived assertions are obtained with the exact contravariant
complex transformation D(K)^r=(K^{-r})^op and
d_D^r=(d_K^{-r-1})^op used in PART14. It interchanges the canonical
upper and lower truncations, sends H^r to H^{-r}, reverses arrows,
and changes the interval [a,b] to [-b,-a].
The positive left-derived index n in this dual assertion means the
vanishing in degree -n of LF(A[0]), equivalently R^n(F^op)=0.
The right interval [-b,-a+n-1] therefore returns exactly
[a-n+1,b]. The canonical comparisons become

 H^i(LF(τ_{\leq a+n-1}E))→H^i(LF(E)), i≤a,
 H^i(LF(E))→H^i(LF(τ_{\geq b}E)), i≥b.

These are the source's maps with MC-STK-ERR-0880's restored parenthesis.
The dual finite window is [i,i+n-1]. At the first additional degree
the upper-cut comparison is monomorphic; the lower-cut comparison is
epimorphic at degree b-1. For n=0 all these functors are zero, as in
the right-derived case. No replacement by a differently indexed
derived functor has been made.
