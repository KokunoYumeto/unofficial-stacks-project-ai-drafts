## 1. The exact pushout construction and common refinement

For a degree n extension E of B by A, put A in degree -n, its successive
middle objects in degrees -n+1 through 0, and use its actual arrows as the
differentials. Write C\_E for this complex, s\_E:C\_E→B[0] for the augmentation,
and f\_E:C\_E→A[n] for the projection with component id\_A in degree -n.
Thus δ(E)=f\_E s\_E^{-1}. The identity component is the natural projection in
the source's display; retaining it matters for Section 3.

Suppose s:L→B[0] is a quasi-isomorphism and f:L→A[n] is a chain map.
Replace L by τ\_{\\leq0}L, restricting both maps. This keeps the represented
morphism and makes L^r=0 for r>0. In particular H^r(L)=0 for r<0, and
L^0→B is an epimorphism with kernel im(d\_L^{-1}).
The component f^{-n} kills im(d\_L^{-n-1}). Therefore it factors through
Q=L^{-n}/im(d\_L^{-n-1}). Exactness at degree -n identifies Q with
im(d\_L^{-n}) and gives a monomorphism j:Q→L^{-n+1}.

Form the pushout

P=coker((d\_L^{-n},-f^{-n}):L^{-n}→L^{-n+1}⊕A).

Equivalently, push j out along Q→A. The resulting map A→P, a↦[0,a],
is a monomorphism, and coker(A→P)=coker(j). This follows directly from
the pushout of the exact sequence 0→Q→L^{-n+1}→coker(j)→0 in an
abelian category. For n>1 the next map is
[x,a]↦d\_L^{-n+1}x; it is well defined and its kernel is the image of A.
The remaining terms are L^{-n+2},…,L^0 followed by B.
For n=1 the next map is [x,a]↦s^0(x), and the same pushout sequence
identifies its cokernel as zero and its kernel as A. Thus both cases
give an exact degree n extension E(f,s).

There is a chain map u:L→C\_{E(f,s)}. Its components are zero below -n,
f^{-n} in degree -n, x↦[x,0] in degree -n+1, and identities above that.
The essential equation is [d x,0]=[0,f x], which is precisely the relation
in the pushout. All other chain equations follow from f d=0 and d²=0.
The equalities s\_E u=s and f\_E u=f are strict. On H^0 the first equality
shows that u is an isomorphism, and all other cohomology objects on either
side vanish. Hence u is a quasi-isomorphism and δ(E(f,s))=f s^{-1}.
This proves the surjectivity in the source's Yoneda classification.

For injectivity, let E,E' have equal classes. The common-denominator
description of the localization of K(A) gives a complex L and
quasi-isomorphisms t:L→C\_E and t':L→C\_{E'} such that
s\_E t=s\_{E'}t' and f\_E t=f\_{E'}t' in K(A). Replace L by τ\_{\\leq0}L.
A homotopy from L to B[0] could contribute to degree 0 only through
a map L^1→B. That term is zero. Consequently the first equality is
already a strict equality of chain maps.

Set α=t^{-n}:L^{-n}→A and α'=t'^{-n}. The second equality says
α-α'=h d\_L^{-n} for a map h:L^{-n+1}→A. There are no other possibly
nonzero components in a homotopy to A[n]. Let ι':A→Z'\_{n-1} denote the
first arrow of E'. Modify t' by the homotopy whose only component is h:

 t̃'^{-n}=α,       t̃'^{-n+1}=t'^{-n+1}+ι'h,

and keep every other component equal to t'. These formulas are t̃'=t'+d h+h d.
They remain valid for n=1: the augmentation kills ι'h, so it is unchanged.
Thus t̃' is a chain map, is homotopic to t', and has the same augmentation
as t. Both maps now have the same top component α strictly.

Apply the pushout construction to (α,s\_E t). The map from its first
middle term to Z\_{n-1} is [x,a]↦t^{-n+1}x+ι a. It kills the relation
(d x,-α x) by the chain equation for t. The analogous formula using t̃'
maps to Z'\_{n-1}. The later components are those of t and t̃', and both
endpoint maps are identities. This constructs the actual common middle
extension in the definition, including the homotopy adjustment that the
source leaves to the reader. Conversely, a map of extensions fixing A,B
commutes with s and f and is a quasi-isomorphism, so it preserves δ.
The classification follows, and the stated common-refinement relation
is reflexive, symmetric and transitive because equality of δ is.

The omitted adjustment is supplied here as an editorial proof. The
source explicitly omits details, so omission alone is not counted as
another mathematical error.

## 2. Naturality and the actual group law in degree one

For a map of extensions from E of B by A to F of B' by A' with endpoint
maps β:B→B' and α:A→A', the induced chain map v satisfies
s\_F v=βs\_E and f\_F v=α[n]f\_E. In D(A) this proves

 δ(F)β=α[n]δ(E).

Pushout of the first monomorphism along α and pullback of the last
epimorphism along β exist in an abelian category and preserve the exact
extension. The displayed identity proves their usual covariance and
contravariance, with no assumption about enough injectives or projectives.
Direct sums give δ(E⊕F)=δ(E)⊕δ(F).

In degree one, morphisms of extensions fixing the endpoints are
isomorphisms: apply the kernel and cokernel exact sequences to their
commutative diagram with exact rows, or the short five lemma. Thus the
common-refinement classification is the isomorphism classification in
Homology's definition. Its Baer sum is the direct sum followed by the
pushout along Σ:A⊕A→A and pullback along Δ:B→B⊕B. Naturality gives

 δ(E+F)=Σ[1](δ(E)⊕δ(F))Δ=δ(E)+δ(F).

For the split extension A→A⊕B→B, projection A⊕B→A is a homotopy
from f\_E to zero on its two-term complex, so δ(E)=0. Pushout by -id\_A
gives additive inverses. This proves the group identification, not merely
the set bijection. These are details supporting the original lemma.

One sign from an earlier source section must remain explicit.
For a short exact sequence 0→A→Z→B→0, C\_E is the source cone of A[0]→Z[0].
Its cone differential is the positive inclusion A→Z. The source's
distinguished cone triangle has last map -p, so its connecting morphism is

 ∂\_E=-p s\_E^{-1}=-δ(E).

This is the convention in current derived.tex 4286–4326. Both maps have
been retained; the proof below never silently identifies them.

## 3. Endpoint variance and the full signed splicing formula

Let E be a degree i extension of B by A, and E' a degree j extension of
C by B. Their classes belong to Ext^i(B,A) and Ext^j(C,B). The appropriate
composition is δ(E)[j]δ(E'):C[0]→A[i+j]. Therefore the source display at
8913–8915 must have domain Ext^i(B,A)×Ext^j(C,B) and codomain
Ext^{i+j}(C,A). This is DERIVED-NEW-028. The preceding display with the
general objects X,Y,Z is correct and is retained.

Let S be the splice with all the original arrows:

 0→A→Z\_{i-1}→…→Z\_0→Z'\_{j-1}→…→Z'\_0→C→0.

The joining arrow is Z\_0→B→Z'\_{j-1}. Its kernel and image are exactly
the required adjacent image and kernel, so S is exact.
Let J=C\_S, K=C\_E, and L=C\_{E'}. Define a:J→L as follows:
it is the identity on the Z' terms in degrees -j+1,…,0,
the augmentation Z\_0→B in degree -j, and zero in all lower degrees.
It is a chain map, its augmentation is s\_S, and it induces the identity
on the only nonzero cohomology, C in degree 0. Hence a is a quasi-isomorphism.

The shift convention is d\_{K[j]}^r=(-1)^j d\_K^{r+j}. Define b:J→K[j] by

 b^r=(-1)^{j(r+j)} id\_{K^{r+j}}       (-i-j≤r≤-j),
 b^r=0                              (r>-j).

Outside the displayed support its components are zero. For r<-j the
chain equation is the scalar identity

 (-1)^j(-1)^{j(r+j)}=(-1)^{j(r+1+j)}.

At r=-j both sides of the chain equation are zero. Thus b is a chain map.
Its degree -j component is id\_{Z\_0}, and consequently

 s\_E[j] b=f\_{E'}a.

In the derived category this equality and the invertibility of s\_E[j]
compute the desired composition without omitting any shift:

 δ(E)[j]δ(E')
 = f\_E[j] b(s\_{E'}a)^{-1}
 = (-1)^{ij} f\_S s\_S^{-1}
 = (-1)^{ij}δ(S).                                      (3.1)

The sign comes from b^{-i-j}=(-1)^{ij}id\_A. In particular, the natural
projection convention in Section 1 does not send unsigned splicing to
composition. DERIVED-NEW-029 records this sign and its propagation into
the higher-vanishing proof. The correction makes f\_E^{-i}=id\_A explicit;
it does not change the differentials or rescale the existing class.
Because the source leaves the component of f implicit, the finding is
also a convention-clarity issue: a different, degree-dependent choice of
f would be a different convention and cannot silently repair this calculation.

Here is a counterexample to dropping the sign. Put R=Q[ε]/(ε²) and
k=R/(ε). The sequence E:0→k→R→k→0 has injection 1↦ε and quotient q.
The complex P with P^r=R for r≤0, P^r=0 for r>0, differential ε in every
negative degree, and augmentation q is a projective resolution of k:
ker(ε)=εR=im(ε), and coker(ε)=k.
The chain map P→C\_E has components id\_R in degree 0, q in degree -1,
and zero below. It represents δ(E) by q:P^{-1}→k.

A lift g:P→P[1] of that class has g^r=(-1)^{r+1}id\_R for r≤-1
and zero in degree 0. The chain equation -ε g^r=g^{r+1}ε holds.
Then δ(E)[1]δ(E) is represented in degree -2 by q g^{-2}=-q.
The splice S has middle arrow ε:R→R; a map P→C\_S has components
id\_R in degrees 0 and -1 and q in degree -2, so δ(S) is represented by q.
Every differential in Hom\_R(P,k) is zero because ε acts by zero on k.
Thus Ext^2\_R(k,k)=k, and q and -q are distinct. This verifies an actual
failure of unsigned composition, not just a formal discrepancy of chain maps.

The signs are associative: either bracketing of three splices contributes
ij+ik+jk modulo 2. In particular no associativity axiom of the graded
derived composition has been changed.

## 4. Zero products, the extension diagram, and the set of lifts

Write E:0→A→Z→B→0 and E':0→B→Z'→C→0, with inclusion u:B→Z'
and quotient v:Z'→C. Applying Hom\_D(-,A[1]) to the source's triangle
for E' gives the exact segment

 Hom(B,A) --∂--> Ext^1(C,A) --v\*--> Ext^1(Z',A)
          --u\*--> Ext^1(B,A) --boundary--> Ext^2(C,A).

For e=δ(E) the last boundary is e[1]∂\_{E'}=-e[1]δ(E').
Its vanishing is therefore equivalent to vanishing of the product in the
original lemma, even though the sign must be kept in the exact formula.
By exactness this holds precisely when there is w∈Ext^1(Z',A) with u\*w=e.
Section 2 represents w by 0→A→W→Z'→0. Its pullback to B is isomorphic,
fixing A and B, to E. Choose that isomorphism and compose it with
W×\_{Z'}B→W to obtain Z→W.

The induced square is a pullback, so Z→W is a monomorphism, and its
quotient is C: compose W→Z'→C, whose kernel is the inverse image of B.
The result is precisely the diagram in original 8941–8966, with all
rows and columns exact. Conversely such a diagram identifies Z with
W×\_{Z'}B by the short five lemma, so its middle row gives a lift of e
and the product vanishes. This proves both directions of the omitted proof.

DERIVED-UNDER-017 records the extra information supplied by this argument.
The set

 F\_e={w∈Ext^1(Z',A):u\*w=e}

is empty if the product is nonzero. If it is nonempty, addition makes it
a torsor under

 im(v\*) ≅ Ext^1(C,A)/im(∂:Hom(B,A)→Ext^1(C,A)).

Indeed the difference of two lifts lies in ker(u\*)=im(v\*), every such
element can be added to a lift, and the resulting action of im(v\*) is
free. Exactness identifies its kernel description as the stated quotient.
These are isomorphism classes of middle-row extensions admitting a
compatible top row; a choice of the isomorphism to that top row is additional
data. The assertion is deliberately not a classification of diagrams
with that choice fixed.

The earlier boundary is ∂(a)=-a[1]δ(E'); in particular its image equals
the image of the pushout classes a[1]δ(E'). This keeps the sign while
identifying the same quotient group. If that quotient is zero, an existing
middle-row class is unique. This consequence is editorial, without a
replacement of the source lemma.

## 5. The receiving ideal-filtration diagram

Current cohomology.tex 14916–14934 cites the zero-product criterion.
For every index i for which these ideal powers are defined, retain exactly

 A=I^{i+2}/I^{i+3},   B=I^{i+1}/I^{i+2},   C=I^i/I^{i+1},
 Z=I^{i+1}/I^{i+3},   Z'=I^i/I^{i+2},      W=I^i/I^{i+3}.

All maps are induced by inclusions or quotient maps of the original ideal
powers. The needed rows and columns are

 0→A→Z→B→0,          0→A→W→Z'→0,
 0→Z→W→C→0,          0→B→Z'→C→0.

They are exact because for subobjects J⊆K⊆L the sequence
0→K/J→L/J→L/K→0 is exact. Applied to each consecutive triple of the
actual ideal powers, this also proves commutation of every square.
Thus Section 4 proves the product is zero. Both source connecting maps
b^i and b^{i+1} are negatives of the corresponding degree-one Yoneda
classes, so the two minus signs cancel in b^{i+1}[1]b^i.
Applying M⊗^L- to this zero composite still gives zero; applying cohomology
therefore gives the zero composite of the Bockstein maps.

Original cohomology.tex 14425–14426 describes W as an extension in the
opposite order from its quotient sequence. DERIVED-NEW-031 corrects that

0→I^{i+2}/I^{i+3}→I^i/I^{i+3}→I^i/I^{i+2}→0,
whose pullback along B→Z' is the top row. This both corrects the reversed

No ideal factor, exponent, or connecting map has been discarded.

## 6. Higher vanishing, including p=0 and p=1

For the original higher-vanishing lemma, if p=0 then Hom(A,A)=0 for
every A, whence id\_A=0. Every A is a zero object and so is every complex
up to its unique maps. All claimed Ext groups vanish.
This case cannot use a positive-degree Yoneda extension of degree p.

Now let p≥1 and i>p. Represent ξ by the exact extension E as in Section 1
and set C=im(Z\_p→Z\_{p-1}). This is defined also when p=1.
There are the two exact extensions printed by the source,
E' of B by C of degree p and E'' of C by A of degree i-p.
Equation (3.1) gives the complete equality

 δ(E)=(-1)^{p(i-p)}δ(E'')[p]δ(E').

The assumption makes δ(E') zero, so ξ=0. The case i=p is the
assumption itself. DERIVED-NEW-030 inserts the p=0 argument and uses the
image formula for C: the source's additional kernel formula contains
Z\_{-1} when p=1 and neither formula is defined as written when p=0.
The signed equality is the propagation of NEW-029, not another counted
finding. The theorem itself remains valid with its original hypotheses.

## 7. A smaller splitting hypothesis and all choices of splitting

Let K∈D^b(A), and write H\_r=H^r(K). Assume only

 Ext^{r-s+1}(H\_r,H\_s)=0 for every r>s.                 (7.1)

This is weaker than requiring every degree at least two for every such
pair, as the original lemma does. Choose a≤b containing the cohomology
support. The canonical truncation triangle at r is

 τ\_{\\leq r-1}K→τ\_{\\leq r}K→H\_r[-r]→(τ\_{\\leq r-1}K)[1].

Inductively the lower truncation has an isomorphism
τ\_{\\leq r-1}K≅⊕\_{a≤s<r}H\_s[-s] inducing identity on its cohomology.
Consequently the group containing the last arrow is

 ⊕\_{a≤s<r} Hom\_D(H\_r[-r],H\_s[-s+1])
 = ⊕\_{a≤s<r} Ext^{r-s+1}(H\_r,H\_s)=0.

The triangle splits. Choose the splitting that is compatible with its
first and second maps; combined with the previous identity on cohomology,
it gives τ\_{\\leq r}K≅⊕\_{a≤s≤r}H\_s[-s], still inducing the identity.
The one-degree and zero-cohomology cases follow from canonical truncation
quasi-isomorphisms. This completes the finite induction and proves (7.1)
is sufficient. Without the group vanishing, the actual last arrow is the
exact obstruction: its being zero is necessary and sufficient for this
stage to split, whether or not its ambient group is zero.

Set T=⊕\_{a≤r≤b}H\_r[-r]. A matrix entry from H\_r[-r] to H\_s[-s] is
Ext^{r-s}(H\_r,H\_s). It vanishes for r<s by negative Ext vanishing.
For r=s it is Hom(H\_r,H\_r), and induces precisely that map on H^r.
Thus endomorphisms of T inducing zero on every cohomology object form
the strictly triangular ideal

 N=⊕\_{a≤s<r≤b}Ext^{r-s}(H\_r,H\_s) ⊆ End\_D(T).

Its multiplication is actual shifted composition. A product of m entries
requires a strictly descending chain of m cohomological degrees; hence
N^{b-a+1}=0. Every 1+n is invertible, with inverse
1-n+n²-…+(-1)^{b-a}n^{b-a}. Conversely an automorphism inducing identity
on cohomology has the form 1+n. Once an identity-on-cohomology splitting
T→K exists, all such splittings form a torsor under 1+N by precomposition.
The action is free and transitive by composing one splitting with the
inverse of another.

Therefore the extra vanishing
Ext^{r-s}(H\_r,H\_s)=0 for r>s makes the identity-on-cohomology splitting
unique. Without it there is no general claim of a canonical splitting.
DERIVED-UNDER-018 comprises (7.1), the precise obstruction, and this
description of the choices. All are proved consequences of the source
argument, kept in editorial material.

The source's global Ext²=0 conclusion follows from Section 6 and then
(7.1). The two checked More on Algebra receivers, current 21522–21531
and 28714–28725, assume projective or free cohomology. Their positive
Ext groups vanish, so (7.1) and the uniqueness condition both hold.
Their stated existence conclusions remain valid; no receiving body
replacement is needed for the stronger editorial result.

## 8. K-groups and all omitted verifications in this interval

Use the source's convention that object collections are sets (or work in
the chosen universe). In a triangulated category, the triangle of zeros
implies [0]=0. An isomorphism X→Y has a triangle with third object zero,
so [X]=[Y]. The triangle X→0→X[1] gives [X[1]]=-[X]; induction in both
directions gives [X[n]]=(-1)^n[X] for every integer n. Thus the definition
using all objects has the required invariance under isomorphism.
The report P02-E0068/OCC-01538 corrects the preposition in original 9067.
There is no prior admitted correction of this locus.

For a finite exact sequence
0→M\_a→M\_{a+1}→…→M\_b→0 in an abelian category, let B\_r be the image
entering M\_r. The short exact sequences
0→B\_r→M\_r→B\_{r+1}→0 give [M\_r]=[B\_r]+[B\_{r+1}].
The alternating sum telescopes, since B\_a=B\_{b+1}=0.
Consequently any bounded long exact cohomology sequence of a triangle
X→Y→Z gives χ(Y)=χ(X)+χ(Z), with
χ(X)=Σ\_r(-1)^r[H^r(X)]. The signs follow by arranging its terms
H^r(X),H^r(Y),H^r(Z),H^{r+1}(X),… in their actual sequence order:
up to an overall sign the three coefficients in degree r are
(-1)^r,-(-1)^r,(-1)^r.

The assignment A↦A[0] sends each short exact sequence to the source's
distinguished triangle and so induces K\_0(A)→K\_0(D^b(A)).
The Euler assignment above descends in the reverse direction and their
composition on [A] is the identity. For any K∈D^b(A), the finite canonical
truncation triangles give

 [K]=Σ\_r[H^r(K)[-r]]=Σ\_r(-1)^r[H^r(K)[0]].

The finite cohomological support makes this induction terminate at zero.
This proves the other composition is the identity, without assuming the
initial representative of K is termwise bounded. If needed, a bounded
representative is obtained by the actual canonical truncations at the
two cohomological endpoints. The source's alternative finite induction
on stupid truncations of that representative is valid.

If B⊆A is weak Serre, it is a full abelian subcategory whose inclusion
is exact. In the long cohomology sequence of a triangle in D^b\_B(A),
all terms lie in B. Kernels and cokernels of its maps belong to B,
so all intervening images belong to B as well. The preceding telescoping
argument therefore takes place in K\_0(B), not just K\_0(A).
The canonical truncations stay in D^b\_B(A), since their cohomology objects
are retained or replaced by zero. The same formula gives mutually inverse
maps K\_0(B)⇄K\_0(D^b\_B(A)). This fills the proof at original 9164–9176.
It does not assume that every such complex has a representative with
all its terms in B.

An exact functor of triangulated categories sends each triangle relation
to a triangle relation, so its assignment on objects descends to K\_0.
For the source's homological functor H with finite support on every
shift orbit, apply its long exact sequence to a triangle. The union of
the three finite supports is finite, and the same Euler calculation
proves that [X]↦Σ\_r(-1)^r[H(X[r])] respects every relation.
Finally, a bifunctor exact in each variable respects the triangle
relations separately in each input. Its map on pairs of generators
therefore descends to the claimed bilinear map on the two K-groups.
These arguments complete the omitted verifications through original 9196.
