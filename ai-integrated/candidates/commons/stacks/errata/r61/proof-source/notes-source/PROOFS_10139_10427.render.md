## 1. The defining triangle and actual comparison maps

For a sequence K\_1→K\_2→… with maps f\_n put S=⊕\_n K\_n and let
ι\_n:K\_n→S be its coprojections. Define u:S→S by the exact formulas

 u ι\_n=ι\_n-ι\_{n+1}f\_n.

Each column has two components, so the universal property of the
coproduct defines u even if the individual K\_n are not compact.
A homotopy colimit is a choice of distinguished triangle

 S --u--> S --v--> C --w--> S[1].                       (1.1)

Its structural maps are i\_n=vι\_n, and v u=0 gives
i\_{n+1}f\_n=i\_n. TR1 gives existence as soon as S exists.
Two choices with this same u admit a map of triangles whose first two
maps are identities, by TR3. Applying representable Hom gives maps of
long exact sequences; the five-lemma argument shows that the third map
induces bijections on every representable Hom, so is an isomorphism.
This is uniqueness up to a possibly nonunique isomorphism compatible
with both v and w, as stated in the source.

For a morphism of sequences a\_n:K\_n→L\_n satisfying
a\_{n+1}f\_n=g\_n a\_n, let A=⊕a\_n:S\_K→S\_L. The two maps in the
square are both A, and the column equation gives A u\_K=u\_L A.
TR3 supplies a map a:C\_K→C\_L with

 a v\_K=v\_L A,        w\_L a=A[1]w\_K.

After precomposition with ι\_n this says a i\_n=j\_n a\_n.
The source's printed a i\_n=j\_n has different domains on its two sides.
Existing unit MC-STK-ERR-0881 supplies precisely the missing a\_n;
no new operation is needed for that reported defect.

For completeness, differences of two such completions ε:C\_K→C\_L
satisfy ε v\_K=0 and w\_L ε=0. Exactness of Hom applied to (1.1)
gives ε=t w\_K for some t:S\_K[1]→C\_L. If η:C\_L→C\_M is another
such difference, write η=s w\_L. Then ηε=s w\_L ε=0.
Thus these differences form the square-zero ideal already proved as
DERIVED-UNDER-001. This is a receiving application of that result,
not a new underclaim. It does not assert a canonical choice of a.

## 2. Maps out of a telescope and their ambiguity

Apply Hom(-,L) to (1.1). The relevant exact segment is

 Hom(S[1],L) --u[1]^\*--> Hom(S[1],L) --w^\*-->
 Hom(C,L) --v^\*--> Hom(S,L) --u^\*--> Hom(S,L).

The coproduct property gives Hom(S,L)=∏\_n Hom(K\_n,L).
Under this exact identification,

 u^\*((x\_n))=(x\_n-x\_{n+1}f\_n).

The same formula on shifted objects identifies u[1]^\* with the
difference map for the tower Hom(K\_n,L[-1]), using the exact shift
adjunction Hom(K\_n[1],L)=Hom(K\_n,L[-1]). If Δ denotes that product
difference map, the exact segment becomes

 0→coker Δ\_{Hom(K\_n,L[-1])}→Hom(C,L)→lim Hom(K\_n,L)→0.  (2.1)

The left injection sends a family z\_n:K\_n[1]→L to z w, where
z:S[1]→L has those components. A change by an element of im Δ
does not change z w because u[1]w=0.
The right map sends b:C→L to (b i\_n).
Thus every compatible family lifts, and its lifts form a torsor
under the displayed cokernel; no functorial choice of a lift follows.
The source's R^1 lim notation denotes this cokernel for abelian-group
towers. This proves the exact maps in the displayed source sequence
and the receiving More on Algebra lemma without a new count.

## 3. Passing to a subsequence: all four maps

Let n\_1<n\_2<… be the given subsequence. Write

 t\_{q,p}=f\_{q-1}⋯f\_p:K\_p→K\_q for q>p,   t\_{p,p}=id,
 T=⊕\_i K\_{n\_i},          g\_i=t\_{n\_{i+1},n\_i},
 u\_T κ\_i=κ\_i-κ\_{i+1}g\_i,

where κ\_i:K\_{n\_i}→T. Retain S,u from Section 1.
The two telescope triangles and forward comparison have this form:

 T --u\_T--> T --v\_T--> C\_T --w\_T--> T[1]
 | b        | a        | φ          | b[1]
 v          v          v            v
 S ---u---> S ---v---> C ---w-----> S[1].

The reverse comparison has first map d, second map c and third map ψ.
Define all components, including the initial segment before n\_1, by

 a κ\_i=ι\_{n\_i},
 b κ\_i=Σ\_{n\_i≤q<n\_{i+1}}ι\_q t\_{q,n\_i},
 c ι\_j=κ\_i t\_{n\_i,j}, where i is the least index with j≤n\_i,
 d ι\_j=κ\_i if j=n\_i, and zero otherwise.                (3.1)

Each expression defines a map from a coproduct by finitely many
components on each source summand. The least index in c exists since
the strictly increasing natural-number subsequence is unbounded.
At j=n\_i the transition composite in c is the identity.

Telescoping each finite sum proves

 u b κ\_i=ι\_{n\_i}-ι\_{n\_{i+1}}t\_{n\_{i+1},n\_i}
         =a u\_T κ\_i.

For the reverse square, if j is not a selected index, j and j+1
have the same least selected upper endpoint and c u ι\_j=0=u\_T dι\_j.
If j=n\_i, then
c u ι\_j=κ\_i-κ\_{i+1}g\_i=u\_T dι\_j.
Hence u b=a u\_T and c u=u\_T d, exactly the two required squares.
TR3 gives φ and ψ with all triangle compatibilities.

The identities c a=id\_T and d b=id\_T hold on every κ\_i:
the finite interval [n\_i,n\_{i+1}) contains exactly one selected index,
namely n\_i. These are the first two maps of the composite of triangles
from the T triangle to itself. Consequently ψφ is an isomorphism.
The printed φψ at original 10278 reverses these domains.

For the other composite, define h:S→S on each summand as follows.
Let i be the least index with j≤n\_i. Set

 h ι\_j=Σ\_{j≤q<n\_i}ι\_q t\_{q,j}.                          (3.2)

The sum is empty, hence zero, when j=n\_i.
This includes every positive j before n\_1.
The last summand is K\_{n\_i-1}, with transition
f\_{n\_i-2}⋯f\_j; there is no K\_{n\_i} summand.

The first exact identity is obtained by telescoping:

 u h ι\_j=ι\_j-ι\_{n\_i}t\_{n\_i,j}=(id\_S-a c)ι\_j.

For the second, when j<n\_i the difference of (3.2) at j and j+1
leaves exactly ι\_j, so h u ι\_j=ι\_j=(id\_S-b d)ι\_j.
When j=n\_i, the first sum is zero and the second has upper endpoint
n\_{i+1}; therefore

 h u ι\_{n\_i}
 =-Σ\_{n\_i<q<n\_{i+1}}ι\_q t\_{q,n\_i}
 =ι\_{n\_i}-bκ\_i
 =(id\_S-b d)ι\_{n\_i}.

Thus the two source homotopy equations hold exactly:

 u h=id\_S-a c,               h u=id\_S-b d.              (3.3)

The printed h\_j includes its upper endpoint. This is a real defect.
For example, work in D(Ab), let every K\_j=Z[0] and every f\_j=id,
and choose n\_i=2i. At j=3 the printed h has column ι\_3+ι\_4.
Its image under u is ι\_3-ι\_5, whereas (id\_S-a c)ι\_3=ι\_3-ι\_4.
These maps of degree-zero free abelian groups are different.
The corrected formula gives hι\_3=ι\_3 and satisfies (3.3).

Now θ=φψ is the endomorphism on C. Its triangle's first two
components are b d and a c. Let e=id\_C-θ. Then

 e v=v(id\_S-a c)=v u h=0,
 w e=(id\_S-b d)[1]w=h[1]u[1]w=0.

Exactness gives e=t w for some t:S[1]→C; hence e²=t w e=0.
It follows that θ=id\_C-e is invertible, with inverse id\_C+e.
This supplies the source's omitted small argument and shows why the
printed ψφ in original 10292 and 10294 must instead be φψ.
DERIVED-NEW-035 records these three reversed composite expressions.
DERIVED-NEW-036 records the wrong homotopy endpoint, with its zero
case and initial-segment indexing made explicit in the corrected formula.

Finally both ψφ and φψ are invertible. The two candidates
(ψφ)^(-1)ψ and ψ(φψ)^(-1) for an inverse to φ coincide: multiply
(ψφ)ψ=ψ(φψ) on the left and right by the respective inverses.
They are respectively a left and a right inverse, so φ is an isomorphism.
Its component equation φ v\_T=v a gives
φ v\_T κ\_i=vι\_{n\_i}, which is precisely the source's required diagram.
No compactness, enhancement, infinite sum in an individual column,
or uniqueness of a TR3 completion was assumed.

## 4. Direct sums in the derived category, including uniqueness

Assume A has exact countable direct sums. For complexes K\_i let
S be their termwise direct sum. Exactness gives
H^r(S)=⊕\_i H^r(K\_i): apply direct sums to the short exact sequences
defining cycles, boundaries and cohomology. In particular a direct
sum of quasi-isomorphisms is a quasi-isomorphism.

For maps α\_i:K\_i→L in D(A), choose roofs
K\_i←s\_i M\_i→f\_i L with s\_i a quasi-isomorphism.
Their coproduct s:⊕M\_i→S is a quasi-isomorphism, and the maps f\_i
define f:⊕M\_i→L. The roof f s^(-1) restricts to every α\_i,
which proves existence of the required coproduct map.

Here is the uniqueness omitted in the source. Suppose γ:S→L
restricts to zero on every K\_i. Represent it by f s^(-1) with
s:M→S a quasi-isomorphism. For each coprojection K\_i→S,
the fraction calculus supplies a quasi-isomorphism t\_i:N\_i→K\_i
and u\_i:N\_i→M with s u\_i=ι\_i t\_i in K(A).
The zero restriction implies f u\_i=0 in D(A).
The zero criterion for localization supplies a further
quasi-isomorphism q\_i:N'\_i→N\_i with f u\_i q\_i=0 in K(A).
Replace t\_i,u\_i by their composites with q\_i.

Let U:⊕N'\_i→M have components u\_i and let T=⊕t\_i:⊕N'\_i→S.
The component homotopies combine to give s U=T in K(A),
because maps and homotopies out of a termwise coproduct are
specified by maps and homotopies on its summands.
T and s are quasi-isomorphisms, so U is one.
Likewise the component nullhomotopies combine to f U=0 in K(A).
Consequently γ=f U(s U)^(-1)=0 in D(A).
This proves the full coproduct property without presuming that
derived Hom out of a coproduct is a product.

The same argument applies to any indexing set for which the
corresponding direct sums exist and are exact; the countable source
statement remains the one under review. This routine index-set
observation is not counted as another editorial result.

## 5. Exact sequential colimits and the telescope sequence

Assume sequential colimits exist and are exact in A.
The colimit of the finite partial sums gives a countable coproduct:
maps from the colimit to an object are precisely compatible maps from
the finite sums, equivalently a family of maps from their summands.
Finite sums are exact, and taking the assumed exact sequential colimit
proves countable sums are exact. The existing correction 0885 changes
only the source's plural verb.

For a sequence A\_1→A\_2→… set B\_n=⊕\_{1≤j≤n}A\_j and
B'\_n=⊕\_{1≤j<n}A\_j. Define β\_n:B'\_n→B\_n by columns
ι\_j-ι\_{j+1}f\_j and q\_n:B\_n→A\_n by columns t\_{n,j}.
Then q\_n β\_n=0, and q\_n is split epic using the last summand.
The sequence 0→B'\_n→B\_n→A\_n→0 is exact.
For a completely explicit verification, β\_n is injective by successive
coordinates: its first coordinate is x\_1 and its j-th coordinate is
x\_j-f\_{j-1}x\_{j-1}. An element y of ker q\_n has unique preimage with
x\_1=y\_1 and x\_j=y\_j+f\_{j-1}x\_{j-1} for 1<j<n.
The last-coordinate equation is precisely q\_n(y)=0.
These calculations are matrix identities of finite biproduct maps,
so hold in any abelian category, without an element interpretation.

The transition maps B'\_n→B'\_{n+1} and B\_n→B\_{n+1} are inclusions;
the right transition is f\_n. The column equations show these form
a morphism of short exact sequences. Their colimit is therefore

 0→⊕\_n A\_n --(1-f)--> ⊕\_n A\_n→colim\_n A\_n→0.           (5.1)

For a sequence of complexes, apply (5.1) in every degree.
The differentials commute with every map, so this is a short exact
sequence of complexes. Its distinguished triangle in D(A), together
with Section 4, identifies the actual termwise colimit and its
canonical stage maps as a homotopy colimit.
The source's exact sequential-colimit assumption is retained.

## 6. Homological functors and compact-source maps

Let H:D→A be homological, commute with countable coproducts, and
let A have exact sequential colimits. Apply H to (1.1).
Shift is an equivalence, hence preserves the existing coproduct S.
The exact segment is

 ⊕H(K\_n) --(1-H(f))--> ⊕H(K\_n)→H(C)→
 ⊕H(K\_n[1]) --(1-H(f[1]))--> ⊕H(K\_n[1]).

The last map is monic by (5.1); its first map's cokernel is
colim H(K\_n). Thus the induced map colim H(K\_n)→H(C) is an
isomorphism. Its components are H(i\_n), so the asserted
identification is the actual canonical one.

Take A=Ab and H=Hom\_D(P,-). It is homological by the triangle
axioms. If Hom\_D(P,-) commutes with countable sums, the preceding
argument proves the source comparison

 λ:colim\_n Hom\_D(P,K\_n)→Hom\_D(P,C)

is bijective. It follows explicitly that every map P→C factors
through some i\_n and that two stage maps giving the same map to C
become equal at a later common stage. Both assertions are the
element description of a sequential colimit of abelian groups.
No countability assertion about the individual Hom groups is needed.

## 7. The exact comparison defect without compactness

DERIVED-UNDER-024 gives a criterion for this one sequence and object P.
Do not assume P compact or that Hom(P,-) preserves any infinite sum.
Retain the actual triangle (1.1). For r=0,1 define abelian groups

 A\_r=⊕\_n Hom\_D(P,K\_n[r]),
 B\_r=Hom\_D(P,S[r]),
 Q\_r=B\_r/A\_r.

The canonical map A\_r→B\_r is injective: if a finite sum of stage
maps gives zero in B\_r, compose with each coordinate projection
S[r]→K\_n[r] to show that every component is zero.
Let d\_r:B\_r→B\_r be postcomposition by u[r].
Its restriction δ\_r to A\_r has the original finite-support columns
1-(f\_n[r])\_\*; it is injective, by successive components.
It induces an endomorphism e\_r:Q\_r→Q\_r.
These definitions retain all the actual maps, not only their kernels.

The triangle yields a short exact sequence

 0→coker d\_0 --v\_\*--> Hom\_D(P,C) --w\_\*--> ker d\_1→0.    (7.1)

The square of exact sequences 0→A\_0→B\_0→Q\_0→0 with vertical
maps δ\_0,d\_0,e\_0 has kernel-cokernel exact sequence

 0→ker d\_0→ker e\_0 --∂--> coker δ\_0
   →coker d\_0→coker e\_0→0.                             (7.2)

Here ∂ has an exact representative description. If q∈Q\_0 is
killed by e\_0, choose b∈B\_0 lifting q. Then d\_0(b) lies in A\_0,
and ∂q is its class in coker δ\_0. Changing b by an element of A\_0
changes this representative by im δ\_0. This proves well-definedness,
and the kernel and image assertions follow by lifting and subtracting
representatives in the two rows.

Since coker δ\_0=colim Hom\_D(P,K\_n), its composite into (7.1) is
exactly λ, not a different comparison. Equations (7.1)–(7.2) give

 ker λ ≅ coker(ker d\_0→ker e\_0),                       (7.3)
 0→coker e\_0→coker λ→ker d\_1→0.                        (7.4)

In (7.3), the isomorphism is induced by ∂; in (7.4), the first
injection comes from the quotient of coker d\_0 by im(coker δ\_0),
and the last map is induced by w\_\*.
Thus λ is bijective exactly when

 ker d\_0→ker e\_0 is surjective, coker e\_0=0, ker d\_1=0.  (7.5)

In particular it suffices that e\_0 is invertible and d\_1 is injective.
It also suffices that just the two comparison maps A\_r→B\_r for
r=0,1 are surjective: then Q\_0=Q\_1=0 and d\_1=δ\_1 is injective.
This is weaker than requiring preservation of every countable sum.
The source's compactness assumption implies these conditions, and
its theorem remains unchanged.

The distinction is substantive even for familiar categories.
For a constant sequence K\_n=X and f\_n=id, define
ε:S→X by ε ι\_n=id and σ:S→S by

 σ ι\_n=-Σ\_{1≤j<n}ι\_j.

Finite telescoping gives σ u=id\_S and
u σ=id\_S-ι\_1 ε, while εu=0 and ει\_1=id\_X.
The maps (σ,ε):S→S⊕X and (u,ι\_1):S⊕X→S are inverse:
the two displayed identities give their products, with
σι\_1=0 and εu=0 supplying the off-diagonal zeros.
Therefore u is identified with the split inclusion S→S⊕X,
whose distinguished cone triangle has third object X.
The comparison from (1.1) gives C≅X with every i\_n=id\_X.
Hence λ is bijective for every P.

This example also directly satisfies the weaker criterion:
σ preserves the finite-support subgroup A\_r, and on Q\_r its
products with e\_r are identities because ι\_1 ε factors through
one summand. Thus e\_0 is invertible; d\_1 is split monic with
left inverse induced by σ[1].
For a concrete noncompact P take ⊕\_m Z[0] in D(Ab).
The identity map P→⊕\_m Z[0] has infinitely many nonzero target
coordinates and therefore is not in ⊕\_m Hom(P,Z[0]).
Nevertheless the constant-sequence comparison above holds for it.
This proves an actual application beyond the global compactness
hypothesis, without claiming a new result in the literature.
