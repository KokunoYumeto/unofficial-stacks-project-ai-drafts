## 1. The operations, their exact witnesses and finite closure

Write T for the given triangulated category. A full subcategory A is an
actual subset of Ob(T), as specified at original 10695–10696. Thus
A[a,b] contains precisely the objects A[-i] for A in A and integer
a <= i <= b; it is not implicitly closed under arbitrary isomorphism.
In contrast, smd(A) and add(A) include objects isomorphic to actual
direct summands and finite direct sums, respectively. The empty finite
sum is allowed. A star B consists of the middle objects of distinguished
triangles A -> X -> B -> A[1].

For associativity, start with triangles

\[
 A\xrightarrow f X\xrightarrow p B\xrightarrow h A[1],
 \qquad X\xrightarrow g Y\xrightarrow q C\xrightarrow k X[1].
\]

Complete gf to A -> Y -> Z -> A[1]. TR4 gives a distinguished
triangle B -> Z -> C -> B[1], whose last map is p[1]k, and maps of
triangles having first two components (id_A,g) and (f,id_Y).
Therefore Y belongs to A star (B star C).

Conversely take triangles

\[
 A\xrightarrow f Y\xrightarrow g Z\xrightarrow h A[1],
 \qquad B\xrightarrow u Z\xrightarrow v C\xrightarrow w B[1].
\]

Apply TR4 to Y --g--> Z --v--> C, using the rotated triangles
(Y,Z,A[1],g,h,-f[1]) and (Z,C,B[1],v,w,-u[1]).
Choose a triangle (Y,C,W,vg,p,d). Its cone triangle is

\[
 A[1]\xrightarrow\alpha W\xrightarrow\beta B[1]
       \xrightarrow{-(hu)[1]} A[2].
\]

Put X=W[-1]. Inverse rotation gives
X --(-d[-1])--> Y --vg--> C --p--> X[1].
Inverse shift of the cone triangle, with its triangle boundary sign,
gives
A --alpha[-1]--> X --beta[-1]--> B --hu--> A[1].
Here maps are transported along the chosen inverse-shift identifications;
no literal equality of composed shift functors is assumed. This proves
the other inclusion with the original A, B, C and Y. It supplies the
direction left implicit at 10733–10735.

Next let A1 -> X -> B1 -> A1[1], with maps f,g,h, be distinguished.
Choose the actual complement witnesses for membership in smd(A) and
smd(B): isomorphisms A1+ A2 -> A0 in A and B1+ B2 -> B0 in B.
The sum of this triangle with
A2 --id--> A2 -> 0 and 0 -> B2 --id--> B2 gives, after permuting
the middle summands, the distinguished triangle

\[
 A_1\oplus A_2
 \xrightarrow{(a_1,a_2)\mapsto(a_2,f a_1,0)}
 A_2\oplus X\oplus B_2
 \xrightarrow{(a_2,x,b_2)\mapsto(gx,b_2)}
 B_1\oplus B_2
 \xrightarrow{(b_1,b_2)\mapsto(hb_1,0)}
 (A_1\oplus A_2)[1].
\]

Transport its endpoints to A0,B0. Its middle object has X as a
specified summand, proving smd(A) star smd(B) subset smd(A star B).
Actual complement witnesses compose: if Y=X+X' and Z=Y+Y', then
Z=X+(X'+Y'). Hence smd(smd(A))=smd(A).
The reverse inclusion in the asserted equality after another smd
follows from A subset smd(A) and B subset smd(B). No assumption
that every idempotent in T splits has entered this proof.

Finite sums of distinguished triangles are distinguished. Combining
the finite lists of summands proves add(A) star add(B) is closed
under finite sums; combining complement witnesses proves the same
for smd(add(A)). The assertions about direct sums at 10763 and 10783
are used in this finite sense, as in their displayed binary-sum proof.
They do not establish closure under infinite coproducts. For example,
every object in smd(add({Z})) in D(Ab) has finitely generated H^0,
whereas the countable direct sum of Z does not.

Set C_n(A)=smd(add(A)^{star n}), n>=1. The preceding arguments
prove that C_n(A) is strictly full, closed under finite sums and
actual summands. Associativity and the smd equality give

\[
 C_{n+m}(A)=smd(C_n(A)\star C_m(A)).
\]

Since 0 is an empty finite sum, adding a zero factor through
X --id--> X -> 0 proves C_n(A) subset C_{n+1}(A).
All these operations are monotone in A.

For an increasing sequence A_j, every operation in 10823–10841
commutes with union: a shift or summand has one witness in some A_j;
a finite sum has finitely many such witnesses; a fixed n-fold
extension has finitely many leaves in its extension tree. Their
indices have a common upper bound. The same triangles, maps,
complements and sums then witness membership using that A_j.
The reverse inclusions follow from monotonicity. This is an actual
finite-witness proof and makes no assertion for infinite sums.

## 2. Exact functors and literal object sets — DERIVED-NEW-037

The source definition equips an exact functor F with natural
isomorphisms xi_X:F(X[1])->F(X)[1]. Iterating xi, its inverse and
the chosen inverse-shift identifications gives, for every integer i,

\[
 \xi_X^{(-i)}:F(X[-i])\xrightarrow{\sim}F(X)[-i].
\]

Consequently F(A[a,b]) and F(A)[a,b] have the same isomorphism
closure. This is sufficient for every subsequent occurrence inside
add, smd or C_n. It does not give the literal object-set equality
printed at 10800.

Here is a counterexample in the source's actual category conventions.
Let Q be the complex Z --id--> Z in degrees 0 and 1 in D(Ab).
Its contracting homotopy has h^1=id_Z and all other components zero,
so id_Q=0 in D(Ab). Define F on every object to be Q and on every
morphism to be the unique morphism Q->Q, which is simultaneously
zero and id_Q. This respects identities and composition and is
additive. The unique map Q->Q[1] is an isomorphism, since both are
zero objects in D(Ab); it defines xi. Every image triangle is
isomorphic to the zero triangle, so F is exact.

Take A to have the single object the zero complex, and a=b=1.
F(A[1,1]) has the sole object Q. F(A)[1,1] has the sole object
Q[-1]. These are different complexes, with different nonzero terms,
and thus different objects of D(Ab), although isomorphic there.
The literal assertion is false. The repair states equality of
isomorphism closures without changing either F or A.

The other four inclusions need no repair. An additive functor sends
an actual decomposition X+Y -> A0 to a decomposition
F(X)+F(Y) -> F(A0), and sends a finite sum to its canonically
isomorphic finite sum of images. For an extension it sends the
distinguished triangle to

\[
 F(A)\xrightarrow{F(f)}F(X)\xrightarrow{F(g)}F(B)
       \xrightarrow{\xi_A F(h)}F(A)[1].
\]

Thus its actual middle object belongs to F(A) star F(B).
Induction gives the n-fold version. At n=1 this is the identity
inclusion, not an appeal to isomorphism closure of A.

MC-STK-ERR-0888 changes “objects consists” to “objects consist”.
It corrects the plural verb and is separate from the formula issue.
The public source comparison is
[the operations remark](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FX5)
and [the exact-functor definition](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05QK).
The frozen original TeX, not that mutable comparison, is the authority.

## 3. Cohomology layers and actual complex terms

Let A be an abelian category and let E be the given set of objects
of A, embedded in D(A) in degree zero. Suppose H^i(K)=0 outside
[a,b]. If a=b, the canonical truncation maps identify K with
H^a(K)[-a]. They induce the identity on its sole cohomology object
and are quasi-isomorphisms. This proves the repair in
MC-STK-ERR-0889: the index is a, not an unbound i.

For b>a the canonical truncation triangle is

\[
 \tau_{\leq b-1}K\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow H^b(K)[-b]
                 \longrightarrow(\tau_{\leq b-1}K)[1].
\]

More precisely, its middle term is first tau_{\leq b}K, whose
canonical map to K is a quasi-isomorphism under the stated upper
bound, and the displayed triangle is its transport. Its third term
is cohomology, not the last term of an arbitrary representative.
For example, for the contractible complex M --id--> M in degrees
b-1,b, both K and its canonical lower truncation are zero in D(A),
but K^b[-b]=M[-b] is nonzero when M is nonzero. It cannot be the
third term of that triangle. This verifies MC-STK-ERR-0890.

If each H^i(K) belongs to smd(add(E)), then H^i(K)[-i] belongs
to C_1(E[a,b]), by shifting its actual finite-sum and complement
witnesses. Induction on b-a, using Section 1, gives K in
C_{b-a+1}(E[a,b]). Membership H^i(K) in E is the special case
used in part (1). Zero intermediate layers cause no problem because
0 belongs to C_1 regardless of whether E contains zero.

The omitted termwise proof uses a different triangle. If K^bullet
has actual terms zero outside [a,b], its top term is a subcomplex,
and the degreewise split exact sequence of complexes is

\[
 0\longrightarrow K^b[-b]\longrightarrow K^\bullet
   \longrightarrow \sigma_{\leq b-1}K^\bullet\longrightarrow0.
\]

Here sigma is the stupid quotient truncation, with last term
K^{b-1} and outgoing differential zero; it is not canonical
cohomological truncation. The connecting map of this sequence
retains d^{b-1}:K^{b-1}->K^b with the shift sign of its triangle.
Removing top terms successively proves parts (3) and (4), in
descending term order, using K^i[-i] in C_1(E[a,b]).
The entire differential information remains in the extension maps.
No direct-sum splitting of K into its terms or its cohomology is
asserted.

## 4. Degree support is independent of extension length

For the homological functor in the source let H^q(X)=H(X[q]).
Shift comparisons give H^q(E[-j])=H^{q-j}(E), through the
specified natural identifications. Thus if E has support in [a,b],
E[-j] has support in [a+j,b+j]. For -m<=j<=m the union of
these intervals is contained in [a-m,b+m].

Additivity preserves vanishing under finite sums and summands.
In a distinguished triangle A->X->B->A[1], the exact sequence
H^q(A)->H^q(X)->H^q(B) proves H^q(X)=0 whenever its two
neighbors vanish. Induction proves that every X in
C_n(E[-m,m]) has support in [a-m,b+m], for every n>=1.
This verifies MC-STK-ERR-0891 without an unstated boundedness
assumption on a chosen representative.

The former interval [-m+na,m+nb] is false: in D(k), take the
singleton E={k[-2]}, a=b=2, m=1 and n=2. The split triangle
E --id--> E -> 0 and 0 in add(E[-1,1]) put E in C_2(E[-1,1]).
Its H^2 is k, outside the formerly claimed interval [3,5]. The argument
also proves the more precise support statement: for any set S
of degrees supporting all E and any set J of permitted shift
indices, all finite extensions, sums and summands of E[-j]
have support contained in {s+j:s in S,j in J}. Extension length
does not multiply or fill gaps in that set.

## 5. Generator levels, minimality and detection

Let A=E[-infinity,infinity]. The source definition gives
<E>_1=smd(add(A)). Induction with Section 1 gives

\[
 \langle E\rangle_n=C_n(A)
 =smd(\langle E\rangle_1^{\star n})
 =\bigcup_{m\geq1}C_n(E[-m,m]).
\]

The last equality follows from the finite-witness union argument.
In particular every individual membership has a finite shift
window, even though no common window for all members is claimed.
We also have <E>_{n+n'}=smd(<E>_n star <E>_{n'}).
MC-STK-ERR-0892 supplies the missing “by” in “Denote by”.
MC-STK-ERR-0893 removes the unmatched bracket in the formula for
<E>_1; the balanced formula above is the source's definition.

The increasing union <E> of these levels is closed under finite
sums and summands. A shift of a finite witness is again a finite
witness with all its indices shifted, so it is closed under both
shifts. If X is in level r and Y in level s, a cone triangle
X->Y->Z->X[1] puts Z in level s+r. Thus this strictly full
subcategory is triangulated and saturated.

If D' is another strictly full saturated triangulated subcategory
containing E, it is stable under shifts, finite sums, actual summands
and extensions. Each witness for every level therefore lies in D'.
This proves minimality. The correct closure assertion is
add(D') subset D', as in MC-STK-ERR-0894. The former
add(D) subset D' is false: take D=D(k), E=0, and D' the strictly
full subcategory of zero objects. It contains E but not k.
The P02-E0085 explanation concerning a functor landing in D'
does not describe this proof; the replacement is justified instead
by this exact closure argument.

For the right orthogonal, assume Hom(E,K[i])=0 for every i.
Shifting the source and target shows Hom(E[j],K)=0 for every j.
Finite sums and summands preserve this vanishing. For A->X->B
in an extension witness, exactness of
Hom(B,K)->Hom(X,K)->Hom(A,K) proves the same vanishing for X.
Induction over the levels proves Hom(X,K)=0 for all X in <E>.
Conversely choose X=E[-i]. If E is classical, <E>=D, so this
vanishing includes id_K=0. The unique zero maps between K and a
zero object are then inverse, proving K is zero. Hence classical
generators are weak generators.

The source already proves the change-of-generator level bound:
if E' is in <E>_r, every shift, finite sum and summand of E'
is in <E>_r, so <E'>_1 subset <E>_r and
<E'>_n subset <E>_{rn}. Thus a classical generator is strong
whenever D has a strong generator. This is not counted as a new
editorial consequence.

Finally consider the object property T in 11094–11108. From T(E)
and the triangle E --id--> E -> 0 one obtains T(0).
If X is isomorphic to Y, the triangle X->Y->0 shows T(X)
implies T(Y). Thus isomorphism invariance follows from the actual
assumptions; it need not be added. The triangle X->0->X[1] and
its inverse rotation give shift closure. The stated sum, triangle
and summand assumptions now show that the objects satisfying T
form a strictly full saturated triangulated subcategory containing
E, so minimality proves the remark.

## 6. Sparse and weighted generation — DERIVED-UNDER-027

Keep the actual K and its canonical truncation maps. Let
p1<...<pt be its nonzero cohomology degrees, a finite nonempty
list. Suppose E is a full subcategory of D(A) and, for each pj,

\[
 H^{p_j}(K)\in C_{r_j}(E),\qquad r_j\geq1.
\]

Write a=p1 and b=pt. Then

\[
 K\in C_{r_1+\cdots+r_t}(E[a,b]).
\]

Indeed, shifting each witness gives
H^{pj}(K)[-pj] in C_{rj}(E[a,b]). Between two consecutive
nonzero cohomology degrees, the canonical truncation maps are
quasi-isomorphisms, since they induce identity on every retained
cohomology object and zero-to-zero elsewhere. Compose these actual
maps across each gap. The remaining tower has distinguished
triangles

\[
 \tau_{\leq p_{j-1}}K\longrightarrow\tau_{\leq p_j}K
 \longrightarrow H^{p_j}(K)[-p_j]
 \longrightarrow(\tau_{\leq p_{j-1}}K)[1]
\]

for j>=2, after transport along those quasi-isomorphisms.
Its first stage is H^{p1}(K)[-p1], and its last stage is
canonically isomorphic to K. Apply the level-addition identity
at every displayed triangle. This proves the stated sum of costs.
When all cohomology is zero, K is isomorphic to zero and belongs
to C_1(E[a,b]) for any chosen nonempty interval; no level zero is
introduced into the source notation.

There is an equally exact termwise statement. For a given bounded
complex with nonzero terms in degrees q1<...<qu, assume
K^{qj} in C_{sj}(E). Peel off its actual top term using the
degreewise split sequence in Section 3, then repeat with the
quotient. The resulting extension order is qu,...,q1, and its
cost is s1+...+su in the shift window [q1,qu]. All intervening
differentials are the original differentials in those triangles.

For the source hypothesis rj=1, this reduces the displayed
width b-a+1 to the number of nonzero layers. Weighted costs allow
different finite constructions for different layers. Neither bound
is asserted to be minimal, and no splitting of the actual extension
data is used. This consequence belongs in editorial material.

## 7. Change of generator with the full shift window — DERIVED-UNDER-028

Let G,E be objects of T, let a<=b and c<=d be finite integers,
and let r,n>=1. For the original witnesses satisfying

\[
 E\in C_r(G[c,d]),\qquad X\in C_n(E[a,b]),
\]

one has

\[
 X\in C_{rn}(G[a+c,b+d]).
\]

To prove this, fix i in [a,b]. Shift every triangle, finite sum,
complement and isomorphism in the witness for E by [-i]. Its
leaves become G[-j][-i], j in [c,d]. The specified composition
isomorphisms identify these with G[-(i+j)]. Therefore
E[-i] belongs to C_r(G[c+i,d+i]), which is contained in
C_r(G[a+c,b+d]). This receiving category is strictly full,
closed under finite sums and actual summands, so the same statement
holds for every object of smd(add(E[a,b])).

For each of the n extension factors witnessing X, substitute that
membership. Apply Section 1 successively to the same extension
triangles and the final summand: their costs add to rn. Their
common window remains [a+c,b+d]. It is not multiplied by n or r.
Forgetting the finite windows gives the source's already established
unbounded rn rule; retaining the exact windows is the refinement
recorded here. It does not change the source definitions.

## 8. The tensor factor in the geometric receiver — DERIVED-NEW-038

In equiv.tex 1784–1842, X is proper and smooth over the field k,
E and G are the fixed finite locally free sheaves chosen from the
diagonal construction, and

\[
 \mathcal O_\Delta\in C_n((E\boxtimes G)[-m,m]).
\]

For a perfect K, the exact functor

\[
 \Phi_K(M)=R{\rm pr}_{2,*}(L{\rm pr}_1^*K
                   \otimes_{\mathcal O_{X\times X}}^{\mathbf L}M)
\]

sends O_Delta to K and E boxtimes G to

\[
 V_K\otimes_k G,\qquad
 V_K=R\Gamma(X,K\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}^{\mathbf L}E).
\]

The first identification is restriction to the diagonal followed
by its inverse projection. For the second, flat base change from
Spec(k) identifies Rpr_{2,*}Lpr_1^*(K tensor^L E) with
RΓ(X,K tensor^L E) tensor_k O_X, and the projection formula
tensors it with G. All schemes here are quasi-compact and
separated, and all complexes have quasi-coherent cohomology; over
the field k the base change is flat. The projection formula used
is perfect.tex, lemma-cohomology-base-change, current 5051–5094.
These are isomorphisms applied inside the strictly full C_n,
so the repair in Section 2 preserves the entire argument.

The complex K tensor^L E is perfect: on each open with a bounded
finite locally free representative for K, tensor it with the
finite locally free E. Properness gives finitely many such opens,
hence a bounded coherent cohomology complex globally.
Applying perfect.tex, lemma-direct-image-coherent, current
2634–2660, to X->Spec(k) proves V_K has finite-dimensional
cohomology in a finite interval [a,b]. Its hypotheses hold:
Spec(k) is Noetherian, X is of finite type, and every support is
closed in proper X, hence proper. The bounds must therefore refer
to H^i(X,K tensor^L E), not H^i(X,K).

Here is the precise use of the field. For any complex V of
k-vector spaces write Z^q=ker d^q and B^q=im d^{q-1}.
Choose vector-space complements Z^q=B^q+T^q and
V^q=Z^q+S^q. The map d^q restricts to an isomorphism
S^q->B^{q+1}; T^q maps isomorphically to H^q(V).
Thus V is the direct sum of its zero-differential T^q[-q]
pieces and the two-term disks S^q --d^q--> B^{q+1}.
Every degree has just the stated finite number of types of
pieces, so this decomposition is well-defined even when V itself
is unbounded. On each disk the inverse of d^q is a contraction.
The projection onto the T pieces and their inclusions give an
explicit homotopy equivalence, with the contractions accounting
for every discarded disk.

Apply this to V_K. Its nonzero T pieces are finite-dimensional
and confined to [a,b], so
V_K tensor_k G is isomorphic to
the finite sum of H^q(V_K) tensor_k G[-q], a member of
add(G[a,b]). Tensoring preserves the displayed contraction.
The same shift calculation as Section 7 gives

\[
 C_n((V_K\otimes_k G)[-m,m])
       \subset C_n(G[a-m,b+m]).
\]

This proves the corrected inference at 1836–1840 with its
original n,m,K,E,G, and completes the strong-generator argument.

For the next lemma, replace K by a coherent sheaf F. Smooth X
is regular of finite dimension, so F is perfect locally; moreover
F tensor^L E=F tensor E because E is locally free. This is a
coherent sheaf in degree zero. The bounds a=0,b=dim(X) apply
to its cohomology, by the Noetherian dimension vanishing theorem
(cohomology.tex, proposition-vanishing-Noetherian, current statement
4083–4086). Thus the source's m=max(m',m'+dim(X)) still works.
The corrected arbitrary-window assertion at 1862 must name
H^i(X,F tensor E), with E the fixed sheaf from the preceding
proof. Neither lemma's final conclusion or dimension constant
needs weakening.

The missing tensor factor is demonstrably substantive. On
X=P^1_k use homogeneous coordinates [X0:X1], t=X1/X0 on U0,
and s=X0/X1=t^{-1} on U1. The frames X0^e and X1^e of O(e)
obey X1^e=t^e X0^e. The two-affine-cover Cech complex is

\[
 k[t]\oplus k[t^{-1}]
 \xrightarrow{(f,g)\mapsto t^e g-f}
 k[t,t^{-1}].
\]

All opens and their intersection are affine, so quasi-coherent
affine acyclicity identifies its kernel and cokernel with H^0
and H^1, with higher groups zero. For e=-1 the two sets of
exponents are >=0 and <=-1. They are disjoint and their union
is Z, so both kernel and cokernel vanish. For e=-2 their union
omits exactly -1; the kernel is zero and the cokernel is
k times t^{-1}. Therefore

\[
 H^*(X,O(-1))=0,\qquad H^1(X,O(-2))=k.
\]

Take K=O(-1) and E=G=O+O(-1). These E,G meet the actual
diagonal condition: the determinant X0Y1-X1Y0 defines the
diagonal and gives the exact resolution

\[
 0\to O(-1,-1)\xrightarrow{X_0Y_1-X_1Y_0}
 O\to O_\Delta\to0.
\]

On the coordinate charts the diagonal equation is the coordinate
difference, or its invertible-coordinate counterpart, a
non-zero-divisor; its quotient is precisely the diagonal chart.
This proves injectivity, identifies the quotient and establishes
the sequence globally. Both vector bundles are summands of
E boxtimes G. The associated triangle
O -> O_Delta -> O(-1,-1)[1] puts O_Delta in
C_2((E boxtimes G)[-1,1]).

Nevertheless K tensor E=O(-1)+O(-2) has nonzero H^1, while
K has zero global cohomology in every degree. For example [0,0]
bounds H^*(X,K) but does not bound V_K. The stated inference
from that range is therefore invalid.

The unqualified “for any a<=b” claim in the next proof also
fails. For any fixed m', choose F=O(-1) and a=b=m'+1.
Its global cohomology is zero and satisfies that printed
condition. Yet G is a sheaf in degree zero and the alleged
generating window becomes [1,2m'+1]. Section 4 shows that
every object of C_n(G[1,2m'+1]) has zero cohomology sheaf
in degree zero. F has nonzero degree-zero cohomology sheaf
O(-1), a contradiction. Using F tensor E in the premise
excludes this false range and proves the intended assertion.

These two loci are recorded as one propagated missing-factor
finding, with two exact operations. The 101 equiv.tex report
routing records have no unlocated record and none overlaps
1836–1838 or 1862–1863. This bounded inventory check is not a
claim to have reviewed the remaining Equivalences reports.

## 9. Propagation, scope and remaining work

The downstream boundedness lemma equiv.tex 1871–1888 remains
valid. Choose the uniform m,n,G from the repaired diagonal lemma.
For its homological functor choose the finite [a,b] supporting
H^*(G), as allowed by that lemma's assumptions. Section 4 gives
the uniform range [a-m,b+m] for every coherent F. Enlarge it
to [-M,M] with M=max(1,m-a,m+b). This explicit M does not
depend on the extension count n. For a cohomological functor
apply the same reasoning with its reversed exact Hom sequence
and its corresponding shift indices; a symmetric interval is
unchanged by reversing signs.

The call to the classical-to-strong-generator lemma in
more-algebra.tex 21561–21578 is preserved: if the cited earlier
ring-specific lemmas give a classical generator R and any strong
generator, Section 5 supplies the precise finite rn comparison.
Only this categorical implication is checked here; this pass
does not certify the unreviewed ring-specific premises.

corrected units 0888–0894. P02-E0086 reports the unbound base-case
index, although its range also touches the different truncation
repair; its disposition is joined to 0889 by its actual text.
