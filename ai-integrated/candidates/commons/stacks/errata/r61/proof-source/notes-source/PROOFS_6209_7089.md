# Injective and projective resolutions: signs, relative lifts, and fixed outer terms

This editorial review covers original Derived Categories 6209–7089 at
authority commit a04446e57ec1fbc252a871afcec7752fb2807b14. Earlier
corrections are checked against that source and the frozen current
chapter. The two strengthenings below remain separate from the source
body. They are consequences of the reviewed constructions; no novelty
is asserted. Cohomological shifts have differential
d_{X[1]}^n=-d_X^{n+1}, and a homotopy from a map f to zero satisfies
f^n=d_Y^{n-1}h^n+h^{n+1}d_X^n.

## 1. Existence and the original bounds

An injective resolution of an object A is the source's complex I with
I^n=0 for n<0, injective terms, A identified with ker(d_I^0), and
H^n(I)=0 for n>0. Thus A[0] -> I is a quasi-isomorphism. A resolution
of a general complex K requires I to be bounded below, so its existence
forces H^n(K)=0 in all sufficiently small degrees.

Conversely, if H^n(K)=0 for n<a, use the actual canonical truncation
q:K -> tau_{>=a}K. Its degree-a term is coker(d_K^{a-1}), its terms
above a are K^n, and its terms below a are zero. The map q induces
the identity identifications on H^n for n>=a and is an isomorphism
on the zero cohomology below a. It is therefore a quasi-isomorphism.

If the category has enough injectives, embed A into I^0, then embed
I^0/A into I^1, then coker(d_I^0) into I^2, and continue. The
differential is the quotient map followed by the chosen monomorphism.
Its kernel is the preceding image, including ker(d_I^0)=A; successive
differentials compose to zero. This proves the object resolution with
all its original terms. DERIVED-RECON-036 reconciles P02-E0041 and
French DERIVED-033 with MC-STK-ERR-0844: the quotient at original
6297 is I^0/A, not the undefined I_0/A.

For a complex whose terms are zero below a, apply the source's
subcategory-right-resolution lemma, original 5386–5405, to the class
of injective objects. It produces a termwise monomorphism K -> I,
an isomorphism on cohomology, and I^n=0 for n<a. The construction and
its exact pushout maps are proved in section 3 of
PROOFS_5091_5900.md, by dualizing its fully written pullback induction.
This application satisfies both hypotheses: zero is injective, and
every object embeds into an injective. For K with only the cohomological
bound, compose the quasi-isomorphism q above with the resolution of
tau_{>=a}K. That composite need not be a termwise monomorphism; no
such assertion is made in the cohomologically bounded case.

## 2. The null-homotopy induction and its corrected indices

Let K be acyclic, let every I^n be injective, and suppose I^n=0 for
n<a. For a chain map alpha:K -> I set h^j=0 for j<=a. Suppose h
has been defined through h^n with

\[
 \alpha^j=d_I^{j-1}h^j+h^{j+1}d_K^j\quad(j<n).
\]

The next defect is b^n=alpha^n-d_I^{n-1}h^n:K^n -> I^n. With every
domain explicit, the source calculation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 b^n d_K^{n-1}
 &=\alpha^n d_K^{n-1}-d_I^{n-1}h^n d_K^{n-1}\\
 &=d_I^{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}-d_I^{n-1}h^n d_K^{n-1}\\
 &=d_I^{n-1}(d_I^{n-2}h^{n-1}+h^n d_K^{n-1})
                      -d_I^{n-1}h^n d_K^{n-1}=0.          \tag{2.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus b^n kills Im(d_K^{n-1})=ker(d_K^n). The universal property of
the cokernel identifies its induced map with a map
Im(d_K^n) -> I^n. Extend it along Im(d_K^n) -> K^{n+1}, using
injectivity of I^n, to h^{n+1}:K^{n+1} -> I^n. The resulting equation
is precisely alpha^n=d_I^{n-1}h^n+h^{n+1}d_K^n. Induction proves the
null-homotopy in all degrees without an assumption on the lower
terms of K.

all six operations in MC-STK-ERR-0845. Five occurrences of h^{n-1}
outside the previous-degree identity must be h^n, and the first

For a quasi-isomorphism alpha:K -> L, its original cone C(alpha)
is acyclic. The distinguished homotopy-category triangle is
(K,L,C(alpha),alpha,i,-p). Applying Hom_K(-,I) gives the exact segment

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(C(\alpha),I)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(L,I)\xrightarrow{\alpha^*}
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(K,I)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(C(\alpha)[-1],I).
\]

Both outer groups vanish by the just proved induction, including the
shifted acyclic source. Hence alpha^* is an isomorphism. This proves
existence and uniqueness of a lift up to homotopy independently of
the direct proofs that follow.

## 3. Strict lifting and the remaining sign error

Suppose alpha:K -> L is a termwise monomorphic quasi-isomorphism and
gamma:K -> I is given. We construct beta:L -> I with beta alpha=gamma.
Set beta^j=0 in degrees j<a. Assume the construction through degree
n-1 is compatible with the differentials. To prescribe beta^n on
Im(d_L^{n-1}), first show that d_I^{n-1}beta^{n-1} kills ker(d_L^{n-1}).

It kills Im(d_L^{n-2}) by the already established chain equation.
Its restriction to ker(d_L^{n-1}) therefore induces a map
H^{n-1}(L) -> I^n. Composing with H^{n-1}(alpha) gives zero:
on K-cycles it is d_I^{n-1}gamma^{n-1}=gamma^n d_K^{n-1}=0.
Since H^{n-1}(alpha) is an isomorphism, the induced map is zero.
This proves the required factorization through Im(d_L^{n-1}).
At the starting degree it also follows immediately from I^{a-1}=0.

The prescription on alpha(K^n) is gamma^n. The intersection identity
needed to glue it to the prescription on Im(d_L^{n-1}) is

\[
 \alpha(\operatorname{Im}d_K^{n-1})
       =\alpha(K^n)\cap\operatorname{Im}d_L^{n-1}.         \tag{3.1}
\]

The forward inclusion follows from the chain equation. For the reverse,
pull back the intersection along the monomorphism alpha^n. Its
objects lie in ker(d_K^n), because alpha^{n+1} is monic; their images
in H^n(L) vanish. Injectivity of H^n(alpha) forces their images in
H^n(K) to vanish, so this pullback is contained in Im(d_K^{n-1}).
This proves the subobject identity (3.1) in the abelian category.
On that intersection the two maps agree by beta^{n-1}alpha^{n-1}
=gamma^{n-1} and the chain equation for gamma. They consequently
give a map from the sum of the two subobjects to I^n. Injectivity
extends it to all of L^n. Induction proves strict lifting. This
also supplies the implicit well-definedness argument in the source's
direct proof; no additional source-body reconstruction is required.

## 4. DERIVED-UNDER-009: degree windows and relative homotopies

Keep I^n injective and zero for n<a. The proof of section 2 only
uses exactness of the source at degrees n>=a. It therefore proves:

\[
 H^n(C)=0\ (n\ge a)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{every chain map }C\longrightarrow I
                    \text{ is null-homotopic}.          \tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, start h^j=0 for j<=a as before. At each subsequent step n,
the only source exactness used is Im(d_C^{n-1})=ker(d_C^n).
No vanishing below a is used or claimed.

Now let alpha:K -> L induce cohomology isomorphisms only in degrees
n>=a. Every chain map gamma:K -> I factors uniquely through the
canonical map K -> tau_{>=a}K: its degree-a component kills
Im(d_K^{a-1}), and all higher terms of the truncation are unchanged.
A homotopy h^n:K^n -> I^{n-1} has h^n=0 for n<=a, and its remaining
components also factor uniquely. The homotopy equation at degree a
descends because d_K^a kills Im(d_K^{a-1}). These statements give
canonical, functorial isomorphisms of homotopy-class groups

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(\tau_{\ge a}K,I)
       \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_K(K,I),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(\tau_{\ge a}L,I)
       \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_K(L,I).        \tag{4.2}
\]

The actual map tau_{>=a}(alpha) is a quasi-isomorphism. Apply section
2 to it and use (4.2). We obtain

\[
 \alpha^*:\operatorname{Hom}_K(L,I)
             \xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_K(K,I)
 \quad\text{if }H^n(\alpha)\text{ is an isomorphism for }n\ge a.
                                                               \tag{4.3}
\]

If alpha is also termwise monomorphic, the truncated map is termwise
monomorphic. In its bottom degree this follows exactly from (3.1),
using the injectivity of H^a(alpha); above a it is the original
monomorphism. Applying strict lifting to the truncated map and
composing with L -> tau_{>=a}L therefore gives, for every gamma,
a strict lift beta with beta alpha=gamma.

There is a further relative assertion for two such strict lifts.
Their difference factors as L -> C=L/K -> I. The long exact
sequence and the isomorphisms H^n(alpha),H^{n+1}(alpha) give
H^n(C)=0 for n>=a. Apply (4.1) to the factored map C -> I and
compose its homotopy with L -> C. The resulting homotopy h satisfies

\[
 \beta_1-\beta_2=d_Ih+h d_L,\qquad h^n\alpha^n=0
                                                    \text{ for every }n. \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus both strict lifts and their uniqueness can be taken relative
to the entire original subcomplex K. The source theorem only states
ordinary uniqueness up to homotopy.

For precision about duality, pass from an A-complex K to the
A-opposite complex DK with

\[
 (DK)^n=K^{-n},\qquad d_{DK}^n=(d_K^{-n-1})^{op}.
\]

It reverses chain maps and satisfies D(K[1])=(DK)[-1], including
the minus sign in both shifted differentials. A homotopy h dualizes
with component (h^{-m+1})^{op} in degree m; reversing the two
compositions in its equation gives the same sum of the two required
homotopy terms. Applying (4.1)–(4.4) in A-opposite proves the exact
dual: if P consists of projectives with P^n=0 for n>b and
alpha:L -> K induces cohomology isomorphisms for n<=b, then

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(P,L)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_K(P,K).
                                                               \tag{4.5}
\]

For termwise epimorphic alpha, any map P -> K lifts strictly.
Two strict lifts beta_1,beta_2 have a homotopy h with
alpha^{n-1}h^n=0 in every degree. This dual statement retains the
original complexes and upper bound; it does not replace them by
unspecified projective models.

## 5. Projective resolutions and the cohomology bounds in lifting

resolution results: bounded-above resolutions, canonical truncation
tau_{<=b}K with degree-b term ker(d_K^b), termwise epimorphic
resolutions for complexes whose terms vanish above b, null-homotopy
of maps from bounded-above projectives to acyclic complexes, strict
lifts through termwise epimorphic quasi-isomorphisms, uniqueness,
and the Hom_K to Hom_D isomorphism.

## 6. Short exact sequences and DERIVED-UNDER-010

Consider 0 -> A --j--> B --q--> C -> 0 in bounded-below complexes.
The source construction first chooses, or retains, a resolution

\[
 E=\operatorname{Coker}\big((f_1,-j):A\longrightarrow I_1\oplus B\big).
                                                               \tag{6.1}
\]

It has the induced short exact sequence 0 -> I_1 -> E -> C -> 0
and a map of short exact sequences from the original one. The
outer maps are f_1 and the identity of C, so the long exact

\[
 E^n=I_1^n\oplus C^n,\qquad
 d_E^n=\begin{pmatrix}d_{I_1}^n&\delta^n\\0&d_C^n\end{pmatrix}.
