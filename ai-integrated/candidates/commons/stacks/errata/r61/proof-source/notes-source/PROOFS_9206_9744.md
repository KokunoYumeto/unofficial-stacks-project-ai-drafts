## 1. A strict resolution system with its actual upper bounds

Let A be abelian and let P contain zero, be closed under finite direct sums,
and provide an epimorphism from an object of P onto each object of A.
For an arbitrary complex K write T_n=τ_{\leq n}K, n≥1. We construct
termwise epimorphic quasi-isomorphisms p_n:P_n→T_n with

 P_n^r=0 for r>n,

strictly compatible with termwise split monomorphisms P_n→P_{n+1},
whose termwise cokernels belong to P.

The bounded resolution lemma, current derived.tex 5654–5737, gives
P_1→T_1 with upper bound 1 and with all the required termwise properties.
Its descending construction is worth specifying: to extend a partial
resolution in degrees ≥r, choose an epimorphism from P^{r-1} onto
K^{r-1}×_{ker(d_K^r)}ker(d_P^r). Projection to the two factors gives
the augmentation and differential. Surjectivity onto cycles at degree r
and exactness on the previously established cohomology follow from this
pullback. Starting with zero terms above the bound produces the stated
resolution. This is the bounded construction used below.

Suppose P_n and p_n are given. Let Y=T_{n+1} and let
a:P_n→Y be the composite with the canonical truncation inclusion.
Use the actual source cone

 C(a)^r=Y^r⊕P_n^{r+1},
 d_C^r=[[d_Y^r,a^{r+1}],[0,-d_{P_n}^{r+1}]].

It vanishes above n+1. Choose a termwise epimorphic quasi-isomorphism
q:Q→C(a) from a complex with terms in P and Q^r=0 for r>n+1.
Write q^r=(u^r,v^r), so

 d_Y u+a v=u d_Q,                  -d_{P_n}v=v d_Q.       (1.1)

Define the next complex and map by

 P_{n+1}^r=P_n^r⊕Q^r,
 d_{n+1}^r=[[d_{P_n}^r,-v^r],[0,d_Q^r]],
 p_{n+1}^r=(a^r,u^r):P_n^r⊕Q^r→Y^r.                    (1.2)

Equation (1.1) gives d_{n+1}²=0 and
p_{n+1}d_{n+1}=d_Y p_{n+1}. The inclusion of P_n is a chain map, and
its composite with p_{n+1} equals a strictly. The quotient complex is Q.
Thus the inclusion is termwise split, its cokernel terms are in P,
and the terms of P_{n+1} belong to P. Its upper bound is n+1.

The map u^r is the composite of the epimorphism q^r with the biproduct
projection C(a)^r→Y^r; hence u^r, and therefore p_{n+1}^r, are epimorphisms.
Finally, both C(p_{n+1})^r and C(q)^r are, in the same ordered coordinates,

 Y^r⊕P_n^{r+1}⊕Q^{r+1},

and both differentials have the full matrix

 [[d_Y^r, a^{r+1}, u^{r+1}],
  [0, -d_{P_n}^{r+1}, v^{r+1}],
  [0, 0, -d_Q^{r+1}]].

The associativity map of these biproducts is an isomorphism of complexes.
Since q is a quasi-isomorphism, its cone is acyclic; the same is true of
C(p_{n+1}). Thus p_{n+1} is a quasi-isomorphism. This proves the induction.
No compatibility merely in the homotopy category has been substituted for
compatibility of the actual resolution maps.

DERIVED-UNDER-019 records the term bounds P_n^r=0 for r>n and this explicit
strict construction, as well as its dual in Section 4. The source states
only boundedness above, although its hypotheses permit these sharper bounds.
The proof is editorial; the existing source proof is also valid as checked next.

## 2. The original homotopy adjustment, disk correction, and colimit map

The source first constructs a middle complex P' in a triangle and a map
f:P'→Y with f i-a=d h+h d on P_n. Here i:P_n→P' is termwise split.
Choose graded retractions r^m:P'^m→P_n^m and extend the homotopy by
h̃^m=h^m r^m. Then

 f̃=f-d_Y h̃-h̃ d_{P'}

is a chain map homotopic to f, and f̃ i=a strictly. The equality holds
because i is a chain map and r i=id; r need not be a chain map.
This verifies the source's omitted adjustment, including its sign.

After that adjustment, the source adds the contractible disk D with copies
of an object P in degrees n,n+1, differential id_P, and map to T_{n+1}
given by q:P→K^n and d_K^n q:P→ker(d_K^{n+1}). Here q is epic.
The map is a chain map, the disk is contractible, and adding it preserves
the quasi-isomorphism and the split inclusion of P_n.
Surjectivity in degree n is immediate. In degree n+1, the disk image is
im(d_K^n). The map H^{n+1}(f̃) is epic; hence the image of f̃ in
ker(d_K^{n+1}), together with im(d_K^n), is all of ker(d_K^{n+1}).
This proves termwise surjectivity in the remaining degree. At degrees
below n it already follows from the previous epimorphic augmentation.
Thus the disk argument is correct; Section 1 gives a choice for which
this final disk is unnecessary.

Assume A has exact sequential colimits. Put P_∞=colim_n P_n termwise.
The compatible p_n give a chain map p:P_∞→K because colim_n T_n=K.
Indeed in each fixed degree r the term and adjacent differential of T_n
are those of K for all sufficiently large n. Exactness of colimits,
applied to kernels, images and their quotient sequences, gives

 H^r(P_∞)=colim_n H^r(P_n)=H^r(K).

For n≥max(1,r), the maps identify H^r(P_n) with H^r(K) and are identities
under these identifications. Hence p is a quasi-isomorphism.
It is termwise epic too: choose n>r, so P_n^r→K^r is already epic,
and factor it through P_∞^r.

The upper bound from Section 1 means that the map P_n→P_∞ factors
strictly through τ_{\leq n}P_∞. At degree n its image lies in the kernel
of d_{P_∞}^n because P_n^{n+1}=0. This factorization is a quasi-isomorphism,
by the preceding cohomology calculation. This supplies exactly the stage
maps used in the source's comparison proof; it does not assume that a
cohomological upper bound alone gives a factorization of arbitrary chain maps.

For each fixed degree r, construction (1.2) gives the compatible decompositions

 P_n^r=P_1^r⊕Q_1^r⊕…⊕Q_{n-1}^r,
 P_∞^r=P_1^r⊕⊕_{n≥1}Q_n^r.                              (2.1)

The last isomorphism follows from the coproduct universal property:
a compatible cocone on the finite partial sums is exactly one map out
of each listed summand. This observation concerns the terms; the complex
differential retains all the off-diagonal maps -v_n from (1.2).

## 3. Unbounded left derivation and the weaker additive hypothesis

Here is the complete strengthened assertion, DERIVED-UNDER-020.
Let A,B be abelian categories with exact sequential colimits.
Let P satisfy Section 1's hypotheses, and let F:A→B be additive.
Suppose:

* F takes every bounded above acyclic complex with terms in P to an
  acyclic complex;
* F commutes with countable coproducts.

Then the termwise functor has a left derived functor on all of D(A).
For the system of Section 1 it is computed by F(P_∞).
The original proposition follows: its right exact functor is additive,
and preservation of sequential colimits implies preservation of countable
coproducts by expressing them as colimits of finite partial sums.
Right exactness is not used in the argument below.

First consider bounded above complexes. Each has a resolution by a
bounded above complex with terms in P. A quasi-isomorphism between two
such resolutions has an acyclic cone whose terms are finite sums of P
objects. F preserves the actual cone matrix because it is additive.
The first assumption therefore makes its image a quasi-isomorphism.
The left version of the source's existence-and-computation criterion
(current 5444–5470) produces LF^- on D^-(A), computed by these resolutions.
Canonical upper truncation supplies bounded representatives of
cohomologically bounded-above objects; all comparisons are in D^-(A).

Let S be the class of complexes that admit a presentation as a colimit
of Section 1's systems, with the stage maps to their own truncations as
in Section 2. Every complex receives a quasi-isomorphism from an object
of S. For P,Q in S choose witnesses P_n,Q_n and let α:P→Q be any
quasi-isomorphism. It need not preserve these witnesses.

Write e_{P,n}:F(P_n)→LF^-(τ_{\leq n}P) and
e_{Q,n}:F(Q_n)→LF^-(τ_{\leq n}Q) for the isomorphisms induced by their
resolution maps. The maps

 θ_n=e_{Q,n}^{-1} LF^-(τ_{\leq n}α)e_{P,n}
       :F(P_n)→F(Q_n)                                  (3.1)

are isomorphisms in D(B).
They are compatible with the actual maps P_n→P_{n+1}, Q_n→Q_{n+1}:
the truncation inclusions commute with τ_{\leq n}α, LF^- is a functor,
and the identifications e respect maps between the P resolutions.
This proves stage compatibility, not just existence of unrelated isomorphisms.

It remains to identify their colimit with the map induced by F(α).
In K^-(A), choose a common roof R→P_n and R→Q_n over τ_{\leq n}α,
commuting up to homotopy after the augmentation maps.
One obtains such a roof by the calculus of quasi-isomorphisms, then by
truncating its numerator above n and resolving it by a bounded above
complex of P objects. Both R→P_n and R→Q_n are quasi-isomorphisms.
Denote them a and b. The bounded computation says θ_n=F(b)F(a)^{-1}.
After composing to P and Q, the roof identity gives the homotopy
α i_{P,n}a∼i_{Q,n}b. Additivity of F preserves this homotopy, so in D(B)

 F(α) F(i_{P,n})=F(i_{Q,n})θ_n.                          (3.2)

The definition (3.1) proves independence of all roof choices. It also
proves that equality of alternative roofs, which can be checked after a
common refinement, is carried to equality, not only to an unspecified
isomorphism.

By (2.1) and preservation of countable coproducts,
F(P)=colim_n F(P_n) and F(Q)=colim_n F(Q_n), canonically degree by degree.
The comparison is a chain map because it commutes with the maps from
each finite stage and those maps determine a map out of the colimit.
Exact sequential colimits in B therefore give

 H^r(F(P))=colim_n H^r(F(P_n)),
 H^r(F(Q))=colim_n H^r(F(Q_n)).

Equations (3.1) and (3.2) identify H^r(F(α)) with the colimit of the
compatible isomorphisms H^r(θ_n), so it is an isomorphism for every r.
Thus F sends every quasi-isomorphism between members of S to a
quasi-isomorphism. The same existence-and-computation criterion, now in
the full homotopy category, constructs LF everywhere, with every member
of S computing it. This proves the strengthened assertion.

We have retained exact sequential colimits in both categories. No claim
that exact coproducts alone make all sequential colimits exact is needed.
The weaker preservation condition on F suffices because the systems being
used are split in each degree, not because arbitrary colimits were ignored.

For a concrete instance beyond right exact functors, take abelian groups,
P the free groups, and F(M)=M[ℓ]=ker(ℓ:M→M), for a prime ℓ.
F is additive and commutes with countable coproducts, since an element
of a direct sum is killed by ℓ exactly when each of its finitely many
nonzero components is. It is zero on free groups, so it sends all the
required bounded above P complexes to the zero complex.
It is not right exact: the epimorphism Z→Z/ℓ induces 0→Z/ℓ.
Nevertheless the preceding proof constructs LF=0 on all of D(Ab),
since every term in (2.1) is free. This is its left derived functor,
not its right derived functor. Without right exactness there is no claim
that H^0(LF(M[0])) equals F(M).

The received direction corrections are now semantically reconciled.
MC-STK-ERR-0876 makes α run from P to Q, as (3.1) and (3.2) require,
and writes its resulting map as H^r(F(α)). P02-E0070 proposes reversing
the arrow rather than exchanging the displayed objects; both yield the
same typed map. The three admitted operations are sufficient.
P02-E0071 and the French DERIVED-065 report are completed with this
entire argument, including compatibility in n.

## 4. The dual system with the exact reversed indexing

Apply Section 1 in A^op using the complex D(K)^r=(K^{-r})^op and
d_{D(K)}^r=(d_K^{-r-1})^op. This is a complex because its two consecutive
differentials compose to the opposite of d_K². It sends a chain map
K→L to D(L)→D(K), and H^r(D(K))=H^{-r}(K)^op:
kernels and cokernels exchange under passage to the opposite category.
Direct sums become finite products, and epimorphisms become monomorphisms.
The actual canonical truncations satisfy

 D(τ_{\geq-n}K)=τ_{\leq n}D(K).

At the endpoint this equality identifies the kernel in A^op with the
cokernel of d_K^{-n-1}; away from the endpoint it is the identity.
Apply D back to the constructed diagram. It gives
τ_{\geq-n}K→I_n with termwise monomorphic quasi-isomorphisms,
I_n^r=0 for r<-n, and compatible termwise split epimorphisms
I_{n+1}→I_n whose kernel terms belong to the given class I.
All squares commute as chain maps. This proves the original inverse-system
lemma and the lower-bound part of UNDER-019. No exact inverse-limit
hypothesis has been asserted, and no conclusion about K→lim I_n is
deduced from this construction alone.

## 5. Derived adjunction with explicit representatives and naturality

Retain the source's exact functors F:D→D' and G:D'→D with G left
adjoint to F, and the multiplicative systems S,S'. Fix K and M at which
RF and LG respectively exist. Let s:K→I range over S, and let t:P→M
range over S'. The values F(I) are an essentially constant ind-system
in (S')^{-1}D' with value RF(K), and G(P) form an essentially constant
pro-system in S^{-1}D with value LG(M).

Write c_s:F(I)→RF(K) for the ind-system structure maps and
d_t:LG(M)→G(P) for the pro-system structure maps, in the respective
localized categories. The Hom formulas for essentially constant
systems and for the calculus of fractions identify each side of

 Hom_{(S')^{-1}D'}(M,RF(K))
       ≅ Hom_{S^{-1}D}(LG(M),K)                          (5.1)

with the filtered colimit, in the two indices s and t, of
Hom_{D'}(P,F(I))≅Hom_D(G(P),I). The first index is K/S and the second
is (S'/M)^op. Thus they are independent filtered indices; changing the
order of their colimits is justified by the universal property of a
cocone on their product, not by an exchange of a limit with a colimit.

More concretely, if h:P→F(I) and h^♭:G(P)→I are adjoint, then (5.1) sends

 c_s h t^{-1}  to  s^{-1}h^♭d_t.                        (5.2)

Changing s or t along a refinement gives the same localized maps:
the naturality identities of the original adjunction identify the
refined h^♭, and the structure maps c_s,d_t are compatible with those
refinements. Conversely any arrow on the right is represented, by
essential constancy of the pro-system, by some map G(P)→K in the
localization. A left fraction writes it as s^{-1}b with b:G(P)→I.
Adjoint b back to h:P→F(I) and use the left expression in (5.2).
Equality of fractions or of representatives is exactly equality after
a refinement, so these constructions are inverse.

These formulas prove naturality for actual arrows in K and M. A localized
arrow is a composite of such arrows and inverses of arrows in S or S'.
Naturality for those invertible arrows implies naturality for their
inverses by multiplying the naturality square by the inverse isomorphisms.
Hence (5.1) is functorial on the full domains where the two derived
functors are defined, as stated by the source.

MC-STK-ERR-0877 correctly replaces the two undefined abelian derived
categories in the general proof by (S')^{-1}D' and S^{-1}D.
The two P02 reports and French DERIVED-066 are reconciled by (5.1)–(5.2).

For adjoint functors between abelian categories, G preserves zero objects
and finite coproducts, while F preserves zero objects and finite products.
Since the latter are biproducts, both functors are additive. Their
degreewise adjunction restricts to chain maps: naturality in each variable
equates the two chain-map equations. It also equates the homotopy
relations, by additivity and the same naturality for their degree -1
components. Thus it descends to an adjunction on homotopy categories.
Both induced functors preserve the actual shift and cone formulas,
so they are exact there. This proves the abelian-category specialization.

If RF and LG are everywhere defined, define the unit η_M:M→RF LG(M)
as the preimage of id_{LG(M)} under (5.1), and the counit
ε_K:LG RF(K)→K as the image of id_{RF(K)}.
Naturality in M gives ε_{LG(M)} LG(η_M)=id_{LG(M)}.
For the other identity, naturality in K sends the image of η_{RF(K)}
under (5.1) to ε_K. But the image of id_{RF(K)} is also ε_K, and
(5.1) is injective; therefore RF(ε_K)η_{RF(K)}=id_{RF(K)}.
This proves the actual adjunction, including its two triangle identities.
No such composition is asserted in a partial domain unless its terms exist.

## 6. K-injectivity, localization, and bounded below complexes

The defining vanishing for maps from acyclic complexes also holds for all
their shifts. If I is K-injective and u:M→N is a quasi-isomorphism, apply
Hom_K(-,I) to the cone triangle. The neighboring Hom groups of C(u) and
its shifts vanish, so precomposition by u is bijective.
Conversely, this bijectivity identifies a fraction f s^{-1}:N→I with
the unique g:N→I in K satisfying g s=f. It is independent of the
fraction because a common refinement identifies both such g.
It is also injective: if g maps to zero in D, some quasi-isomorphism s
makes g s zero in K, whence g=0 by that same bijectivity.
Thus Hom_K(N,I)→Hom_D(N,I) is an isomorphism.
If this last condition holds and N is acyclic, N is zero in D, so its
Hom to I is zero in K. This proves all three equivalences.

For a distinguished triangle in K, apply Hom_K(N,-) for acyclic N and
each of its shifts. Its long exact sequence implies that if two terms
are K-injective, the third is as well. Shifts of a K-injective complex
are K-injective because Hom_K(N,I[r])=Hom_K(N[-r],I).

For completeness let I have injective terms and vanish below a.
Given f:N→I from an acyclic N, construct h^r:N^r→I^{r-1} with
f=d_I h+h d_N. Set h^r=0 for r≤a. At r=a, f^a kills
im(d_N^{a-1})=ker(d_N^a), so it factors through im(d_N^a)⊆N^{a+1};
injectivity of I^a extends this factor to h^{a+1}.
Inductively f^r-d_I^{r-1}h^r kills ker(d_N^r): on the image of
d_N^{r-1} this follows from the chain equation for f and the preceding
homotopy equation, with d_I²=0. Extend again to obtain h^{r+1}.
The construction proves every component of the homotopy equation.
Hence f is zero in K and I is K-injective.

## 7. The Hom-complex index correction and the product theorem

For arbitrary complexes K,I define the full Hom complex of abelian groups by

 C^r=∏_{b∈Z}Hom_A(K^b,I^{b+r}),
 (d_C^r f)^b=d_I^{b+r}f^b-(-1)^r f^{b+1}d_K^b.          (7.1)

Expanding d_C^{r+1}d_C^r gives the two terms containing d_I² and d_K²,
which vanish, and two terms containing d_I f d_K whose coefficients
-(-1)^r and -(-1)^{r+1} cancel. Thus it is a complex.
A degree r cocycle is exactly a chain map K→I[r], since the target
differential is (-1)^r d_I. If f=d_C^{r-1}h, the corresponding chain
homotopy has components (-1)^r h^b:K^b→I[r]^{b-1}. Indeed its boundary is

 (-1)^r d_I((-1)^r h)+((-1)^r h)d_K
 = d_I h+(-1)^r h d_K=d_C^{r-1}h.

Conversely any such homotopy arises this way. Therefore
H^r(C)=Hom_K(K,I[r]), including H^0(C)=Hom_K(K,I).

The six factors in original 9712–9714 and 9720–9722 have K^{-b}
where (7.1) requires K^b. DERIVED-NEW-032 corrects them and makes the
full Hom complex explicit. This is not an innocuous dummy-index choice:
changing b to -b in all factors would also change the exponents of I.
For example, take K=I=Q[-1] in complexes of Q-vector spaces, so both
are Q in degree 1. Their identity gives Hom_K(K,I)=Q. In the printed
middle product, nonzero K^{-b} requires b=-1, whereas nonzero I^b
requires b=1. The product is zero. In this example the two adjacent
printed groups are zero too, so no differential could repair the
asserted middle cohomology. I is a bounded below complex of injectives,
so this example is within the stated lemma's setting.

Now let {I_t} be the given set-indexed family, with the termwise products
I^m=∏_t I_t^m existing in A. The product universal property defines d_I
by its components d_{I_t}; its square is zero because all its projections
are zero. Formula (7.1) gives the actual isomorphism of complexes

 Hom^•(K,I) ≅ ∏_t Hom^•(K,I_t).

In degree r this is the canonical rearrangement
∏_b Hom(K^b,∏_t I_t^{b+r})≅∏_t∏_b Hom(K^b,I_t^{b+r}).
Both terms in the differential agree after each projection, so this
is an isomorphism of complexes, not just of their graded objects.
Products of abelian groups are exact: kernels are coordinatewise,
and a family of surjections is surjective by choosing a preimage in
each coordinate. Hence cohomology commutes with this product.
In particular, for every K and r,

 Hom_K(K,I[r]) ≅ ∏_t Hom_K(K,I_t[r]).                    (7.2)

For acyclic K each right-hand group vanishes because I_t is K-injective
and K[-r] is acyclic. Thus the full Hom complexes are acyclic, which
justifies the source's acyclicity step at every degree, not only at zero.
Equation (7.2) proves I is K-injective. Applying Section 6 to I and
the I_t, now for arbitrary K, yields

 Hom_D(K,I) ≅ ∏_t Hom_D(K,I_t).

These isomorphisms are induced by the actual product projections and
are natural in K, so they prove the universal property of the product
in D(A). Only the displayed term products in A were required.
No exactness of products in A was assumed: the exact products used
above are products of abelian groups. The theorem remains true.
