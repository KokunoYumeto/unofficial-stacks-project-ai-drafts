\begin{history}
The cited source uses ``injective'' as a synonym for monomorphism and
``surjective'' as a synonym for epimorphism in an arbitrary category.
We keep the terms monomorphism and epimorphism in that generality; for
$\textit{Sets}$ they agree with injective and surjective maps by
Example \ref{example-mono-epi-sets}.
The cited source also calls a morphism which is both a monomorphism and an
epimorphism ``bijective''. We do not use this terminology here: such a
morphism need not be an isomorphism in an arbitrary category, as
Example \ref{example-mono-epi-not-isomorphism} shows.
\end{history}
